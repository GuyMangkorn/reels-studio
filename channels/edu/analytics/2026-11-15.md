# Channel retention and script analysis — 2026-11-15

## Answer

The strongest **combined** examples are **ant-sized human (EP05), falling elevator (EP11), and disappearing Sun (EP07)**. They have the three lowest observed Studio swipe-away rates and the strongest near-end retention curves among the 16 videos with curves available in this pull. Their shared storytelling pattern is a personally imaginable, counter-intuitive situation, successive connected questions, and a final rule reversal or reframe.

This is an observational pattern, not evidence that any single script choice caused the result. In particular, a spectacular physics premise alone did not make flea or gecko perform as well.

## What the winning scripts do

Among the 16 Studio videos with at least 1,000 views in this period, the three finalists form the complete tradeoff frontier for low swipe-away and high APV: every other eligible row has both a higher swipe-away rate and lower APV than at least one finalist. This remains true for the near-end curve dimension among videos with available curves. No arbitrary score or external benchmark was needed.

### EP05 — ant-sized human: successive survival problems

Studio: **21.6% swipe-away, 87.9% APV, 0:58 AVD**. API curve:71.3% at 90% runtime, 51.1% at 100%.

Opening: “ถ้าคุณตัวเล็กเท่ามด…” → “ตกจากตึกสิบชั้น… คุณจะแทบไม่เป็นอะไรเลย”. It makes a concrete promise about the viewer's own body that conflicts with intuition. The explicit shrinking-weight premise follows immediately.

The integrated caption sequence is: fall → gentle landing → “แต่ข้างล่าง… มีหยดน้ำค้างรออยู่” → stuck hand → cold → hunger → ant carrying a load → smallness becomes strength. The viewer learns physics by following the consequences for one character. The dewdrop problem appears before the term “แรงตึงผิวน้ำ”; the term answers a question the character already experienced.

The ending changes the meaning of the same rule: the size that made life difficult also gives relative strength. The crumb changes from a food burden to a lifting triumph. The final line is “โลกใบเดียวกันแท้ ๆ… แค่ขนาดตัวเปลี่ยน เกมก็เปลี่ยนทั้งเกม”. A closed payoff works here; a loop is not required for strong hold.

Primary local sources: [integrated caption text](../../../src/videos/edu-ep05/shots.ts), [registered 66.30 s timeline](../../../src/videos/edu-ep05/index.ts), [script: story core and full spoken script](../../../scripts/edu-series/ep05/script.md#story-core).

### EP11 — falling elevator: answer, objection, new question, rescue

Studio: **23.3% swipe-away, 91.8% APV, 0:55 AVD**. API curve:78.2% at 90% runtime, 68.3% at 100%.

Opening: “ถ้าสลิงลิฟต์ขาด ร่วงจากชั้นยี่สิบ… คุณกระโดดตอนท้าย รอดมั้ย”. The answer comes early: “จากความสูงขนาดนี้… แทบไม่ช่วยอะไรเลย”. It does not withhold the original answer for a minute.

The character voices the obvious attempted solution: “เอาไงดี… โดดสวนขึ้นไป!”. The explanation lands as a physical comparison: jumping changes a twentieth-floor fall into roughly a seventeenth-floor fall. Then come new questions: jump too early or too late; can an elevator actually fall like this; what stops it? “แต่เดี๋ยวนะ… ลิฟต์ร่วงแบบนี้ได้จริงเหรอ” opens the history/brake sequence while the original person's fate still matters.

The same shared-speed rule returns as reassurance: “ลิฟต์หยุด… คุณก็หยุดไปด้วย”. The rescue resolves the larger scene; a brief practical instruction closes it. This is evidence against a blanket rule that advice endings always lose viewers.

Primary local sources: [timed narration](../../../src/videos/edu-ep11/cues.ts), [registered 59.60 s timeline](../../../src/videos/edu-ep11/index.ts), [script](../../../scripts/edu-series/ep11/script.md).

### EP07 — disappearing Sun: countdown, serial consequences, present-day reframe

Studio: **26.6% swipe-away, 102.5% APV, 0:54 AVD** on a53 s upload. API curve:90.1% at 90% runtime, 81.3% at 100%.

Opening: “ถ้าดวงอาทิตย์หายไปตอนนี้… คุณจะยังไม่รู้ตัวไปอีก 8 นาที”. The basic answer follows: light travel time. The story then makes the audience wait for an event and discover what changes next: gravity's message, no possible warning, darkness, Earth's orbit, the Moon, then Jupiter. It converts an abstract scientific fact into a sequence of visible consequences and remaining questions.

The ending reframes the viewer's real present: “ที่จริง… ดาวทุกดวงที่คุณเห็น คือภาพในอดีต” → “และแสงแดดที่โดนตัวคุณอยู่ตอนนี้… ออกจากดวงอาทิตย์มาตั้งแต่ 8 นาทีที่แล้ว”. The registered timeline explicitly selects `B_loop`,52.67 s, with matching first/last picture intent. Replay is a plausible contributor to APV above 100%; this analysis does not measure replay counts or establish that the loop caused the result.

- **Dog:** 36.5% swipe-away,81.9% APV,61.2% curve at 90% runtime. It demonstrates a mistake, explains the dog's view, then shows the blue-ball alternative. [Flea](../../../scripts/edu-series/ep13/script.md), [gecko](../../../scripts/edu-series/ep15/script.md).
- **The Bloop:** 82.4% APV with54.1% swipe-away. A mystery can retain those who stay while failing to win the initial feed decision. Its71s duration and different topic/audience confound comparisons; it is not a combined winner simply because APV exceeds80%.

## Next content direction and test

Prioritize **a surprising consequence that could happen to the viewer, told as a scene with one person's situation still unresolved**. Ant/elevator provide the strongest combined templates; Sun provides the strongest hold/reframe template. Do not reduce this to “make disaster clips”: cat shows relatable everyday behavior can also win attention, and dog demonstrates a viable explanation-plus-demonstration format.

For the next 3 comparable edu uploads, test this sequence: concrete counter-intuitive hook → early honest answer → immediate new consequence or objection → minimal explanation attached to what happens → final reversal/reframe. Give the first answer early enough to reward trust; keep a larger situation or genuinely new question open. A withheld first answer, faster speech/cuts, fear, and mandatory looping are not established requirements.

Compare actual Studio swipe-away and APV at the same post age (e.g. 72 h), then check curves around explanations and at 90% runtime. Existing finalist values are channel-specific references, not universal platform thresholds. Avoid interpreting raw view count or an uncontrolled before/after as a causal experiment.
