## Visual intent and chosen approach

The viewer is a passenger who confidently predicts an ordinary toy's movement, sees the prediction fail, and gets a second chance to use the same rule. Make the **passenger–balloon opposition** the visual sentence. Playfulness comes from the passenger's eyes and the held contrast, not violent driving or a rapid succession of diagram cards.

**VD20-01 — Painted cabin, one returning comparison.** Use the project-default painted p5.brush world-metre set through the kit yaw camera. Keep the car's front toward screen-right throughout. A recognizably enclosed rear-passenger compartment, modest parallax, a foreground door-frame edge and a visible front-seat back establish place immediately. The cabin remains the location during the explanation. SVG is reserved for crisp Thai labels and a clearly labelled explanatory pressure inset, above the single painted canvas.

**VD20-02 — Story-first character and prop design.** Before kit selection, the story calls for an ordinary adult rear passenger: short tidy hair, muted blue casual clothing, simple dark trousers, relaxed hands on the lap, a clearly visible seat belt, and a small face reaction from certainty to surprise. The hero is a warm coral, visibly buoyant helium balloon: rounded envelope, small tied neck, continuous pale string and a low floor anchor. The cabin is cream and muted blue-grey in warm daylight. The balloon has the strongest colour accent; set details are subordinate.

After that design, the inspected kit offers a matching cast: **`man-crop + hoodie, P31, neutral → surprised → happy`**, `view: right`, four-head adult, using the existing person's height and facial identity. The hood stays down. P31 is the existing `sit_chair_hands_lap` pose and has a profile view; the seated support/seat-belt adaptation remains new work. A cropped, restrained driver may be indicated at the distant front seat but is not an additional acting lead. No driver handles the balloon. No animal, handheld prop or extra character dialogue is needed.

**VD20-03 — Reconstructed view, enclosed system.** Use a painted near-side cutaway treatment to let the camera see the cabin interior without implying an open window: retain the closed pane perimeter, door seals, roof and windshield outline; give the near pane only a narrow edge highlight, not a bright veil over the hero. A small Thai `ภาพจำลอง` label identifies the staged reconstruction. This is an illustration, not recorded experiment footage. The illustration's cutaway is a viewing convention, not a physical opening or draft.

Approaches considered: (a) a continuing cabin demonstration plus one schematic inset, selected because it keeps the person and the observed result present; (b) a full-screen pressure lecture, rejected because it would interrupt that causal experience; (c) live driving footage, rejected because no actual filmed trial or supplied media exists and the assignment is illustrated. These are Film's design calls under the recorded defaults, not additional user selections.
