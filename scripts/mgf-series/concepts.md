## R1 — Preaw pitch (2026-11-15)

### C1 — ฝอยทองของนักโทษ: ท้าวทองกีบม้า

- **Type:** real person (Maria Guyomar de Pinha, ท้าวทองกีบม้า), told through her husband's rise and fall.
- **Era / place:** Ayutthaya, reign of สมเด็จพระนารายณ์, coup of 1688 and after.
- **Logline:** a mixed Japanese-Portuguese Catholic girl marries Constantine Phaulkon, the Greek sailor who became the most powerful foreigner in Siam; when the court turns in 1688 she loses husband, house and freedom in days, and the story Thais know her for, the egg-and-sugar sweets, begins in that loss.
- **Hook:** "ฝอยทองที่คุณกินในงานบวช… อาจเริ่มจากผู้หญิงที่เพิ่งเสียทุกอย่างในคืนเดียว"
- **Central question:** how did the wife of the second most powerful man in the kingdom end up working in a kitchen, and did she really give Thailand these sweets?
- **Beats (Long):**
  1. Cold open: a tray of ทองหยิบ ฝอยทอง at a merit-making; a name nobody remembers.
  2. Phaulkon's climb: cabin boy → interpreter → the king's trusted minister; Maria marries into the palace's centre of power.
  3. 1688: the king falls ill, พระเพทราชา moves, Phaulkon is taken and killed; Maria runs to the French for protection and is handed back.
  4. Prison, then work in the royal kitchen (as the story is told); Portuguese egg sweets meet Siamese sugar and palm.
  5. Payoff: what the records actually support vs the legend; she outlives the regime and writes to France for what she is owed. Closing: the sweet you eat is a survivor's recipe, whether or not she wrote it.
- **Ending type:** bittersweet reveal (legend vs record, stated honestly).
- **Voice / tone:** warm storyteller, intimate; tension in beat 3, tenderness in 4–5.
- **Why it holds and shares:** everyday object hook (every Thai knows ฝอยทอง), a palace thriller in the middle, and a "the real story is more complicated" payoff people forward to family.
- **Research load:** named real case, Boat researches and fact-checks. Hardest: (1) what any primary source says about her inventing or teaching the sweets (likely legend); (2) her imprisonment and kitchen work, dates and conditions; (3) her later petitions/pension from France. **Sensitivity:** Ayutthaya royalty and the 1688 succession (portray kings with care, no invented royal dialogue); Catholic persecution framing.
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera: Ayutthaya palace, Phaulkon's house in Lopburi, palace kitchen.
- **Long size:** ~11 min; production L (period court sets, crowd). Shorts (3): S1 "ใครคิดฝอยทอง" (hook + legend question, answer: Portuguese roots), S2 "ฝรั่งที่ใหญ่ที่สุดในอยุธยา" (Phaulkon's rise), S3 "คืนที่ทุกอย่างพัง" (1688, ends on her capture, not her fate).

### C2 — อิน-จัน: สามชั่วโมงสุดท้าย

- **Type:** real person(s) (Chang and Eng Bunker, แฝดสยาม อิน-จัน).
- **Era / place:** แม่กลอง, Siam (born 1811) → touring world → North Carolina, USA (died 1874).
- **Logline:** two fishermen's sons joined at the chest are taken from Siam as a show, buy back their own lives, become rich American farmers with two wives, two houses and twenty-one children, and a side most Thais never hear: they owned enslaved people. One January night one brother wakes up beside the other's body.
- **Hook:** "คืนนั้นอินตื่นขึ้นมา… แล้วพบว่าจันที่ตัวติดกับเขา ไม่หายใจแล้ว"
- **Central question:** what happened to Eng in the hours after Chang died, and why did nobody separate them, then or ever?
- **Beats (Long):**
  1. Cold open: January 1874, a cold room, a son runs for a doctor who lives too far away.
  2. แม่กลอง: the boys selling duck eggs, spotted by a Scottish trader, the deal with their mother, the ship out.
  3. The show years: being displayed, then firing their managers and owning the act; the word "Siamese twins" is born from them.
  4. The American turn: citizenship, farms, marrying the Yates sisters, two houses, alternating days; and the slavery chapter, told plainly.
  5. Cracks: the Civil War takes their wealth, Chang drinks, they quarrel, they ask doctors about separation; Chang's stroke.
  6. Payoff: the three hours (as recorded), the autopsy, what it showed about whether surgery could have saved them. Closing on the two houses.
- **Ending type:** tragic, quiet.
- **Voice / tone:** restrained documentary-storyteller; no freak-show tone, the brothers are people with opinions and fights.
- **Why it holds and shares:** the opening image is unforgettable, the slavery turn breaks the "Thai pride" version people expect, and the central question pays off with a real medical answer. Thai + world audience both search this name.
- **Research load:** named real case, Boat researches and fact-checks. Hardest: (1) the death night and the time gap between deaths (commonly "about 2–3 hours"); (2) children count and how many enslaved people they held, by census; (3) separation consultations and autopsy findings. **Sensitivity:** disability and bodily difference (no spectacle framing), slavery (no softening), living descendants in the US, Siam-era royal involvement in their departure if any (check).
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera: Mae Klong river house, show halls, North Carolina farmhouse at night.
- **Long size:** ~12 min; production L (new two-body joined character rig is the main build, Film/Boss to judge). Shorts (3): S1 "แฝดที่ให้กำเนิดคำว่า Siamese twins" (origin), S2 "ภรรยาสองคน บ้านสองหลัง" (the arrangement), S3 "ด้านมืดของอิน-จัน" (slavery); the death night stays in the Long.

### C3 — สตราสบูร์ก ฤดูร้อนที่คนเต้นจนตาย

- **Type:** real event (the dancing plague of 1518).
- **Era / place:** Strasbourg, Holy Roman Empire, July–September 1518.
- **Logline:** a woman starts dancing in the street and cannot stop; within weeks dozens, then many more, join her; the city's doctors prescribe more dancing, build a stage and hire musicians, and the cure feeds the epidemic.
- **Hook:** "กรกฎาคม ปี 1518 ผู้หญิงคนหนึ่งเดินออกมากลางถนน แล้วเริ่มเต้น… หกวันต่อมา เธอยังไม่หยุด"
- **Central question:** why couldn't they stop, and what finally stopped them?
- **Beats (Long):**
  1. Cold open: Frau Troffea in the lane, bleeding feet, a crowd watching.
  2. The spread: neighbours, strangers, the numbers rising (as chronicles tell it); the city panics.
  3. The fatal cure: physicians call it "hot blood"; the council clears a hall, builds a stage, pays drummers and pipers. It gets worse.
  4. Midpoint turn: the council reverses, bans music and dancing in public.
  5. Theories laid out as suspects: ergot poisoning, a curse of Saint Vitus, famine and fear (mass psychogenic illness); which fits the facts.
  6. Payoff: the dancers are sent up the mountain to Saint Vitus's shrine with red shoes, and it ends; the best-supported answer is belief itself. Closing: what a city's fear can make bodies do.
- **Ending type:** mystery with the best-supported answer, open question flagged.
- **Voice / tone:** eerie, dry-wry narrator, building dread; one dark laugh at the "more dancing" cure.
- **Why it holds and shares:** absurd-but-true hook, a clear villain (the cure), and a "suspects" structure that invites comments on which theory is right.
- **Research load:** named real event, Boat researches and fact-checks. Hardest: (1) number of dancers and whether anyone died (the "15 a day" figure is disputed); (2) what the council records say about the stage and musicians; (3) the shrine pilgrimage and red shoes detail. **Sensitivity:** religion (Saint Vitus, Catholic ritual) handled as history; mental-health framing kept respectful.
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera with a woodcut/engraving grade and period woodcut-style inserts for chronicles.
- **Long size:** ~10 min; production L (crowd motion, dance loops). Shorts (2): S1 "เมืองที่รักษาโรคด้วยการเต้นเพิ่ม" (the cure backfires), S2 "ผู้ต้องสงสัยสามราย" (theories, no verdict).

### C4 — วันทองไม่ได้สองใจ

- **Type:** novel / literary epic (เสภาเรื่องขุนช้างขุนแผน), retold from วันทอง's side.
- **Era / place:** the epic's Ayutthaya-era สุพรรณบุรี and the royal court.
- **Logline:** the woman Thai culture brands "two-hearted" is married off, told her husband is dead, forced into a second home, abducted back, and finally made to choose between two men before the king; she cannot, and the sentence is death.
- **Hook:** "ผู้หญิงที่คนไทยด่าว่าสองใจมาเป็นร้อยปี… ถูกสั่งประหาร เพราะตอบคำถามเดียวไม่ได้"
- **Central question:** did วันทอง ever actually choose, or was every choice made for her?
- **Beats (Long):**
  1. Cold open: the court, the question, the silence.
  2. พิมพิลาไลย and พลายแก้ว: young love, marriage; he is sent to war.
  3. ขุนช้าง's lie and the mother's pressure: news of death, the forced second marriage.
  4. ขุนแผน returns with ลาวทอง, the fight, then the night he takes her back by force; years on the run.
  5. พลายงาม petitions the king to bring his mother home; the court scene: asked to choose, she answers neither cleanly.
  6. Payoff: count every decision in the story and who made it; the verdict reframed. Closing line returns to the word "สองใจ".
- **Ending type:** tragic, reframing twist (the label was never hers).
- **Voice / tone:** lyrical, a little defiant; short quotes of the เสภา verse where they land.
- **Why it holds and shares:** every Thai knows the label from school, the reframe is a debate starter ("ทีมวันทอง"), and the court scene is pure drama.
- **Research load:** literary, Boat researches. Hardest: (1) which version (ฉบับหอพระสมุด and others) carries which scenes and exact verses; (2) claims about a real historical kernel behind the story; (3) any modern scholarly reading quoted as fact. **Sensitivity:** a king character orders the execution: keep to the text, no link to real reigns beyond what sources state; sexual coercion content handled without graphic detail.
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera: Thai house on stilts, forest camp, royal court.
- **Long size:** ~12 min; production L (court set, period costume). Shorts (4): S1 "ข่าวตายปลอม" (the lie), S2 "คืนที่ถูกพาหนี" (abduction), S3 "คำถามในท้องพระโรง" (ends before the verdict), S4 "นับดูว่าใครเลือก" (the count, teaser of the reframe).

### C5 — เจิ้งอี้เส่า: โจรสลัดที่ไม่เคยแพ้

- **Type:** real person (Zheng Yi Sao / Ching Shih).
- **Era / place:** Canton and the South China Sea, 1801–1810, retirement to 1844.
- **Logline:** a woman from Canton's floating brothels marries a pirate lord, takes his fleet when he dies, writes a code that turns a mob into a navy, beats the Qing, the Portuguese and the British, and then does what no pirate does: negotiates her own pardon and keeps the money.
- **Hook:** "โจรสลัดที่แกร่งที่สุดในประวัติศาสตร์ไม่ใช่แบล็กเบียร์ด… เป็นผู้หญิงจีนคนหนึ่ง และเธอได้เกษียณ"
- **Central question:** how does someone who starts with nothing beat three empires and walk away free?
- **Beats (Long):**
  1. Cold open: 1810, she walks into the governor's office unarmed, with women and children, to bargain.
  2. 1801: the marriage, and the deal she is said to demand (a share of command).
  3. 1807: the husband dies; she takes the confederation and makes Zhang Bao her captain, then husband.
  4. The code: rules on loot, desertion and captives; a fleet that obeys.
  5. War: Qing fleets lost, foreign navies hired against her, she still wins.
  6. Payoff: why she surrenders while winning, the terms she gets, the gambling house and old age. Closing on the word "เกษียณ".
- **Ending type:** triumph with an ironic twist (the winner chooses to stop).
- **Voice / tone:** brisk, confident, a bit of swagger; tension in sea battles.
- **Why it holds and shares:** "not who you think" hook, underdog-to-ruler arc, a satisfying smart ending; shares well as "ผู้หญิงที่เก่งที่สุดที่คุณไม่เคยรู้จัก".
- **Research load:** named real case, Boat researches and fact-checks. Hardest: (1) fleet size and crew numbers (big round figures circulate); (2) her early life and the marriage-contract story (likely legend); (3) the code's actual articles and the surrender terms. **Sensitivity:** violence and sexual violence in the code's subject matter (state, don't show); no living people.
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera: junks at sea, Canton harbour, the governor's hall.
- **Long size:** ~11 min; production L (ships, water, battle). Shorts (3): S1 "สัญญาแต่งงานของโจรสลัด" (the deal), S2 "กฎเหล็กบนเรือ" (the code), S3 "เดินเข้าไปต่อรองมือเปล่า" (ends before the terms).

### C6 — สงครามที่จบไปแล้ว 29 ปี (wild card)

- **Type:** real person / real event (Hiroo Onoda's holdout on Lubang).
- **Era / place:** Lubang Island, Philippines, 1944–1974.
- **Logline:** a Japanese intelligence officer is ordered never to surrender; he treats every leaflet, newspaper and family letter as enemy tricks for 29 years, while islanders die in his "war"; only one man can end it, his old commander, now a bookseller.
- **Hook:** "สงครามจบปี 1945… แต่ชายคนนี้ยังถือปืนอยู่ในป่าจนถึงปี 1974 เพราะไม่มีใครมาสั่งให้เขาหยุด"
- **Central question:** why would a man refuse to believe the war was over, and who could make him stop?
- **Beats (Long):**
  1. Cold open: 1974, a young Japanese drifter in the jungle, alone, meets a man in a patched uniform.
  2. 1944: the order, the island, the mission.
  3. The leaflets: each proof the war is over, and why each one looks fake from inside his logic.
  4. The cost: comrades lost one by one, and the islanders killed or wounded over the decades (told from their side too).
  5. Norio Suzuki, the dropout looking for "Onoda, a panda and the yeti", gets the one condition: a direct order.
  6. Payoff: Major Taniguchi flies in and reads the order; the surrender, the pardon, and a hero's welcome he never fit. Closing: the war only ends when someone says so.
- **Ending type:** bittersweet irony.
- **Voice / tone:** taut, wondering; never glorifies, keeps the victims in view.
- **Why it holds and shares:** the premise is almost unbelievable, the logic of his disbelief is fascinating, and the Suzuki twist is delightful; debate on loyalty vs harm.
- **Research load:** named real case, Boat researches and fact-checks. Hardest: (1) number of islanders killed and incidents (figures vary); (2) the exact order wording in 1944 and 1974; (3) Suzuki's "panda, yeti" quote and later life details. **Sensitivity:** WWII memory in Japan and the Philippines, victims' families, the Marcos pardon (political), no glorification.
- **Medium (wild card):** archival-photo 2.5D collage with an animated paper map and leaflet inserts; the kit has no photo-parallax or archival document pipeline, and real photos need licensing. Fallback: painted 3D camera jungle.
- **Long size:** ~12 min; production L. Shorts (3): S1 "ใบปลิวที่เขาไม่ยอมเชื่อ" (the logic), S2 "หนุ่มที่ออกตามหาโอโนดะ แพนด้า และเยติ" (Suzuki), S3 "คำสั่งเดียวที่หยุดเขาได้" (ends before the reading).
