## Story core

- **Logline:** A Japanese officer on a Philippine island is ordered by his commander never to surrender or kill himself. Leaflets, newspapers and family letters tell him the war is over; his group treats them as enemy tricks. His three companions are gone one by one (one walks out and surrenders, two are shot dead), and his group's raids are reported to have killed about 30 villagers. In 1974 a Japanese traveller finds him; he will only come out on his commander's order. The old commander flies to Lubang and reads the order rescinding it, and he comes out, almost 29 years after the war ended.
- **Central question (hook):** why would a soldier keep fighting for almost 29 years after the war ended?
- **Second question (twist):** if leaflets and his own family's letters could not end it, what could?
- **Character intention / obstacle:** Onoda wants to carry out his order; the obstacle is that every message saying "it is over" comes from outside the chain of command, so he treats it as a trick.
- **Intended feeling:** disbelief (hook) → stubborn isolation → loneliness as the group empties → discomfort at the villagers' deaths (not a hero) → the strange release of the twist → an open moral question.
- **Knowledge takeaway:** (1) history: Japanese holdouts after WWII; Onoda's surrender came in March 1974 on Lubang, Philippines; (2) how an order works: in this case an order could only be lifted by the man who gave it, so outside evidence (leaflets, papers, letters) did not end it; (3) cost: the holdout was not harmless, villagers were reported killed.

## Truth and ending

- **What the sources report (all lead-only, `reported` at best):** Onoda, IJA 2nd Lt., on Lubang from Dec 1944 (C-01, C-02); Maj. Taniguchi ordered him not to surrender or take his own life (C-03); late-1945 leaflets with the surrender order, judged not genuine (C-05); later newspapers and family letters, again distrusted (C-06); Akatsu left 1949 and surrendered 1950 (C-07); Shimada killed 1954 (C-08); Kozuka killed 1972 while they were burning farmers' rice (C-09); group raids, rice burning (C-13); about 30 villagers reported killed, figure unresolved (C-12); Suzuki met him Feb 1974, he would come out only on his commander's order (C-10); Taniguchi flew to Lubang and read the order relieving him, 9 Mar 1974, surrender followed (C-11).
- **Ending chosen before planting clues:** the twist is the release itself: what leaflets and letters could not do, one man's spoken order did. The video ends on a moral question (victim of the order, or responsible?), not a verdict on him.
- **Remaining uncertainty kept visible:** civilian toll (C-12, "ราว 30 คน ตามที่มีรายงาน", group-level); exact order wording (C-04, not used as a quote); surrender day 9 vs 10 Mar (C-11, "มีนาคม 1974" only); his reasons for distrusting the leaflets (S-01 not opened, not stated).
- **Not claimed:** any quoted wording of the order or the leaflets; "exactly 30"; that Onoda personally killed anyone; that all three companions died; the sword/rifle handover; the Marcos pardon (left out for time, see open questions); "30 years" of war.
