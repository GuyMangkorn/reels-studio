## Story core

- **Logline:** Off Kekova Island, a ship with no ship left (just 30-odd clay jars on a shallow seabed) may be the oldest shipwreck ever found, and the jars almost fooled the man who found them.
- **Central question (hook):** if the ship itself is gone, how can anyone tell how old it is?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the archaeologists want a date. The obstacle is that the wood has rotted away, so there is nothing to radiocarbon-date. The only witnesses left are the jars, and at first they misled the team leader.
- **Intended feeling:** quiet wonder at a ghost-ship site, a jolt at "only a century or two old?", then the satisfaction of seeing how the date was actually read.
- **Learning takeaway:** when no wood survives, archaeologists can date a wreck by **typology**, comparing the shape and decoration of its pottery with jars of known age (here, Egyptian and Levantine finds). The result is about 4,400–4,700 years, and the team still calls that date preliminary.

## Truth and ending

- **What happened (documented):** the Kekova wreck lies at roughly 30 m. No wooden hull survives, only a cargo of more than 30 jars (some broken). It was dated typologically to the Early Bronze Age, about 4,400–4,700 years ago. Guinness lists it as the oldest-known shipwreck. The survey leader first took the jars for 100–200-year-old pieces and changed his view after seeing the same forms in a publication on Egyptian archaeology (single original source, BBC, reported via secondary outlets). Some jars hold residue whose contents are still unidentified.
- **Not known, kept open:** why or how it sank (U03), the route (U04), the crew, the contents of the jars (F010), the exact count and depth (U02), and the discovery year (U01, never stated).
- **Ending chosen before clues:** the payoff is the method (shape and decoration of the jars give the date), followed by the honest caveat (preliminary date) and an open closing mystery (unidentified residue). The closing line hands back to the hook for the `B_loop` seam.
