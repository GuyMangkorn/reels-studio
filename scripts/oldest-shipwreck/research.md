- Central question: how can a ship with no ship left be called the oldest shipwreck? Takeaway: archaeologists dated it from the shape and decoration of its jars, because the wood is gone.

## Identification

The brief matches the **Kekova wreck** (Kekova Island, Demre, Antalya, Türkiye), which was announced on 14 Oct 2026 and has "more than 30" jars. It does **not** match the 2024 Israeli deep-sea wreck, which has *hundreds* of jars at ~1.8–2 km depth and dates to ~1400–1300 BC. Dokos (Greece, ~2200 BC) was the previous record holder. Uluburun (~1300 BC) and Cape Gelidonya (~1200 BC) are much younger and are mentioned here from general knowledge only, not re-inspected.

## Claim ledger (documented fact unless marked)

- **F001** Kekova wreck: no wooden hull survives. Only the cargo of jars remains, plus stone anchors. SRC001, SRC007, SRC008. High confidence.
- **F002** The jar count is **"more than 30"** after the 2026 excavation season (SRC004, SRC006, SRC008); SRC005 says "about 30". In the 2024–25 preliminary report the *visible* cargo was smaller: SRC003 gives "about 18". Some jars are broken (SRC001). Only **two** were raised for study, one in 2024 and one in 2025 (SRC001, SRC008). It is unclear how many have been raised in 2026.
- **F003** Age: Early Bronze Age, ~2700–2400 BC (≈4,400–4,700 years). SRC003, SRC004, SRC005, SRC008. The minister rounds this to "4,700 years". The team calls the date preliminary; earlier or later dates are possible (SRC012 via SRC004).
- **F004** Dating method: **typology**, meaning the jars' shape, handles, flat bases and incised decoration are compared with dated Egyptian/Levantine finds. Jar form: "flat-based jars with two handles", some with "a raised horizontal clay 'belt'" around the widest part (SRC012); "two side handles" (SRC005); "side handles and flat bases" (SRC001). One recovered jar has two parallel incised lines on its upper part and a zigzag in the middle; the other jars vary in technique and decoration (SRC001). SRC012 says the date rests on "clues from the cargo, including pottery". There is no radiocarbon date because no wood survives. SRC001, SRC003, SRC008.
- **F005** Depth: shallow, diver-accessible. Figures vary: 20–24 m (SRC003), ~30 m (SRC004, SRC008, SRC010) and 25–40 m on a slope (SRC005). Usable wording: "ลึกราว 30 เมตร".
- **F006** Ship size: a small vessel. "About 10 m" is an *inference* from the cargo size (SRC004, SRC006).
- **F007** Three stone anchors: two with three holes and one with a single hole. They lie at varying distances, and **it is not known whether any belong to the wreck** (SRC001, SRC008).
- **F008** Clay analysis points to Jordan Valley workshops. This is the minister's statement, worded "probably" (SRC008, SRC009). Similar jars are known from Egypt, Troy and Crete (SRC007, SRC009).
- **F009** Discovery story, reported via BBC (SRC007, SRC005): Öniz first took the jars to be only a century or two old ("Ottoman"). He changed his view after seeing the same amphora forms in a publication on Egyptian archaeology, including forms like those found at Giza (SRC007). This is a single original source (BBC).
- **F010** Some jars still hold residue. Karin Sowada (Macquarie Univ.): "It could be olive oil, it could be wine, it could be tree resins." The contents are **unidentified**; XRF/XRD analysis is ongoing (SRC003, SRC005, SRC006).
- **F011** "Oldest" status: Guinness lists Kekova as "the oldest-known shipwreck" at 30 m (SRC010). Media headlines hedge with "may be" or "could be" (SRC003, SRC008, Fox). Dokos was dated to ~2200 BC by HIMA (SRC013). The claim depends on the typological date holding.
- **F012** Who and how: Akdeniz University surveyed the site, led by Assoc. Prof. Hakan Öniz. Excavation began in 2026 under the Antalya Museum Directorate (SRC004, SRC008, SRC009). Divers did the work, not an ROV (Fox photo caption, SRC005 depth).
- **F014** Location: in shallow water near/off Kekova Island, Demre district, Antalya province, southern Türkiye, on a rocky slope that is partly sand and partly rock (SRC001, SRC005, SRC007, SRC008, SRC012). Thai: "นอกเกาะเคโควา ประเทศตุรกี". The Thai MFA form is "ทูร์เคีย"; "ตุรกี" remains common usage.
- **F013** Contrast case, Israel 2024: Energean found it with an **ROV** camera in 2023 at ~1.8–2 km depth, ~90 km offshore. It holds **hundreds** of Canaanite jars, ~1400–1300 BC, and two were raised. The IAA calls it the oldest *deep-sea* wreck (SRC014, SRC015).

## Uncertain or disputed (do not state as fact)

- **U01 Discovery year.** First identified in **2022**, per BBC ("four years ago", SRC005, SRC007), or found/documented in **2024**, per the paper and the team member (SRC001, SRC008, SRC012). Avoid giving a year, or say "สำรวจพบเมื่อไม่กี่ปีก่อน".
- **U02 Exact count and depth.** See F002 and F005. Always say "กว่า 30 ใบ" and "ราว 30 เมตร".
- **U03 Cause of sinking: not established.** r2 correction: a team member writes that the ship "likely sought refuge in the lee waters during a storm" and pictures it crashing against the rocks (SRC012). This is a **hypothesis** with no published physical evidence. "Sank while sailing east to west" (SRC001) is an inference from the debris layout. "Dangerous waters near the island" (SRC006) is a suggestion.
- **U04 Route.** Levant toward Troy or Crete is a hypothesis (SRC007, SRC009).
- **U05 Churn jar.** One jar has nine marks and two holes. The minister *suggested* it was a butter/cheese churn (SRC003, SRC009). "Goats on board" (SRC009) is speculation; do not use it.
- **U06 Missing hull.** Shipworm (*Teredo navalis*) is a plausible explanation offered by SRC005, not a demonstrated cause. Usable wording: "ไม้ผุสลายไปหมด".
- **U07 "First humans to touch them in 5,000 years"** is an Öniz quote and rhetorical. "5,000" exceeds the 4,400–4,700-year estimate.

## Where fiction may begin

May be invented or dramatised (label on screen, e.g. "ภาพจำลอง", and keep it out of narrator assertions): the crew, their faces and number; weather and sea state; the moment of sinking; the hull's look; sailors using the churn; the port names on the voyage. Narration presents a sinking scene as "อาจเป็นแบบนี้" or "ไม่มีใครรู้ว่าจมเพราะอะไร", never as fact. The painted jars should follow the described form: flat base, two side handles, a central ridge, incised lines and zigzag. Do not draw the hull as found on the seabed. Draw 3 anchors loose and apart, not attached to anything.

**Sound:** storm, thunder, screams or a creaking hull are reconstruction cues and must sit inside a labelled dramatised beat. Underwater ambience and diver bubbles are fine for the present-day site. No real recordings are known; if any BBC/ministry footage audio is used, it needs a source and licence.

## Recommended framing and Thai wording

The user's claim **needs qualification**, not reversal. "About 30 jars" is right in spirit ("กว่า 30 ใบ"). "Oldest" must be qualified as "oldest known, if the dating holds", and "the ship" is gone.

- Instead of "เรือจมที่เก่าแก่ที่สุดในโลก" → **"ซากเรืออับปางที่อาจเก่าแก่ที่สุดเท่าที่เคยพบ"**, or "…ที่กินเนสบันทึกว่าเก่าที่สุดเท่าที่รู้จัก".
- "เหลือไหราว 30 ใบ" → **"ตัวเรือหายไปหมดแล้ว เหลือแค่ไหดินเผากว่า 30 ใบ"**.
- Age → "อายุราว 4,400–4,700 ปี" or "ราว 4,500 ปี". Avoid "5,000 ปี".
- Method → "ไม่มีไม้เหลือให้ตรวจคาร์บอน นักโบราณคดีจึงดูอายุจากรูปทรงและลายของไห".

**Hook/payoff angle (supported):** the jars were first thought to be only ~100–200 years old (F009). Then their twins turned up in Egyptian Early Bronze Age finds, and the wreck became a record contender (F004, F011). Fair clue: flat bases and side handles. Payoff: you can date a ship that no longer exists from its pottery. Caveat line: "ถ้าการกำหนดอายุนี้ถูกต้อง".
