## Story core

- **Logline:** a young man waters his seaside garden with a hose while video-calling his sister abroad. His voice is not going up to a satellite: almost all traffic between countries runs along cables on the seabed, because one cable carries far more than a satellite can. In the deep ocean that cable is only about as thick as the hose in his hand, yet it rarely breaks out there. It breaks in shallow water near shore, where ships are: anchors and trawl nets drag along the bottom and snag it. Even in 2024 in the Red Sea, when a ship hit by a missile was followed by three cut cables and it sounded deliberate, the White House said the cables were probably cut by that ship's own anchor dragging as it sank. Cables break a few times a week worldwide, and you barely notice, because traffic just takes another cable; his call may be taking a detour right now.
- **Central question:** why does the internet between countries go under the sea, and what actually breaks it?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the man assumes "abroad = satellite" (V01). Each answer opens a new worry: if it's a cable, it must be thick and safe (no: hose-thin, V02); then the deep sea must be where it dies (no: near shore, V03, the boats in his own bay); then, in a war zone, it must be someone cutting on purpose (missile, three cables), but the likely culprit is a ship's anchor again.
- **Feeling:** surprise at the hook; "huh, a helicopter versus a motorway"; disbelief at the hose; unease when he looks at the boats in front of him; a small turn at the Red Sea case; calm wonder at the end.
- **Takeaway:** almost all internet traffic between countries runs on seabed cables because one cable carries far more than satellites; the danger to those cables is not the deep ocean but ships near shore, whose anchors and fishing nets cause most breaks; there are so many cables that a break usually goes unnoticed.
- **The one rule (ER03 refrain):** "สายไม่ได้กลัวทะเลลึก… มันกลัวเรือ". L10–L12 build it, L13 says it; L15 brings it back in the case that sounds most deliberate (a ship hit by a missile, three cables cut), where the likely cause is still a ship's anchor: the sinking ship's own. The flip inside the rule: the thin, exposed deep-sea part sounds fragile but is the safe part, because nobody is out there.
