## Visual intent

- **Audience knowledge and mood.** Every Thai viewer knows Bangkok as the most crowded place they have stood in, and "eight billion" as a number with no picture. The first frame must say *a city from straight above, rivers of people flowing into it* with no words (UT01). Feelings by beat: disbelief (everyone, you too, here?) → "it really fits" as the map fills, and a laugh when the people run out before Nonthaburi is full → a pull-back that makes the patch small against Thailand → fun at the countdown → a deadpan laugh at the speck smaller than an atom → "obviously" at the balance → surprise that one stadium does shake the ground → quiet smallness at the dot on the globe.
- **Story first (designed before the kit was opened).** **MAN** is a Bangkok guy in his twenties caught up in the what-if on a hot late morning: a plain mustard tee, blue shorts, sneakers, phone in hand checking where he is. He is the viewer's stand-in ("รวมทั้งคุณด้วย"): annoyed by the squeeze, game for the jump, relieved on landing. **The crowd** is everyone on Earth: every skin tone, age and dress (office shirts, school uniforms, a monk's robe, a yukata, a nurse, a hoodie, a vendor's apron), packed shoulder to shoulder, mostly patient, some craning to look around. **The city** is Bangkok seen two ways: from straight above as a living painted map (the Chao Phraya's brown loops, the grey grid of the core, green rice land in Nonthaburi and the west, the Gulf in the south), and at street level on a Sukhumvit-like road under the concrete BTS viaduct with 4-storey shophouses, awnings, signs without text, cable bundles on poles. **Seattle** is a generic open bowl stadium, drawn, never named: a grey January afternoon for the game, a warm stage-lit night for the concert, and a small instrument box on the pavement across the street. **Space** is a deep navy with stars, the Earth lit full by a sun from the upper left.
- **Medium.** Painted 2D p5.brush in world units for every place: the street and stadium sets in metres through the kit yaw camera (`ycam.ts`, `cam-moves.ts`); the map, Thailand and the globe in a geographic space in kilometres with a straight-down camera whose altitude is the zoom (EP14 `topdown.ts`/`lod.ts` method, extended to a sphere). People are 4-head kit rig cards squared to the lens; seen from straight above they are a new top-view person (head disc, shoulders, arms). The atom and balance pictures are fixed-plane painted diagrams in the same space palette. Screen layers ("ภาพจำลอง" label, OT01–OT06, captions, channel mark) are Remotion DOM above the painted canvas, as in the series. No footage, no photos, no Three.js (Blender deactivated).

Map: the same sun gives a 1 px shadow on block edges and small drifting cloud shadows at altitude above 20 km. Space: the sun upper left; the globe fully lit with a soft limb darkening and atmosphere glow; no terminator in frame (the dot sits in daylight). Stadium day: soft overcast, no shadows, damp sheen on the concrete. Stadium night: the stage is the key (warm white), coloured beams cut the haze, phone lights speckle the floor, the instrument box lit by one street lamp `#f2c46a` and the stadium glow behind.
- **Camera language.** Map shots are straight down, the altitude carries the move (a "powers of ten" rise and fall), with a slow yaw drift of 2–6° so the map never sits dead still. Street shots are close (UT13) and at head height or just above the crowd, the camera moving 0.2–0.5 m inside the set. The globe is held back until L15: the L08 Earth picture shows only a curved limb (never the whole disc) so the payoff image is new.

## Projection contract and scale baseline

- **Named spaces; one rule: every shot keeps one size reference in frame** (a person, a road, the province outline, the Earth). Status `design choice`; sizes `estimated` (staging values, not narrated).
  - `G` geographic (kilometres). Earth sphere R 6,371 km; points as (lat °N, lon °E, height km).
