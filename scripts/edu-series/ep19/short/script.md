## Story core

- **Logline:** a ten-year-old girl plays hide-and-seek with her mum's trained tracking dog, and keeps losing. Round 1: the dog is brought onto the middle of her trail, slows, sniffs a few of her footprints for a few seconds and picks the right way. Every print smells of her, but the earlier ones are probably a little fainter, so the dog goes toward the stronger ones, like you following the smell of fried rice. Round 2: "the older the smell, the fainter" gives her a plan: make Mum wait twenty minutes so the trail fades away. A twenty-minute-old trail on grass is still enough: the dog picks the right way again. Round 3: if the dog reads the prints one by one in order, she scrambles the order. She steps across a row of square mats to the tree and Mum shuffles the mats. Now the dog stands in the middle and cannot tell which way she went. The fading never hid her; the scrambled order did.
- **Central question:** how can a dog tell which way you walked, from only a few footprints, when every print smells of you?
- **Intention / obstacle:** she wants to beat the dog once. Each plan meets a fact: (1) the prints are all "her", so how can it know the way? (the prints differ step to step); (2) wait until the smell fades (twenty minutes is not enough); (3) break the order (it works, and that shows what the dog was reading).
- **Feeling:** surprise at the hook; "oh, like following a cooking smell"; a giggle at her waiting plan and her confident sniff; deflation when the dog wins again; a spark when she gets the idea; a small triumph at the end, with the dog's puzzled head tilt.
- **Takeaway:** a trained tracking dog can tell which way a person walked from a handful of footprints. The leading explanation is that earlier prints smell a little fainter and the dog follows them toward the stronger ones; what is certain is that the **order** of the prints is the clue: scramble the order and the dog cannot tell the direction.
- **The one rule (ER03 refrain):** "กลิ่นยิ่งเก่า ยิ่งจาง". L04 states it as her hope (wait and the trail fades away); L05 shows it does not hide her; L10 turns it: the fading never hid her, the scrambled order did. The rule moves from her hope, to the dog's arrow (fainter to stronger, in order), to her winning trick (break the order).

## Film brief → scene sheets (story constraints; Film owns the approach)

- **Must be seen:** a ten-year-old Thai girl (four-head cast), playful and stubborn; her mum, a dog trainer (trainer's vest, long line in hand); one dog in a **tracking harness on a long line** in every round, so "trained tracking dog" is read from the picture. No breed is named or claimed; avoid a look that reads as "the bloodhound, the best tracker" (folklore, F011). A grassy training field with a swing at one end and a big tree at the other, a fence with a **stack of square rubber mats** and cones visible from the first shot (CL02).
- **Essential staging (logic of the story):** in all three rounds Mum brings the dog **in from the side to the middle of the trail**, so it truly has to choose left (swing, where she started) or right (tree, where she is). Starting the dog at her start point would make the direction trivial and break the hook.
- **The dog's behaviour:** it **slows and sniffs for a few seconds** over two to five prints before choosing (F006); it never turns at once. In round 3 it sniffs, looks left, right, left, then sits with a head tilt.
- **Glow (illustration of a hypothesis):** only on prints near the dog's nose as it sniffs, reading dim to bright toward where she went. **During the twenty-minute wait (W-WAIT) no glow at all**: do not show the prints fading together or keeping their order over time (F009, unsupported). On the shuffled mats the glow is jumbled (bright, dim, bright, dim), no direction.
