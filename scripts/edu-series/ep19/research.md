## Short answer

1. **The silent, upright response is real as an observation but is one pattern among several, from small samples.** Pia (1974) described it from film/observation of 13 people at Orchard Beach (as reported by SRC001). The 2020 expert video study (23 videos, 24 drowning people) saw the response nine times and found "no evidence ... of persons waving for help" and "no indications that any of the drowned persons tried to shout or scream"; but 17 of 42 people in the qualitative set fit no named pattern, and the most common new pattern ("backward water milling", n=19) made clear splashes (SRC001). So: "silent and no waving" is well supported; "no splashing, always the same look" is not (F01, F02, F08).
2. **The time number must change.** "20–60 s" is Pia-attributed, repeated by Red Cross texts, and traced to one 1995 Red Cross textbook (SRC002); it is not independently measured. The only measured study found a longer struggle: unrescued people (n=5) struggled a mean 92 s, resurfaced about 13 times, and "at most 2 min" to recognise and rescue (SRC001). **Solid line: "ไม่ถึงสองนาที" / "ไม่กี่สิบวินาทีถึงราว ๆ สองนาที". Cut "under a minute"** (F03).
3. **Why they cannot call or wave: observed yes, mechanism only Pia's explanation.** That they do not shout or wave is observed (SRC001). "The mouth is barely above water, so breathing takes every breath and speech comes second; arms press down to lift the head" is Pia's account, repeated by secondary sources (SRC002, SRC003); I could not open an independent physiology source. Narrate it as one plain cause with "แทบ" (ER10) or keep only the observation (F04, F05).
4. **Lookalike checklist (short, secondary-sourced):** upright as if treading water; head tilted back, mouth at or dipping under the surface; arms out to the sides pressing down, "climbing an invisible ladder"; little forward movement, not waving; glassy or blank look. Compared with playing: players splash, make noise, move forward, can answer. Test: ask "ไหวไหม?"; a blank stare is a warning (SRC003, SRC005, SRC007). "Ladder motion" is the weakest item: the 2020 study says it found no evidence for that claim in the cited source (F06). Use two or three items at most.
5. **Thai child number: usable in one line, but it does not match the pool setting.** Opened, official-attributed: **173 children under 15 drowned in Mar–May 2024 (school holiday), nearly 2 a day** (Thai PBS 12 Mar 2025, citing MOPH Strategy and Planning Division; SRC009). Earlier: 953 in Mar–May of 2018–2022 (Thai PBS 3 Mar 2023, citing DDC; SRC010). **Important:** in the same DDC data, 76.5% of child deaths were in natural water and only **1.8% in pools and water parks**; main cause "playing in water" (59.4%) (SRC010). The video's setting (condo pool) is rare in Thai child deaths. Not a contradiction of the lesson, but a one-line stat must not suggest "pools are where Thai children die" (F09, F10).
6. **Safe bystander action: Thai official line is "ตะโกน โยน ยื่น", and do not jump in.** DDC deputy director (Thai PBS 12 Mar 2025): shout for help, throw a floating object (e.g. a closed empty plastic gallon), reach with a stick, rope or cloth; "ไม่ควรกระโดดลงไปช่วย" because the rescuer may drown too (SRC009). Same advice from Red Cross "Reach or throw, don't go" and Outdoor Swimming Society/RLSS-based page (call for help first; if untrained do not enter; throw ring or rope, otherwise reach) (SRC004, SRC008; Red Cross page itself not opened). Solid (F11, F12).
7. **Contested (cut or hedge):** (a) "ไม่มีเสียงน้ำกระเซ็น" (backward milling splashed); (b) "20–60 s" and "under a minute"; (c) "every drowning looks like this" (RLSS UK warns media overstate it; people differ by ability and situation; SRC006 lead-only); (d) "ladder motion"; (e) the IDR was described mainly for non-swimmers (SRC002), while the story's man is an adult of unstated skill; (f) "children go quiet" (one blog list, no study). Also the older terms "silent/dry/active/passive drowning" were dropped by the 2002 World Congress on Drowning (SRC011, secondary): the video can say "เงียบ" as a plain description but should avoid presenting "silent drowning" as a medical term (optional).
8. **YouTube:** the word "ตาย" is not restricted in titles, captions, narration or cover. Policy tests are what is shown and promoted, not vocabulary. See the policy section.

## Contrary evidence and limits (item 6)

- No critique paper of Pia found; the caution is empirical. SRC001 supports the silence and no waving, but narrows the picture: only part of its sample matched Pia's response, new patterns appeared with splashing, and the sample is a pilot of YouTube footage.
- RLSS UK warns that popular "silent drowning" stories imply one look for everyone (lead-only, SRC006). Pia's source sample was 13 people (SRC001) and the response was described mainly for non-swimmers (SRC002).
- Many web pages (Vittone reprints, blogs, SRC004) repeat Pia and one another; they count as one original (Vittone/Pia), not independent corroboration.
- Source for the C55 note "often 20–60 s": the figure traces to a 1995 textbook, not measured research.

Findings:

1. **No word list.** None of the three pages restricts the words "death", "die" or "ตาย". The harmful-content page uses "death" only to describe what it prohibits promoting (dangerous acts "that risk serious physical harm or death"). Drowning appears there only as an example of asphyxiation games and challenges. The tests are what the video shows and encourages, not vocabulary.
2. **Violent/graphic:** prohibited is shocking footage (corpses with severe injuries, road-accident gore, content meant to "shock or disgust"). Dramatized or fictional violence must be clear that it is fiction. EDSA context must appear in the video's images or audio; titles and descriptions alone do not count. Our video shows no body, no real footage and no gore; the dramatization label is a one-line safeguard.
3. **Advertiser-friendly:** "Discussions of loss of life or tragedy that are not exploitative or dismissive can earn full ads" and educational, non-graphic death content can earn full ads. Limited ads apply to "highly graphic descriptions of tragedies". Titles and thumbnails are held to stricter standards than the video: graphic imagery there can lower ad status even when the video is educational. Content that uses a tragedy's keywords to drive traffic is penalized (news events); not our case.
4. **Fake-death pranks:** the harmful page lists "Fake death or suicide" pranks on minors or vulnerable people. The boy shouting "ช่วยด้วย" as a game is a dramatized scene in an educational video, not a prank filmed on real people; keep it clearly illustrated.
5. **Conclusion:** the word is safe in narration, captions, title and cover. Concern is graphic imagery and sensational framing on the cover. Safer and equally clear wording if the user wants extra margin (all optional): title "คนจมน้ำจริง ๆ ไม่ได้โบกมือ" or "จมน้ำเสียชีวิต" instead of "จมน้ำตาย"; "อันตรายถึงชีวิต" for stakes. Keep the hook line natural; "จมน้ำตาย" is ordinary Thai and the policy does not target it. Cover: waterline picture with the still head, no face distress close-up, no dead or limp body, no blood.
6. **Not verifiable:** whether automated classifiers treat any word differently in practice. YouTube publishes no word list; community claims about word-avoidance are unsourced and not evidence. Thai-language versions of the pages and any Shorts-specific limit were not opened.

## Open questions for Preaw

1. Beat 4 clock: replace "under a minute" with "ไม่ถึงสองนาที" (measured, SRC001) or "ไม่กี่สิบวินาทีถึงสองนาที"; the C55 line "Often under a minute" cannot stay.
2. Drop "arms press like pushing up from the pool edge" detail? It is Pia's analogy; if kept, one hedge ("มัก"/"แทบ").
3. The flip line may stay, but a viewer could infer a shouting swimmer is safe. Add or keep a clause so the shouter is not dismissed, or accept as optional.
4. Thai stat: use the 2024 school-holiday figure with its caveat, or no number? Pool is 1.8% of child deaths; Preaw's choice whether one line supports the arc without implying pool is the main place. Boat suggests no number (ER07: it adds a beat, not the answer). If used, add no claim that natural-water drownings are also silent: that is unsourced.
5. Rescue payoff: the story has the woman pull him to the edge. Official advice is shout, throw, reach, and not jump in. The picture should show her throwing or reaching, or calling the lifeguard, not diving in untrained. Film note: the C55 staging "she pulls him to the edge" needs to be a reach/throw.
6. "Adult man, head back": the response is described mainly for non-swimmers. Keep him cast as a weak swimmer; no line should say every adult drowns this way.
