# Research packet: "Dogs can tell which way you walked from about five footsteps" (edu, C72)

**Author:** Boat (research stage only; no script, no wording fact-check yet). **Research date:** 2026-11-15. **Channel:** edu (Thai Short, ~50 s). **Revision:** R1. **Concept:** `scripts/edu-series/concepts/C72.md` (Preaw pitch).
**Story type:** edu, counter-intuitive-claim check. **Central question:** what does the evidence actually show about dogs reading the direction of a human trail, and does the pitch's mechanism ("older steps smell fainter") hold?
**Learning takeaway candidate:** trained tracking dogs can work out which way a person walked from as few as five footsteps (3 was too few); the favoured explanation is that they compare how the scent changes from step to step. That explanation is a hypothesis, not a measurement.
**Access note:** the full text of Hepper & Wells 2005 (OUP) and Wells & Hepper 2003 (ScienceDirect) was blocked (403), and a further fetch attempt to reach the OUP PDF by another route was refused by the permission system, so I did not retry. Both studies are read only through their abstracts (Europe PMC record) plus secondary summaries. Methods detail not in an abstract (trail age, dog breed, spacing) is therefore `unresolved`. Confidence is qualitative.

Tags: `documented fact` / `reported` / `folklore` / `hypothesis`.

## 2. Claim ledger

### F001 Dogs can determine the direction of a human trail from footsteps (the core claim)
- **Verdict:** `documented fact` for trained tracking dogs under test conditions.
- **Evidence:** SRC001: six dogs "able to determine direction", brought in at right angles to the centre of a trail, did better than chance (P < 0.025). SRC002: four trained German shepherd tracking dogs always followed the track in the correct direction in 10 tests on 20-min-old grass tracks and 10 tests on 3-min-old concrete tracks. SRC003: two professional tracking dogs, 50 m tracks, took the walking direction significantly more often than chance, also when the track-maker walked backwards.
- **Limits:** all trained or professional dogs. SRC001 dogs were pre-selected as ones that could already do it. Very small samples (6, 4, 2 dogs). Pet-dog generalisation is not shown (see F010).
- **Usable wording:** "หมาตามรอยที่ฝึกมา บอกทิศทางที่คนเดินได้จากรอยเท้า"

### F002 About five footsteps are enough; three are not
- **Verdict:** `documented fact` for the stated conditions (SRC001).
- **Evidence:** SRC001 abstract: footsteps reduced 21, 11, 9, 7, 5, then 3; "Dogs were able to determine direction from 5 footsteps but not 3." The blog (SRC010) and SRC008 repeat it (not independent).
- **Conditions/limits:** one footstep per carpet square, 21 squares, same person and shoes throughout; dogs entered at right angles to the centre of the trail; six dogs. Four footsteps were **not tested**, so five is the smallest tested number that worked, not a proven minimum. Whether the abstract's 5 means exactly the dogs smelled five squares (not "about five") is the design as stated, so "ห้าก้าว" is accurate for that experiment; "ราว ๆ" is not needed for the study but is safer if the line is read as an everyday claim about real trails.
- **Usable wording:** "ในการทดลอง หมาตามรอยตัดสินทิศได้จากรอยเท้าแค่ห้ารอย แต่สามรอยไม่พอ"
- **Pitch status:** hook "แค่ห้าก้าว" **supported with a scope note** (trained dogs; lab-like carpet trail).

### F003 The cue is the person's own odour, and the order of the steps matters
- **Verdict:** `documented fact` (SRC001 abstract).
- **Evidence:** shuffling the order of the carpet squares removed the dogs' ability; removing the individual's odour while leaving ground disturbance also removed it, "indicating that it was the odour of the individual".
- **Usable wording:** "ถ้าสลับลำดับรอยเท้า หมาก็บอกทิศไม่ได้"
- **Story use:** a strong, true "proof" beat that fits the detective genre (the order of the prints is the clue).

### F004 WHY: "each older step smells slightly fainter than the newer ones"
- **Verdict:** `hypothesis` (the standard explanation, behaviourally consistent, never measured on the footprints).
- **Evidence for:** SRC003: "We suggest that our dogs determined the direction of tracks by comparing the scents of consecutive prints and following the prints in the direction of increasing intensity." The explanation rested on indirect tests (bicycle tracks with leather strips; strips smeared with sausage made the dog go the wrong way until it was rewarded and learned). SRC002 cites that idea. SRC001: the dogs fail when step order is shuffled, consistent with a step-to-step gradient.
- **Evidence against / gaps:** SRC001's abstract says "the odour information in footsteps changes" over about 1-2 s; it does **not** say the older steps are fainter (it may be concentration or composition). SRC001 says only "the process by which dogs may determine direction from odour cues is discussed" (full text unread). I found no measurement of the intensity of individual footprints over time. SRC002 states the dog's skill "must rely on accurate methods of sampling air and a remarkable sensitivity for certain substances", i.e. the authors do not claim to know the cue.
- **Pitch status:** the line "ทุกก้าวเริ่มจางตั้งแต่ก้าวนั้นเกิดขึ้น" and "ก้าวแรกเหยียบก่อน กลิ่นก็จางกว่า" are stated as fact; they are a hypothesis. They need "นักวิจัยคิดว่า" / "น่าจะ".
- **Usable wording:** "นักวิจัยคิดว่า หมาเทียบกลิ่นรอยเท้าทีละรอย แล้วเดินไปทางที่กลิ่นแรงขึ้น"

### F005 "Time between steps is only seconds, yet the dog can read it"
- **Verdict:** `documented fact` as to the number given; the reading of it is the authors' calculation.
- **Evidence:** SRC001: "it takes approximately 1-2 s for the odour information in footsteps to change to provide discernible information". Calculated by the authors (method not seen).
- **Limits:** this is the time for the information to become discernible, not necessarily the spacing of the steps. Unresolved: how it was calculated.
- **Usable wording:** "ผู้วิจัยคำนวณว่า กลิ่นในรอยเท้าเปลี่ยนพอให้หมาจับได้ภายในราว 1-2 วินาที"

### F006 The dog sniffs only a few prints and decides fast
- **Verdict:** `documented fact` (SRC002 abstract), with the "at once" wording needing change.
- **Evidence:** the dogs' "deciding phase lasted 3-5 s, while the dogs sniffed at 2-5 footprints", moving at half the speed with sniffing periods three times longer.
- **Pitch status:** beat 1's "sniffs four or five prints and turns her way" is **supported**; "turns the right way at once / without hesitating" is **not** (the dog slows down and sniffs 3-5 s first). Nicely, "slows down and sniffs 3-5 seconds" is a truer, more detective-like image.
- **Usable wording:** "หมาชะลอ ดมอยู่ราว 3-5 วินาที ที่รอยเท้าสองถึงห้ารอย แล้วจึงเลือกทิศ"

### F007 Dog sniffing: exhaled air exits to the sides/downward and a nose sniffs several times a second
- **Verdict:** `documented fact` for the airflow pattern as seen in models and measurements; the "never blows the trail away" and "pushed sideways and back" wording is wrong.
- **Evidence:** SRC005 (3D-printed dog nose, flow visualisation, Craven co-author): the nostril "ejects an expired air jet that is directed ventral-laterally (downward and to the side)"; this "prevents the blow-off of odorant sample directly in front of the nostril" and "entrains ... air in front of the nose toward the nostril". Also: the jets "mechanically disturb and volatilize latent odorant"; "a complete loss of signal occurs when the air jets blow all vapor ... away" in the artificial-nose test when the source is in the jets' path. A canine sniff is "approximately 5 Hz" (SRC005, citing earlier work). SRC004 (Craven 2010): sniffing is alternating inhalation and exhalation; the nostril's inhalation "reach" at peak is about 1 cm, matching the distance dogs hold the nose from the ground when tracking (cites SRC002); each nostril samples a separate patch of air (bilateral samples, "may be used" for localisation; the authors' wording is a possibility, not a result).
- **Attribution error in the pitch:** Craven et al. 2010 is mainly about airflow **inside** the nose (CFD of one dog's MRI-based model at 5 Hz plus sniff measurements on a few dogs); I found no "side slit" description in its text. The exhale-jet detail is from the external-aerodynamics work (SRC011, not opened) and SRC005. If the script cites a study, cite the correct one.
- **Wording problems:** "ไม่เป่ากลิ่นปลิวเลย" is too strong (see above); "back" is not in the sources; "smell without stopping" is misleading, since the dog smells while inhaling and exhales between. The 3D-printed nose and CFD are models; dog tests were on a few dogs.
- **Usable wording:** "ตอนหายใจออก ลมจากจมูกหมาพุ่งลงและออกด้านข้าง จึงไม่เป่ากลิ่นตรงหน้าจมูกให้ปลิว" (narrow, matches SRC005) or cut the beat (see section 4).

### F008 Human exhaling "blows a print away" (the girl's failed sniff)
- **Verdict:** `unsupported` as a measured fact; comic dramatisation.
- **Evidence:** I found no study of a human exhaling onto a footprint. SRC005 shows jets can blow all vapour away in a model when the source sits in their path, which is analogous but is not the human case.
- **Handling:** label as dramatised or drop the implied claim; the dog-nose line (F007) does not need the human comparison.

### F009 "Wait long enough and the trail goes cold; steps all fade together so the newest is still the strongest"
- **Verdict:** `unsupported` for the "all fade together, order stays the same" mechanism; "wait and it vanishes" is `folklore` that the evidence argues against only in part.
- **Evidence:** no primary source measures footprint decay or tests direction-finding on old trails. Direct data points on age: SRC002: 20-min-old grass tracks, 3-min-old concrete tracks, always correct. SRC006: trained search dogs found a hidden person 800 m away in 93% of trials on trails aged 1-3 h (this tests finding the trail, not reading direction). SRC008: secondary statement that trained dogs read direction from five steps on an "hour"-old trail (citing Hepper & Wells; I could not verify the trail age in SRC001, abstract silent). Practitioner claims of days-old trails (SRC012) are anecdote and not used.
- **Contrary/limits:** trails age with weather, surface, heat and wind; no source shows the ordering of steps survives hours; the dog's ability may fail with age or poor conditions (unmeasured here). "Trail can't go cold" is false as a universal; dogs do lose very old trails (SRC012, anecdote).
- **Pitch status:** problem 3's answer is **not solid**. Recommendation per ER07: cut, or reduce to a measured line: "ในการทดลอง รอยที่ทิ้งไว้ราว 20 นาที หมาก็ยังบอกทิศถูกทุกครั้ง" (SRC002, 4 dogs). That also flips CL01 cleanly (her plan "wait" did not hide her) without asserting a mechanism.

### F010 Does this apply to an ordinary pet dog?
- **Verdict:** `unresolved`; evidence is for trained/professional tracking dogs (SRC001-003). SRC008 states that "for untrained dogs the scenario is sharply different" and cites Polgár et al. 2015 (not opened) for pet dogs sometimes failing to pick up odour more than about a metre away.
- **Impact:** the pitch's girl and her pet dog, plus the hook's bare "หมา", imply any dog does it. This is a wrong-takeaway risk (must-fix scope). Options: make the dog a trained tracking dog in the story, or scope the narrator ("หมาตามรอยที่ฝึกมา").
- **Related lead (unverified):** Wells & Hepper 2003 (SRC007): by one search summary, of 22 police dogs only 8 (36%) were consistently correct and males/younger dogs did better; not confirmed. Treat "not every dog" as plausible but verify before use.

### F011 Breeds
- **Verdict:** no breed comparison found. SRC002 used German shepherds, SRC003 two "professional tracking dogs" (breed not in the abstract), SRC007 police dogs (lead). C72 makes no breed claim; do not add one ("bloodhounds are best" is folklore here).

### F012 Tracking on the ground vs air-scenting
- **Verdict:** the evidence is mixed; keep the claim narrow.
- **Evidence:** SRC006: search dogs hunting a hidden person searched head-up and fast when near the trail and nose-down and slow when farther, i.e. both modes are used; SRC002: the dogs sniffed footprints at about 1 cm from the ground (via SRC004). SRC001 shows the cue is the person's odour (not ground disturbance).
- **Pitch status:** the payoff line "หมาไม่ได้ดมว่าคุณอยู่ไหน… มันดมว่าคุณไปทางไหน" is a fair framing of the direction test but overstates: dogs also find people by air-scent (SRC006). Optional wording fix: "ก่อนจะวิ่งตามรอย หมาต้องรู้ก่อนว่าไปทางไหน"
- Also: SRC003 found dogs could not tell direction of bicycle tracks on grass/asphalt (no discrete footprints), so "a continuous trail without separate prints" is not enough; a limit worth remembering, not needed in the Short.

### F013 "ไม่ได้อ่านเวลา" (does the dog read time from smell?)
- **Verdict:** `unresolved`; nothing in the evidence says dogs read "how old" a step is, only that they compare consecutive prints' odour. C72 correctly avoids it. Keep it out; a popular "dogs smell time" claim (blogs) concerns a different 2018 study (owner's absence, not trails) and is not for this Short.

## 4. Supported story angles
1. **"Can you trick a tracking dog by waiting?"** Her plan: wait behind the tree. The documented side: trained dogs read direction from five footsteps (F002), on 20-minute-old trails (F009 replacement), by comparing the steps in order (F003), a clue that cannot be faked by shuffling (the shuffle test).
2. **"The order is the clue."** The shuffled-carpet-squares experiment (F003) is a true, visual, detective-style proof: same prints, scrambled order, and the dog is lost. Strong as the flip, with "น่าจะ" on the why.
3. **"Hypothesis, honestly told."** Narrator: "นักวิจัยคิดว่าหมาเทียบกลิ่นทีละรอย" (F004), a one-cause answer that stays inside ER10.
4. **Dog-nose beat (optional):** F007's true line is a good single inside-view picture; cut it if it pushes any answer past 10 s (ER09).

## 5. Gaps / unresolved
