## Story core

- **Logline:** in America a person in a mask caught a few crows, put a ring on each leg and let them go. After that, whenever the mask walked by, crows mobbed it, scolding and following. With no mask they barely cared. When a woman wore the same mask, they mobbed her too: they track the face, not the person. Then crows with no ring, never caught, joined in, because they saw the others do it. Years later, young crows hatched after that day scold the mask like their parents. Long after, the crows that saw that day nearly all died and the scolding grew quieter; but a few crows were caught, and the memory reached crows that never saw it.
- **Central question:** what exactly do the crows remember, and how do crows that were never there know it?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the man wants to walk his path in peace; the mask he wore once keeps getting him mobbed. Each "แต่…" tests a simpler explanation (they know him; any stranger; only the caught birds) and the answer narrows to the face, then spreads it.
- **Feeling:** amused surprise at the mob; "huh" at the bare-faced walk; a laugh at the woman's protest; a small chill at the ringless crows joining; a cold turn at the young crows; a quiet dip at the fade; the stare into the lens on the last line.
- **Takeaway:** some crows remember the face of a person who caught them for years, and crows that were never caught, even ones born later, learn to scold that face from the others.
- **The one rule (ER03 refrain):** "มันไม่ได้จำคน… มันจำหน้า" (L07). B02–B03 test it; B04 spreads it across crows; B05 flips it into time: the face outlives the day and the crows that saw it.
