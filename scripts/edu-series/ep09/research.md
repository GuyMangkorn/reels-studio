# EDU-EP09-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP09-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, concept C28 "นิ้วนางที่ยกไม่ขึ้น", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md` `### C28`). Research date: 2026-11-08.
- Central question: why does the ring finger refuse to lift when the middle finger is folded under, and what can the narrator safely say about it, about musicians and about Schumann?
- Short answer: (1) The test result is real and common but varies by person: `settled` as an observation, `hedge` as "almost everyone". (2) Textbook anatomy is `settled`: shared extensor digitorum (ED) tendons joined by juncturae tendinum; index and little finger have their own extra extensors. (3) The exact claim "the ring finger shares ITS lifting muscle with the middle finger, so a folded middle finger pins it" is a `model`: it is the standard teaching explanation, but no inspected source tests this exact posture. (4) Limit is mostly body structure plus some nervous-system limits; sources disagree on the split: say "both", not "tendons only". (5) Schumann: cut or keep only as a hedged aside; the device story is contested, the "tendon" explanation is one minor theory. Recommendation: cut from the 40–60 s Short.
- Tooling limits: web search excerpts plus fetched pages. PMC and PubMed pages returned bot checks, so Lang and Schieber 2004, Häger-Ross and Schieber 2000 and the juncturae anatomy papers are known only through search excerpts and secondary pages (`lead-only` for content). **No real try of the test was possible here (no hands-on tool); the task file requires a real try before scripting: the coordinator or user must do it (see Q1).** No gore or injury imagery is needed anywhere in this episode.
