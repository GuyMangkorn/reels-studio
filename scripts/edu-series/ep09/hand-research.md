## 2. What a viewer sees

### 2a. Visible proximal segment

The kit's shares are of the visible finger. The real first segment is cut by the web (the skin between fingers hides the start of the proximal phalanx). Let f be the fraction of the PP bone that the web hides; the visible total is then 1 - 0.5 f (using the R2 shares, E).

My estimate of f for a dorsal view is **0.3 to 0.5** (E, U): the palmar crease sits at about 0.44 PP (S1, above), and the dorsal web is usually taken as at or somewhat proximal to the palmar one. `hand-proportions.md` assumed about 0.25 (E). I could not verify this, so I give the whole range. Note that if a viewer reads the finger from the knuckle bump instead of the web edge, f = 0 and the middle segment looks short (MP/PP 0.57), which is the most likely source of the user's "too short" if the knuckle is drawn strongly (U).

MP/PP depends on the web: A's flat ring 0.92 matches f of about 0.4, and G's 1.46 would need f of about 0.6, which no inspected source supports.

### 2b. Arched, steep top camera (TP)

Foreshortening (E): each segment's projected length is its length times the cosine of its tilt from the table plane. In a slight arch the knuckle is highest, and the proximal phalanx dips about 10-20 deg, the middle about 30-45 deg, the distal about 40-60 deg (my assumption, no source: U). So middle/proximal falls by cos(mid tilt)/cos(prox tilt), about 0.73-0.93 (centre about 0.85), and middle/distal falls too, by cos(mid)/cos(dist), but only to about 0.9-1.0 (so the real MP/DP seen in TP stays about 1.2-1.4). The kit's own measured TP/flat ratio is 0.74 (A 0.68/0.92, G 1.09/1.46), at the low end of my range.

Where it is uncertain: (1) the web hide fraction f (0.3-0.5 assumed, nothing measured), which sets the proximal vs middle balance; (2) all numbers are adult, none child; (3) the TP tilts are my assumption; (4) G, E and F need f of 0.6 or more to match MP/PP and still overstate MP/DP (1.48-2.0 vs 1.3-1.6), so I cannot support them. If the user's eye wants the middle longer than B, the lever with evidence is the knuckle: a stronger dorsal MCP bump or a visible PIP crease line makes the middle read longer, rather than stretching the share (U, a design judgement, not research).
