## จริงดิ list (ER07)

1. Elite keepers jumped to a side in about 94 of every 100 penalties in the study (93.7%), and stayed in the centre in about 6 (F002). Confidence: medium-high on the figure (two sources, one original-derived), the sample of stays is tiny.
2. In the same data, the centre stay saved 33.3% against 12.6% and 14.2% for jumps to the right and left (F003). Confidence: medium for the numbers, low-to-medium for what they prove (about 18 stays, my arithmetic from 6.3% of 286; not stated in the sources).
3. Keepers said so themselves: of 32 top Israeli keepers surveyed, 15 said their position would change how bad they felt after a goal; 11 of those 15 said they would feel worse after staying in the centre (F005). Confidence: medium (one summary, counts only).
4. A kick takes about 0.4 s to cross 11 m at about 100 km/h (F008, own arithmetic from a source range). Confidence: medium. The keeper has to pick a side before he can see where the ball goes.
5. Do not use as a wow line: "kickers aim at the centre about 29%" (F004) is true-ish but unsurprising and loosely sourced.

## Pitch claims checked (C105)

- "Keepers who stay in the middle save more, yet almost nobody does it": half-supported. Almost nobody stays (F002, true in the study). "Save more": true in the study's numbers (F003) with a tiny, disputed sample (F013). Safe, bold enough: "ในงานวิจัยหนึ่ง".
