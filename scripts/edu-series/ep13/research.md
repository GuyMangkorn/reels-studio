# EDU-EP13-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP13-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, concept C39 "ถ้าหมัดตัวเท่าคน", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md` `### C39`). Research date: 2026-11-09. Mode: scale what-if (animal jumping and body size). No script is written here.
- Short answer:
  1. **"เป็นร้อยเท่า" is not supported for height. Hedge down to "หลายสิบเท่า".** Cat flea body about 2–3 mm (secondary, SRC006); cat flea height: median 13.2 cm, best single jump 17 cm in tubes (SRC001, abstract text via a secondary page). 13–17 cm over 2–3 mm is about 45–85 body lengths, so "หลายสิบเท่า" or "เกือบร้อยเท่า" at the very best, never "เป็นร้อยเท่า". Only the best long jump (48 cm, about 160–240 lengths) reaches a hundred, and that is distance, not height. Film's est. 180 m naive arc (100 lengths) drops to about 80–150 m at 45–85 lengths for a 1.8 m flea; a 30-storey tower (about 100 m) is still a fair stand-in, but label it a model.
  2. **Mechanism: `settled`.** The flea stores energy in an elastic resilin pad slowly and releases it in about 1 ms, so muscle alone could not do it (SRC002, SRC003, SRC008). Take-off acceleration is `hedge`: sources say about 100 g (SRC003) and about 150 g (SRC004 summarising the 2011 paper); both are for the flea species the authors used, not confirmed as the cat flea. Say "หลายสิบเท่าของแรงโน้มถ่วง" or leave it out. Also `settled`: force reaches the ground through the toes, not the knees (SRC002, SRC003); not needed for a 50 s Short.
  3. **Person's jump: about 25–55 cm, so "พอ ๆ กัน" is safe only as "same order of size, tens of centimetres".** Standing countermovement jump 34.6 cm (men) and 25.7 cm (women), sports-science applicants (SRC007); a Canadian norm page gives a middle 50 % of 38–53 cm for men aged 30–34 and 26–36 cm for women (SRC007b, `lead-only`). A real cat flea reaches 13–17 cm, so the real flea jumps **lower** than a person, by a factor of about 2–3. The scaling argument (SRC005): if work per kilogram of muscle is the same at every size, jump height does not depend on size ("Borelli's law"), so a person-sized flea would land within tens of centimetres, not tens of metres. `model`, not a measurement; nobody has a person-sized flea. Wording: "สูงพอ ๆ กัน" with "ราว ๆ" and "ตามแบบจำลอง"; never "เท่ากันเป๊ะ".
  4. **Giant-flea body: `hedge`, only as far as sources go.** Haldane (SRC009): scale ten times in every direction, weight rises a thousandfold, surface a hundredfold; insects breathe through tiny air tubes (tracheae) and diffusion over long distances is slow, so he argued insects of much more than about half an inch thick are not workable. A person-sized flea therefore could not simply be a scaled-up flea; this is the safe line. I found no source for legs snapping, an exoskeleton failing or the giant dying on a specific schedule; do not say them as fact (see Hedges).
  5. **Beat 2 wording: use "work" (energy), not "power" or "strength".** Muscle mass grows with size cubed, as does body mass, so a bigger animal has proportionally the same muscle to lift the same weight over a stroke that is proportionally longer (SRC005, geometric similarity). `settled` as a model, `hedge` as a claim about real animals: real small animals fall short because muscle power per kilogram is limited, which is why fleas need the spring (SRC005, Bennet-Clark 1977 seen only through a citation list).
  6. **Dog/pet safety: no claim needed.** Do not say "หมัดบนตัวหมาคุณ" as if every dog has fleas, do not say fleas harm or are dangerous, and give no treatment, product or "check your dog" advice (concepts.md Film risk 4 agrees). Whether the cat flea is the commonest flea on Thai dogs is `unchecked` (it is the species tested above; it is also the dog flea in SRC001's study design, which compared C. canis and C. felis). Safe hook: "หมัดตัวเล็ก ๆ กระโดดได้สูงกว่าตัวมันเป็นสิบเท่า".
- Tooling limits: web search excerpts plus fetched pages. Full text of Sutton and Burrows 2011 (JEB 214:836), Burrows 2009 (JEB 212:2881), Cadiergues 2000 and Bennet-Clark 1967 returned 403 or a cookie wall or were not found; I used abstract text and press or secondary summaries (all labelled). ## What the evidence supports for a 40–60 s Short

- Flea about 2–3 mm; jumps about 13–17 cm; in tens of body lengths; powered by an elastic pad released in about a thousandth of a second.
- A person jumps about 30–50 cm; the model says a person-sized animal's jump is the same order, tens of centimetres.
- The giant flea in the story is a thought experiment; the beat that a real giant could not exist (breathing and weight) is Haldane's argument and may be said as a hedge.

## Hedges the Thai narration must carry

- "หลายสิบเท่า", not "เป็นร้อยเท่า" (CL04). Tower arc must be shown as "ถ้าเป็นไปตามที่เห็น" (naive extrapolation, not a measurement).
- "ราว ๆ", "ตามแบบจำลอง", "ประมาณ" for CL08, CL09; the flea's real height is lower than a person's, say so or avoid "เท่ากันเป๊ะ".
- "แผ่นยางยืดแล้วปล่อย" for resilin; do not say "สปริงโลหะ".
- Closing: choose between "มันแค่ตัวเล็ก" and any line claiming equal ability (CL13).
- Dramatization labels: the person-sized flea, the man's reaction and the side-by-side jump are fiction; the dog scratching is dramatization of an everyday scene, not evidence the dog is infested.

## Gaps and thin spots

- Cadiergues and Sutton-Burrows numbers not opened at the publisher; one more pass with library access would close CL03, CL06.
- No cat-flea mass and no species confirmation for the 2011 high-speed study; 1.5 mm / 0.45 mg belongs to a different flea in a secondary summary.
- Scaling reviews seen only as excerpts; Bennet-Clark 1977 not opened.
- Human jump data from sports-science applicants and an unverified norm page; no flea-vs-person measured comparison exists (the model is the only support).
- Haldane's essay is an old estimate; no modern source on a person-sized insect.
