## Claim ledger

Confidence is qualitative. "Fact line / hedge / not usable" is the usage verdict. Thai is the boldest wording the evidence supports (ER10).

### The programme

- **F001 — What and when.** In 1964 the CIA began exploring a cat fitted with a microphone and transmitter to listen to open-air conversations (park benches, "foreign agents and their Soviet handlers"), on the idea that the cat "could move close to the conversation without attracting any notice". The programme was ended in 1967, never used operationally. Directorate of Science and Technology ran it. SRC004 (primary, agency), SRC002 (DS&T); Wikipedia agrees (1960s, cancelled 1967). Confidence **high** on 1964–1967 and "never operational". **Fact line.** Safe: "ปี 1964 CIA เริ่มลองใช้แมวแอบฟังบทสนทนาในสวน … โครงการจบปี 1967 โดยไม่เคยใช้งานจริง". "To spy on Soviets": the CIA says handlers of Soviet agents on park benches; the popular "Kremlin" and "Soviet embassy in Washington" versions (Wikipedia, Marchetti) are not in the CIA text: say "สายลับโซเวียต/คู่สนทนาในสวน" only as the CIA does, not "ที่สถานทูตโซเวียต" (**hedge**).
- **F002 — The build.** CIA (SRC004): "a microphone was implanted in one ear, a very small transmitter was embedded under its loose skin, and an antenna was woven into its long fur." Other accounts differ on the transmitter: base of the skull (Anthes via History.com and WETA citing *Spycraft*: "inch-long transmitter"); abdomen / rib cage with a wire from the inner ear to a battery cluster (Bailey via Smithsonian, seen through PopSci); Marchetti: "they slit the cat open, put batteries in him, wired him up. The tail was used as an antenna" (SRC002). Agreed by all: microphone at the ear, a transmitter and battery inside the cat, an antenna in the fur or tail. **Fact line** at that level; **do not state where the transmitter sat** (sources conflict). Safe: "ผ่าตัดใส่ไมค์ที่หู ฝังเครื่องส่งในตัว ถักเสาอากาศไว้ในขน". The pitch "antenna in the tail fur" is Marchetti-only for "tail"; the CIA says "long fur": use "ขน".
- **F003 — Surgery was harmless?** CIA says the devices went in and came out in "a low-risk microsurgical procedure under the same humane conditions found in any veterinary clinic, and the cat was unharmed" (SRC004); Wallace says the cat was re-sewn and "lived a long and happy life" (via WETA/Wikipedia, not seen). Marchetti implies a rougher process ("monstrosity"). Self-interested on one side, hearsay on the other. **Hedge**: "CIA บอกว่าแมวไม่เป็นอะไร"; do not assert it as independent fact. Matches the pitch's off-screen surgery.

### The memo (primary, read)

- **F004 — "Views on Trained Cats".** SRC001, read in full. Facts: March 1967 (stamp of the scan 3-67 in the corner); 2 pages; Doc ID 21897; "MEMORANDUM FOR:" and the audience, the author, the object of the training and the use are redacted. Wording that survives (verbatim):
  - ¶1 "Our final examination of trained cats ... convinced us that the program would not lend itself in a practical sense to our highly specialized needs. Repeated checks on the state of training and equipment showed us that it was indeed possible to train ..." (rest redacted) "... we were not able to visualize ... use for this technique under conditions that prevail".
  - ¶2 "We have satisfied ourselves that it is indeed possible ... This is in itself a remarkable scientific achievement. Knowing that cats can indeed be trained to move short distances ... we see no reason to believe that a ... cat can not be similarly trained to approach ..." (redacted) "Again, however, the environmental and security factors in using this technique in a real foreign situation force us to conclude that, for our ... purposes, it would not be practical."
  - ¶3 "The work done on this problem over the years reflects great credit on the personnel who guided it, particularly [redacted] whose energy and imagination could be models for scientific pioneers."
  - What the memo does **not** say in readable text: microphone, transmitter, taxi, hunger, cost, "cats do not obey". The reason it gives for "impractical" is "environmental and security factors in a real foreign situation", not obedience.
  - **Fact line.** Safe: "เอกสารลับปี 1967 เรียกการฝึกแมวว่า 'ความสำเร็จทางวิทยาศาสตร์ที่น่าทึ่ง' แต่สรุปว่าใช้งานจริงไม่ได้". Hedge for the exact trained skill: only "เดินไปในระยะสั้นได้" is readable; do not write "ฝึกแมวให้ไปแอบฟังได้" (redacted).
  - **Pitch ending "สรุปสิ่งที่คนเลี้ยงแมวรู้ฟรี ๆ: แมวไม่ทำตามสั่ง" is not usable as the memo's conclusion.** It is usable as the narrator's joke if the memo is not said to say it, or attached to the CIA's own 2024 line (F005), which does say the cat could not be controlled.
- **F004b — Release.** The NSA published it in Briefing Book 54 on 10 Sep 2001 (SRC002, SRC003; PDF created 30 Aug 2001). Source line says "Donation". The scan carries a "RELEASED" stamp with a date that Boat reads as September 1983 but could not read with confidence. The date the CIA cleared it is therefore **unresolved**. **Fact line**: "ถูกเปิดเผยต่อสาธารณะปี 2001 ผ่าน National Security Archive"; **do not say** "CIA declassified in 2001" (hedge: "เอกสารที่หลุดออกมา/เปิดเผยปี 2001").

### What went wrong

- **F005 — The cat could not be steered.** CIA (SRC004): "the feline had a mind of its own and was impossible to control. During limited field tests, the cat went wherever it felt like and not to the places we wanted it to go." Wallace (via Wikipedia, WETA): abandoned for training difficulties and operational impracticality. Marchetti: the cat "would walk off the job when he got hungry" (F007). Confidence **medium-high**. **Fact line**: "CIA ยอมรับเองว่า แมวไปไหนก็ได้ตามใจ ควบคุมไม่ได้". The "limited field tests" count is unknown; the CIA wording says "tests", plural.
  - Contrary evidence: animal trainer Bob Bailey (Smithsonian 2013, via PopSci): "We never found an animal we could not train" and disputes that the project was a total failure. Wikipedia: "Other accounts report more success." Keep "ควบคุมไม่ได้" attributed to the CIA.
- **F006 — First test, two versions.**
  - **Version A (taxi).** Source: Victor Marchetti, former CIA officer, in Curtis's BBC film (1995; not seen) and the Telegraph (2001; not seen): "They took it out to a park bench ... They put him out of the van, and a taxi comes and runs him over. There they were, sitting in the van with all those dials, and the cat was dead!" (quote as printed in SRC002 from NSA gloss). The NSA gloss, AP 2001, History.com (via Anthes), Mental Floss and PopSci repeat it. **One chain from Marchetti.** Reliability: Marchetti was a former agency insider (job title varies: "special assistant to the director" / "executive assistant to the deputy director" in different sources; not resolved) who left in 1969 and became a critic; he was not described as present at the test. Colourful and secondhand; the quote is a spoken recollection, not a document.
  - **Version B (no death).** Source 1: the CIA (SRC004): the cat-killed-by-cab-or-bus story "traces to a former CIA official who in 1979 admitted his comments were meant to be a joke". The official is not named; Boat did not find the 1979 admission anywhere else. Source 2: Robert Wallace, former director of the Office of Technical Service (TV documentary, via Wikipedia and WETA, not seen): the equipment was taken out, the cat re-sewn "for a second time" and "lived a long and happy life", relayed from "the guy who was the principal in the project" (SRC006, 2013). Source 3: Bob Bailey (Smithsonian 2013, seen only via summaries) disputes the taxi story.
  - **Which is better supported?** Neither has a primary document: the memo is silent. Version B has the institution that ran the programme on record, and it is a document Boat read; but it is self-interested and unsourced. Version A is the louder, older story but rests on one recollection that the CIA says was a joke. **Verdict: both are claims, not facts.** No decisive evidence; do not choose by taste. As fact lines only the dispute itself is solid: "เรื่องที่เล่ากันว่าแมวถูกแท็กซี่ชน CIA บอกว่าเป็นมุกของอดีตเจ้าหน้าที่คนหนึ่ง" (**hedge**, attributed to the CIA), or "ตามคำเล่าของอดีตเจ้าหน้าที่ … แต่ CIA ปฏิเสธ". Preaw's plan to leave the taxi ending out is consistent with this.
  - Date of the first test: WETA says 1966; no inspected primary says so. **Not usable.**
- **F007 — Hunger and "another wire".** Only Marchetti says it: "They found he would walk off the job when he got hungry, so they put another wire in to override that" (SRC002). Wikipedia and Mental Floss repeat it ("another operation"). The CIA page does not mention hunger; no document does. Confidence **low** (single recollection). **Usable only with the hedge**: "อดีตเจ้าหน้าที่คนหนึ่งเล่าว่า แมวเดินหนีงานเวลาหิว ทีมเลยเดินสายเพิ่มเพื่อกดความหิว". Not a fact line; do not state it as the narrator's assertion. The pitch's B04 hunger-surgery line and the "เด็กฝึกงานผ่าตัดไม่ให้หิว" comparison are acceptable as a joke tied to "ตามคำเล่า" wording.

### Cost

- **F008 — Cost.** No primary figure: no document inspected states a cost; the CIA page gives none. Figures in circulation: about **USD 20 million** attributed to Marchetti (BBC 1995 per Wikipedia; Telegraph 2001 per Mental Floss: "additional surgical and training expenses are estimated to have brought the total cost up to $20 million"); **USD 15 million** (Unredacted 2013; WETA headline: "after five years and fifteen million dollars"); "between $10 and $20 million over years of research" (WETA citing the Telegraph). A 1967 meeting memo that the director could not justify "substantial funds" for more cat training and a 1969 memo ("modest expenditure") are reported by WETA, not opened. **No best-sourced number exists**; the only attributed figure is Marchetti's ~20 million. Confidence **low**: all figures are 1960s dollars, unadjusted, from one chain. **Usable with a hedge**: "มีรายงานว่าใช้เงินราว 10–20 ล้านดอลลาร์ (ตัวเลขจากอดีตเจ้าหน้าที่ ไม่มีเอกสารยืนยัน)" or the safer "หลายล้านดอลลาร์". Not usable as "CIA ใช้เงิน 20 ล้าน" flatly. Do not convert to baht or inflation-adjust without a stated basis (Boat can add if asked).
