## Story core

- **Logline.** In the 1960s the CIA wanted a spy nobody would suspect, so it wired a cat: a microphone in the ear, a transmitter inside, an antenna woven into the fur, to sit near park-bench talks between agents and their Soviet handlers. In tests the cat went wherever it liked except where the team wanted; one former officer says it walked off the job when hungry, so they added another wire. In 1967 an internal memo called the cat training "a remarkable scientific achievement" and concluded it was not practical. Today the CIA's own page says the cat had a mind of its own, something every cat owner knows.
- **Central question.** The world's top spy agency against an ordinary cat: who wins?
- **Victim, intention and obstacle.** The CIA team wants a perfect, invisible spy. The spy is a cat. Every fix (surgery, more wire, more tests) leaves them with the same cat, and the end of the story is their own paperwork admitting it (ER02). The comedy aims at the plan and the agency, never at the cat; the surgery stays off screen.
- **Feeling.** A spy-thriller parody told by a friend who is gleeful about it; a "จริงดิ" on the build, on the memo's praise and on the CIA admitting it in public; every cat owner feels smug by L08.
- **Takeaway.** The CIA really did implant a microphone and transmitter in a cat (Acoustic Kitty); the technology was not the problem; the cat could not be steered, and the 1967 memo shelved it while calling it a scientific achievement.

## Truth and ending

- **What actually happened (research R1; fact-check pending).** From 1964 the CIA explored a cat fitted with a microphone and transmitter to listen to open-air conversations of foreign agents and their Soviet handlers, ended in 1967, never operational (F001, F013). The CIA's own description of the build: microphone in one ear, a small transmitter under the skin, antenna woven into the long fur (F002). The CIA says the cat "had a mind of its own and was impossible to control", going "wherever it felt like and not to the places we wanted it to go" in limited field tests (F005). One former officer (Marchetti) says it walked off the job when hungry and another wire was added (F007, single recollection). A March 1967 memo calls it "a remarkable scientific achievement", gives "environmental and security factors in a real foreign situation" as the reason it is not practical, and praises the people behind it (F004); the memo became public through the National Security Archive in 2001 (F004b).
- **Kept off narration on purpose.** The transmitter's location and any "tail" (F002, sources conflict); the cost (F008; not needed, and never the headline); the taxi story and the first test (F006: a dispute only, and it would cost about 6 s; the pitch's care note already kept it out); the number of cats (F011); "Kremlin" or "embassy" (F001); whether the cat was harmed (F003); the memo's reason, which is not spoken at all, so nothing can make it sound like "cats don't obey"; the 1964 start date (picture only, if Film wants it); the memo's release route (description only, F004b).
- **Joke lines that must stay jokes.** L02 topper; L04 and L07 AGENT lines (generic agent; L07's target / bird / sunny spot is an invented gag, F012); L05 deadpan; L10 "ให้ข้าวก็จบแล้ว"; L14 callback. "เก่งที่สุดในโลก" in L01 is the narrator's attitude, not a claim.
- **Ending.** L13 states the CIA's own 2024 page: the cat had a mind of its own, a thing every cat owner knows. L14 pays the hook: "ใช่… ตัวที่เรียกแล้วไม่เคยมาน่ะแหละ", word for word L02's topper, so the story ends where the viewer knew it would and the agency needed a programme to get there (CL01). Hold 2.0 s (ER03). L13 is attributed to the CIA's page, not to the memo, and opens on "เว็บ CIA" so it never reads as the memo's conclusion.
