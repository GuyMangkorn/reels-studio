# EDU concepts — pitch lessons

Hypotheses and framing notes Preaw and Film wrote between pitch rounds, kept when the unmade concepts were removed (2026-11-13; source commit 8cf02ff). Read before pitching a new round.

## Why EP03 worked (hypothesis) (R2)

Hypothesis only: no view, retention or share analytics were supplied; these are Preaw's guesses to steer R2, not findings.

- **Your own pet, secretly different:** the viewer already lives with the subject, so the reveal is about their home, not a distant fact.
- **One-move visual reveal:** the palette flip shows the dog's world in a single striking change that only a painted frame does cleanly; the idea is grasped before the narration explains it.
- **Myth-buster with a soft landing:** "not black and white" corrects a belief most people hold, without making them feel stupid.
- **A tip owners send each other:** "buy blue toys" is an action, so sharing it is helpful, not just "look at this".
- **Low-stakes warmth:** a cute animal failing then succeeding is safe, cheerful and easy to rewatch on a loop.

R2 concepts each try to keep at least three of these five.

## Why EP05 worked (hypothesis) (R3)

Hypothesis only: no view, retention or share analytics were supplied; the only signal is the user's report that EP05 beats EP03. These are Preaw's guesses to steer R3, not findings.

- **"You" in an impossible body:** the hook puts the viewer's own body under impossible rules, so every beat is felt, not just learned.
- **A promise that sounds false but is true:** "fall ten storeys and be fine" makes the viewer stay to see how; the payoff confirms it.
- **A world-flip image only painting does cleanly:** the macro world (grass as forest, dew as boulder) is beautiful on its own and readable before the narration explains it.
- **A chain of small surprises:** each beat is a new consequence (falling, water, cold), so attention resets every 10 s instead of one fact explained for a minute.
- **Ends on a rule, not a fact:** "same world, different rules, depending on your size" is a big idea people quote and share.

R3 concepts each try to keep at least three of these five. Shared with EP03: "your everyday world, secretly different" (C15, C16).

## What R5 keeps from R3 and R4 (hypothesis) (R5)

Hypothesis only, from the user's keeps and drops (no analytics supplied):

- **The test is in the body, not in the room:** no mirror, cup, stairs or rain needed; hands, breath, nose or a yawn. The viewer can do it the second the hook ends.
- **One painted inside-view per concept:** the dropped C22 had none; every kept R4 concept had one (film strip, ghost body, bubbles, light in a thread). R5 keeps that as a rule.
- **"Wait, it has a name?" plus a twist that flips the obvious:** C23's "dry is the fake colour" and C24's GPS twist were kept; each R5 concept ends on a flip (the fan never cooled the room; you do touch things after all).

## Safety framing (Film, all six) (R10)

General: every danger is a cartoon re-enactment, the character ends alive, there is no gore, burn, wound or body in distress beyond a short flinch or strain (<= est. 1.5 s), no line or picture shows a step a viewer could copy to recreate the danger, and every rescue picture shows only what Boat's source supports. A "ภาพจำลอง" tag on the danger frames is Film's suggestion for C56, C57, C60; the coordinator or Boat decides.

- **C55 (drowning):** the drowning person is an adult, never a child, and his face stays calm, not choking; no submerged body. The rescue is the risk: an untrained bystander jumping in is something water-safety bodies commonly warn against (unchecked, Boat). Film's staging: he is near the corner, so she reaches him from the edge (arm, a pool noodle or a lifebuoy from the wall) and pulls him in; she does not dive in. If Boat's source supports a different safe action, the picture follows it. No breath-hold games on screen.
- **C56 (crash):** dummies, never people, and no blood; the old-car dummy's hit is a lurch and a squeak, with the dummy's face cartoon-dazed. No brand, badge or grille shape that names a maker. No speed number on screen unless Boat's source sets it.
- **C57 (electricity):** the most sensitive. No live wire touched on purpose, no "back-of-hand test", no voltage or current number, no lightning bolt, skeleton flash or burn mark. The contact is a short tremble and a strain, then the breaker. The woman reaches, stops short and turns to the breaker: she never touches him while the current is on, and she is never shocked on screen. The cracked cord is shown as the cause (an old cord), and the fan is not shown being "fixed". The wet hand comes from the shower, but the water heater is never shown as faulty (a common heater is not the claim).
- **C58 (lift):** the shaft gap is shown as a black void with a rail glint; nobody falls, slips or hangs on screen, and the pry never shows the viewer half-out. The passenger is shown waiting calmly by the end; the alarm and intercom are the action.
- **C59 (heat):** the dog is distressed but never collapsed, vomiting or limp; it lifts its head and drinks at the end. No child in the car, no parent blamed. No breaking a car window on screen (not part of the claim).
- **C60 (fire):** the fireball never touches the boy; he stands back from the plume and flinches. No scald, burn or singed hair. The mother turns the gas off first, then slides the lid on from the side; she does not carry the wok, throw flour or salt, or use a towel unless Boat sources it. The water throw is shown once, framed as the mistake.

Ranking by reach vs cost:
1. **C55**: M (high end); the most settled evidence and the strongest life-or-death image in a place every viewer goes; the waterline cut and three swimwear looks are the cost, and the rescue picture needs Boat's safe action.
2. **C56**: M; the strongest comment war answered by one stacked split frame; everything is new but nothing is a new capability (crumple is keyframed polygons).
3. **C57**: M; EP09's see-through forearm method makes the key picture affordable; the most safety-sensitive staging.
4. **C59**: M (low end); the cheapest build and the existing `dog-golden`; the risk is a lecture tone, which the dials help.
5. **C60**: M (high end); a quotable ending, but the only concept that needs a new fire layer and a one-shot fireball.
6. **C58**: M; heavy EP11 reuse, but one untested capability (torch light pool), the thinnest evidence, and the ER02 fix adds a passenger.

**Film's recommendation: C55 first, then C56**, the same order as Preaw. Claims and sound intent are Preaw's R10, unchanged; nothing here is checked fact.
