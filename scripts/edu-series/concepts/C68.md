# C68 — ถ้าการซ่อนคือทางรอด โลกเราซ่อนไม่ทันแล้ว

- Status: **pitched** in round R12 (Preaw, W0.1, 2026-11-14).
- Channel: `edu`. **One standalone Short** (~60 s, estimated; no read-aloud yet). Ends in one go; no series.
- Scope (user via coordinator, 2026-11-14): two concepts only, each one standalone episode.
- Framing (ER07): the Dark Forest idea (a thought experiment from Liu Cixin's novel *The Dark Forest*, one of many proposed answers to the Fermi paradox) is the premise, stated once as "ถ้า…", never as true. Everything after the premise is real: what humans actually sent into space, and what anyone could read from Earth's air without any signal. Facts `unchecked` until Boat's research.
- Angle vs C67: C67 teaches the **logic** of hiding (why a smart civilization stays silent). C68 accepts the rule for a minute and tests **us** against it with real objects and real astronomy: have we already been found out?

## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Genre:** "what if" premise, answered with real-world surprises; a twist ending.
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera (project default), with one flat SVG "blueprint" overlay for the real message diagrams (the golden disc, the 1974 radio picture).
- **Our cast:** a Thai teenage boy (four-head cast) who has just watched a sci-fi clip and decides Earth must hide. He goes round "switching Earth off".
- **Logline:** If being found means being destroyed, humans broke the rule long ago: we mailed our address to space on purpose. Luckily space is too big for the shout to arrive. But when the boy tries to make Earth go fully silent, he hits the one signal no one can switch off: the air we breathe.
- **Counter-intuitive claim (ER01):** humans have already sent Earth's address into space on purpose, and even if we went completely silent from today, Earth could still be spotted as a living planet, because of its air.
- **Rule that flips (ER03):** "ซ่อนเสียงได้… แต่ซ่อนลมหายใจไม่ได้". The boy treats hiding as switching things off; the flip shows the one thing that gives us away is the thing keeping him alive.
- **Hook (Thai):** "ถ้าการเปิดเผยตัวในจักรวาล แปลว่าถูกทำลาย… มนุษย์ส่งที่อยู่บ้านออกไปแล้วนะ ตั้งแต่ก่อนคุณเกิด"
- **Objection by ~7 s:** "ส่งจริงเหรอ?" → a golden disc spins past the camera: "จริง แผ่นนี้แหละ"
- **Beats (each opens the next with "แต่…"):**
  1. Hook (0–7 s): the boy, phone in hand, panics; the golden record flies past into black.
  2. Problem 1 (~8–18 s): "แต่ ยานลำนี้ จะไปถึงใครเมื่อไหร่?" Answer (one cause): it is so slow that it needs tens of thousands of years just to pass near another star (`unchecked`: Voyager 1 / Gliese 445, about 40,000 years). He exhales.
  3. Problem 2 (~18–30 s): "แต่ เราไม่ได้ส่งแค่ยาน… เราส่งด้วยคลื่นวิทยุด้วย" — the 1974 Arecibo picture beamed at a star cluster. Answer: even at the speed of light, it needs about 25,000 years to arrive (`unchecked`). Payoff of the hook at ~30 %: "ตะโกนไปแล้วจริง… แต่จักรวาลกว้างจนเสียงเรา ยังไม่พ้นหน้าบ้านเลย"
  4. Problem 3 (~30–42 s): "แต่ ถ้าตั้งแต่วันนี้ เราปิดทุกอย่างให้เงียบสนิทล่ะ?" The boy flips a giant switch; Earth's radio glow fades out. "แต่ คนอื่นไม่ต้องฟังเรา… แค่มองก็พอ" Answer (one cause): when a planet passes in front of its star, starlight shines through its air, and each gas leaves its own mark in that light (everyday comparison: light through a glass of coloured water; no term). Our own telescopes already read the air of far planets this way (`unchecked`: JWST, e.g. carbon dioxide on WASP-39b).
  5. Problem 4 (~42–52 s): "แต่ อากาศโลกมีอะไรให้ดู?" Answer (one cause): air this full of oxygen is hard to keep without living things making it all the time (`unchecked`; Boat sets the single narrow hedge, ER10).
  6. Payoff flip (~52–60 s): the boy takes a breath; pull back to Earth's thin blue edge glowing against the Sun. "เราปิดเสียงได้ทุกเครื่อง… แต่ลมหายใจของทั้งโลก บอกทุกคนอยู่แล้ว ว่าที่นี่มีชีวิต"
- **Ending:** closed on the flip, ~2 s hold on the glowing blue edge; `_A_fade`.
- **Voice / tone:** channel default narrator **Kore**, warm smiling storyteller, amused at the boy's panic ("นะ", "หรอก"); the boy reacts with small sounds ("เดี๋ยวนะ!", a relieved sigh after beat 2, a held breath before the flip).
- **Why it holds:** two surprises most viewers don't know (we mailed our address; air is a beacon), each grounded in real objects; the relief at beat 3 is pulled out from under the viewer at beat 4, a mid-video rehook; the last line turns breathing into the giveaway, quotable and personal.
- **Research load:** named real cases (Voyager Golden Record / Pioneer plaque, Arecibo message), general astronomy (transit spectroscopy, JWST exoplanet atmospheres, oxygen as a sign of life). The heaviest of the two concepts.
- **Length risk (ER09/ER02):** four problems in ~60 s is tight. If the read runs long, beat 2 (Voyager) merges into the hook and beat 3 carries the "slow shout" alone.
- **Sound intent:** warm low strings under the disc flyby; a playful pluck on "เดี๋ยวนะ!"; each blueprint diagram draws with a soft pen-scratch tick; a radio chirp motif for the Arecibo beat that thins and echoes as it travels; a relief chord at the ~30 % payoff; the big switch is a heavy clunk and the radio hum dies into near silence (beat 4 rehook); a glassy shimmer as starlight passes through air; for the flip the bed drops out, a deep breath-like swell rises with the blue glow, then one soft bell. Sound never replaces a clue: the disc, the switch, the starlight through air and the blue edge are on screen.
