## Pitch (Preaw)


- **Sub-genre:** animal jumping and body size (why big and small animals jump about the same height).
- **Staging:** `dog-golden` scratching on a Bangkok sofa; macro of a flea on its fur; the flea grows to person size beside `man-parted + home-lounge` at a wall height chart; a side-by-side jump.
- **Counter-intuitive claim (ER01):** a flea the size of a person would not jump over a building; it would jump about as high as you.
- **Logline:** A flea jumps many times its own height, so people imagine a person-sized flea clearing a tower. But the muscle that pushes grows with size as fast as the weight it has to lift, so animals of very different sizes jump to roughly the same height. The tiny flea even needs a trick you do not: its legs are too short for muscle alone, so it loads a spring and fires it. The "super flea" is just small.
- **Hook (Thai):** "หมัดบนตัวหมาคุณ กระโดดได้สูงกว่าตัวมันเป็นร้อยเท่า… ถ้ามันตัวเท่าคน จะกระโดดข้ามตึกได้มั้ย"
- **Hook claim status:** `unchecked`, `model` (cat flea, the common one on Thai dogs and cats, jumps about 15–20 cm from a body of about 2 mm; Boat sources the jump study and body length and checks "เป็นร้อยเท่า").
- **Rule that flips (ER03):** "ตัวใหญ่ขึ้น… กระโดดได้สูงเท่าเดิม" (bigger body, same jump height). Cost to the giant flea in beats 1–3; beat 5 flips it into a compliment for the viewer.
- **Key reveal image:** the man and the person-sized flea jump side by side against the wall chart; both peak at about the same line; a tag "สูงพอ ๆ กัน".
- **Beats:**
  1. The flea grows to person size; the man looks up at a 30-storey tower and gulps. `unchecked`, `model`
  2. แต่… its muscle grows, and so does its weight, at the same pace; the extra power only lifts the extra weight. `unchecked`, `model` (shown as two growing weights on a seesaw, no term)
  3. แต่… then how does the tiny flea jump so well? Its legs are too short for muscle to push in time, so it squeezes a rubbery pad like a spring and lets go. `unchecked`, `settled` (resilin catapult; Boat sources)
  4. แต่… a big body does not need a spring: the giant flea jumps with plain muscle and peaks at the man's height. `unchecked`, `model`
  5. Flip and payoff: back to the dog scratching; the flea leaps off. Last line: "หมัดไม่ได้เก่งกว่าคุณหรอก… มันแค่ตัวเล็กมาก" `unchecked`, `model`
- **Ending:** closed, ~2 s hold on the dog and the man on the sofa.
- **Voice / tone:** Kore, brisk deadpan; the man one "เฮ้ย ตัวเท่าเรา!" and a jump grunt; the dog a scratch and a sigh.
- **ER08 pitch check:** (1) Hook: breaks "fleas are super-jumpers that would scale up"; one picture, no term. Pass. (2) Who: everyone who has seen a flea or a pet; pet owners most. Pass. (3) Beats: grow → แต่ weight → แต่ the spring → แต่ the giant jumps; beat 2 is the one explanation beat and is shown as a seesaw, kept short. Pass. (4) Ending: a payoff that flips the myth. Pass. **4/4.**

Flea jump height and the spring pad need one sourced study each. Kept out: "a giant flea would collapse under its own weight" (that is C13's story) and flea-control advice.

#### C39 — scale and perspective plan (Film, W0.2)

All numbers are est. until Boat's research (flea body length and jump height, man's standing-jump height) and the brief; frames at 30 fps (EDU compositions are 1080x1920 at 30 fps, `edu05/look-index.ts`).

- **S1 — One baseline contract, three spaces.** Room space `R`: metres, origin on the floor at the foot of the wall height chart, +x screen right, +y up, +z from the wall toward the camera; the chart on the wall plane z 0, 0–2.6 m, ticks every 10 cm, labels every 50 cm. Macro space `M` (dog fur): 1 set-mm per real mm (k 1000 sm/m), fur strands as world-space ribbons (EP05 `macro.ts` method), a screen-layer scale bar "2 มม." in every macro shot. Street space `S` (the myth shot only): metres, origin at the tower's base. Every shot keeps one size reference in frame: fur and the scale bar (`M`), the man and the chart (`R`), the man and the tower (`S`).
- **S2 — What "ตัวเท่าคน" means on screen (decision for the brief, F-C39-D1).** Recommended **A: the flea's body length = the man's height, 1.80 m** (scale k ≈ 900 from a ~2 mm flea; Boat's number sets k), so its standing height is est. 1.0–1.1 m. Reason: a flea in profile is about twice as long as it is tall; matching its *height* to the man (option B, k ≈ 1,200) makes it est. 2.7 m long, and the side-by-side then needs a 4 m-wide frame at 270 px/m, which shrinks the man to ~490 px. Option A fits a 3.0 m-wide frame at 360 px/m (man ~650 px). With A the honest comparison is **feet clearance**, which is what jump height is: at the apex both actors' feet sit on one chart band. The Thai line "ตัวเท่าเรา" still reads if the first person-size frame shows the flea's length beside the standing man.
- **S3 — The comparison shot (CAM-CHART).** Man at R (-0.95, 0, 0.35), flea at R (+0.40, 0, 0.35) (feet centre; flea spans x -0.50 to +1.30): **both on the same depth plane z 0.35 (Requirement for the reveal;** a depth mismatch fakes a height difference). Camera at R (0, 1.9, 10.3), target R (0, 1.9, 0.35), yaw 0, pitch 0, long lens (vertical fov est. 30 deg: 3.0 m wide, 360 px/m at the actors) so a 0.1 m depth error changes apparent height by ~1 %. Est. read: floor y 1644, man's head y 996 standing and y 834 at the apex, the clearance band (0.40–0.50 m) at y 1464–1500 (below the caption band 860–1248), chart top y 708, the wall and ceiling line above it. Both in profile facing screen right (flea profile is its readable view). The band is drawn as a band, not a hairline, so the picture never claims "เท่ากันเป๊ะ" (Preaw's evidence note).
- **S4 — The jump arc.** Clearance h est. 0.45 m, airtime 2·sqrt(2h/g) ≈ 0.61 s ≈ **18 frames**: take-off f0, apex f0+9, landing f0+18, parabolic y(t) (no ease), feet contact frames exact on f0 and f0+18. Both crouch 8 frames before f0 (man: P35 body with a neutral face; flea: hind legs fold), so the giant flea jumps with **plain muscle, the same way as the man** (beat 4). Story hold at the apex: a freeze of est. 20 frames with the tag "สูงพอ ๆ กัน" on the wall above the actors (est. y 520–680, clear of the top-left channel mark and the caption band 860–1248) with a leader line to the clearance band, then the landing resumes. The real flea's jump in `M` is the opposite picture: take-off in **1 frame** (a smear streak and empty fur on the next frame, est. real airtime 0.38 s for 0.18 m, the flea leaves a macro frame in 1–2 frames); beat 3's spring cutaway is a labelled slow motion ("ภาพช้า"), lock → squeeze → release over est. 24 frames.
- **S5 — Apparent size across the scale change.** Hook ruler: from the flea on the fur, the camera cranes straight up along its jump while a screen-layer counter beside the path ticks "×10, ×50, ×100" (the tall frame carries the climb; the flea is too small to stay readable in a still frame 100 body lengths high: est. 17 px in 1,700 px). Grow shot: in CAM-CHART with the camera **fixed** (the man and chart are the reference, nothing else changes), the flea grows about its feet ground point (feet stay on the floor, no sink or float) on an exponential clock k(t) = 900^(t/T), T est. 45 frames, so each tenfold step takes the same time (~15 frames); it starts as a dot with a magnifier inset showing the macro flea, and the man flinches (P12, `shock`) as it passes his knee. Myth shot (CAM-MYTH, space `S`): a low-angle perspective from behind the man (kit back view, foreground est. y 1450–1920), tower converging upward, the naive arc a dotted screen-layer line from beside him over the roof; the naive apex is 100 body lengths = **est. 180 m** (≈ 55 storeys at 3.3 m), so Preaw's 30-storey tower (≈ 100 m) is cleared with room; the flea at the apex is a few px, so it carries a ring marker. The myth shot is the only steep perspective in the episode; the truth shot (S3) is flat and measured, and that contrast is the visual argument.
- **S6 — Four views squared to the lens.** The flea's profile (left/right) is wide and readable; its front and back are a thin tall sliver (est. width 0.3 × length) and are kept for one comic beat (it turns to look at the man). The man is profile in S3, three-quarter front in the reaction beats, back view in CAM-MYTH.

**Risks for a Short (Film):**
1. **Anti-climax.** The payoff is "the giant flea jumps like you": less spectacular than the myth. Protection: spend the spectacle early (the tower arc in beat 1), then deflate as comedy: matched crouches, a freeze at the apex, the tag, a pizzicato shrug. The seesaw beat (beat 2) stays at or under est. 4 s.
2. **Scale continuity.** Three spaces (fur, room, street) in 40–60 s; a shot without a reference size loses the viewer. Requirement in the brief: one reference per shot (S1), and the grow shot keeps the camera fixed.
3. **Frame width.** A long flea beside a man in 9:16 is the hardest staging; option A of S2 is what keeps the man at ~650 px. If the user prefers option B (heads level), the man drops to ~490 px.
4. **Ick factor and pet health.** A realistic flea or a sore dog turns pet owners away in the first second; the flea look frame (W3) is the gate for this and needs the user's approval before shots.
5. **Cost.** One new creature in four views and four to five new motions (scratch, standing jump, two flea jumps, sigh); an insect body plan has existed only episode-local since EP05, so KIT-P01 filing at the end should cover the flea and ideally harden the insect card.
