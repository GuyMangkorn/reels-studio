## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Subject:** engineering / materials, with a named real case (aviation history).
- **Genre:** engineering mystery with a real-case reveal.
- **Medium (wild card):** a **stress-light look the kit and project defaults cannot make**: clear plastic window shapes seen through polarised light, so the hidden pull in the material shows as rainbow bands that crowd into sharp corners and spread smoothly around round ones. Mixed with the painted 3D camera for the character in a plane seat. Needs a new capability (Film/Boss decide).
- **Our cast:** a Thai boy on his first flight (four-head cast) with his aunt; one bus beat at the start. He meets each problem on screen (ER02).
- **Logline:** Bus windows are square and last for years, so why are plane windows always rounded? Because a plane is blown up like a balloon every flight, and sharp corners are where the pull piles up. Early jets with sharp-cornered openings cracked in the sky.
- **Counter-intuitive claim (ER01):** a sharp corner, which looks strongest, is the weakest point in a skin that is stretched and relaxed every flight; rounded corners spread the pull out. Sharp-cornered openings on an early jet, the Comet, led to cracks and two losses in 1954 (`unchecked`: Comet inquiry, metal fatigue starting at the corner of a cut-out; exact window detail to be checked).
- **Rule that flips (ER03):** "แรงชอบไปกองที่มุมแหลม". First it is the danger (the corner that tore the plane); the flip: the fix was not thicker metal but a corner rounded a little.
- **Twist clue planted:** CL01 the square bus window that never breaks (beat 2), CL02 the paper clip he bends back and forth in his nervous hands until it snaps at the bend (beat 4). Both return in the answer.

## Beats (five-step structure; times estimated)

1. **HOOK (0–6 s):** he presses his face to the plane window and traces its rounded corner with a finger. "ทำไมหน้าต่างเครื่องบิน ไม่มีมุมเหลี่ยมเลยสักบาน… เพราะครั้งหนึ่ง มุมเหลี่ยมเล็ก ๆ เคยทำเครื่องบินแตกกลางฟ้า"
2. **QUESTION + DELAY (6–16 s):** Question: "มุมเหลี่ยมมันผิดตรงไหน?" Delay: flashback to the airport bus that morning: square windows, rattling over bumps for years, never cracking (CL01); his aunt's phone shows their house windows, square too. "หน้าต่างรถบัสก็เหลี่ยม หน้าต่างบ้านก็เหลี่ยม… ใช้กันมาเป็นสิบปี ไม่เห็นแตก"
3. **REHOOK (16–24 s):** the plane climbs; a cutaway shows the cabin swelling gently like a balloon, then shrinking on landing. "แต่ เครื่องบินไม่เหมือนรถบัส… ทุกครั้งที่บินขึ้น ตัวเครื่องถูกอัดลมจนพองเหมือนลูกโป่ง แล้วแฟบลงทุกครั้งที่ลงจอด" (pays the "not like a bus" half at ~40 %.)
4. **QUESTION + DELAY #2 (24–34 s):** Question: "พองแล้วแฟบ… แล้วยังไง?" Delay: nervous, he bends a paper clip from his aunt's boarding-pass folder back and forth; it snaps exactly at the bend (CL02); he hides it guiltily. "ลวดเส้นเดียว ดัดไปดัดมาไม่กี่สิบที… ก็หักตรงที่งอ ทั้งที่ไม่ได้ออกแรงเยอะเลย" (the viewer can try).
5. **ANSWER (34–50 s):** stress-light view: a square window shape glows with rainbow bands crowding into its corners; a round one shows smooth even bands; then a short archive-style silhouette of an early jet (no people, no wreck). "เพราะทุกครั้งที่ตัวเครื่องพอง แรงดึงจะไปกองที่มุมแหลม มุมเลยถูกงอซ้ำ ๆ เหมือนลวดที่คุณดัด จนร้าว… ส่วนมุมโค้ง แรงกระจายไปทั่วขอบ" (~9 s). Flip: back in the cabin he looks at the rounded window again, calmer. "สิ่งที่ทำให้เครื่องบินปลอดภัย… ไม่ใช่เหล็กที่หนาขึ้น แต่คือมุมที่โค้งขึ้นนิดเดียว" ~2 s hold on the window corner with clouds outside.

## Checks for Boat

- Comet 1954 losses: what the inquiry found (fatigue cracks starting at the corner of a cut-out in the cabin roof, an aerial window per common accounts); were the passenger windows themselves the origin? The hook says "มุมเหลี่ยมเล็ก ๆ" (an opening's sharp corner), not "หน้าต่างผู้โดยสาร"; confirm that wording is true, or narrow it.
- Did the Comet failures directly lead to rounded windows industry-wide, or is that a popular simplification? Keep the hook's claim only as strong as the record allows.
- Pressurisation: the cabin is pressurised in flight and the skin expands slightly each cycle: true simplification for "พองเหมือนลูกโป่ง".
- Stress concentration at sharp corners and fatigue from repeated cycles: true one-cause wording; the paper-clip analogy (repeated bending) is fair for fatigue?
- "ไม่ใช่เหล็กที่หนาขึ้น": the Comet's skin is aluminium, and the fix included more than rounder corners; reword the flip if "only the corner" overstates it (e.g. "ไม่ใช่โลหะที่หนาขึ้น").
