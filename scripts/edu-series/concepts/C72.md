## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Genre:** detective-style hide-and-seek with a flip ending (how a dog reads a scent trail).
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera (project default): a Thai park lawn in late afternoon; the signature picture is the scent trail painted as glowing footprints that fade from dim (old) to bright (new), plus one inside-view of air leaving the nose's side slits.
- **Our cast:** a Thai girl about ten (four-head cast) playing hide-and-seek with her dog, sure she can trick it. She meets each problem on screen (ER02).
- **Logline:** She hides behind a tree and the dog sniffs only a few of her footprints, then turns the right way at once. Each step smells a little fainter than the one after it, and a nose that never stops sniffing reads that difference. The fading she hoped would hide her is the arrow pointing at her.
- **Counter-intuitive claim (ER01):** from just about five footprints, a dog can tell which way you walked, because the older steps smell slightly fainter than the newer ones (`unchecked`: Hepper & Wells 2005, Chemical Senses).
- **Rule that flips (ER03):** "กลิ่นยิ่งเก่า ยิ่งจาง". At first it is her hope (wait long enough and the trail goes cold). The flip: the fading itself is what tells the dog the direction; the trail can't go cold in a way that hides which way it points.
- **Hook (Thai):** "หมาดมรอยเท้าคุณ แค่ห้าก้าว… ก็รู้แล้วว่าคุณเดินไปทางไหน"
- **Objection by ~7 s:** viewer: "รอยเท้ามันก็กลิ่นเดียวกันทุกก้าวนี่?" → narrator: "ไม่เหมือนหรอก… ก้าวแรกเหยียบก่อน กลิ่นก็จางกว่า"
- **Twist clue planted:** CL01 she says "รอนาน ๆ กลิ่นก็หายเอง" in beat 1 and waits behind the tree, counting (her plan is the burden). CL02 the glowing footprints already fade dim-to-bright in beat 1, unexplained (background). Both return in the flip.
- **Beats (each opens the next with "แต่…"; times estimated):**
  1. **Hook (0–7 s):** she sets the dog to "wait", runs across the lawn, hides behind a tree whispering her plan (CL01). The dog sniffs four or five prints (CL02 glow) and turns her way without hesitating.
  2. **Problem 1 (~7–17 s):** "แต่ ห่างกันแค่ไม่กี่วินาที กลิ่นจะต่างกันได้ไง?" The prints glow dim to bright toward the tree. Answer (one cause): every step starts fading the moment it is made, so the dog walks from the fainter step to the stronger one. Rule stated: "กลิ่นยิ่งเก่า ยิ่งจาง". Hook payoff at ~30 %: "หมาไม่ได้ดมว่าคุณอยู่ไหน… มันดมว่าคุณไปทางไหน"
  3. **Problem 2 (~17–28 s):** "แต่ ต่างกันนิดเดียวแบบนี้ ดมแป๊บเดียวก็ไม่ทันหรอก?" She tries to sniff a print herself, breathes out and loses it. Inside-view of the dog's nose: air goes out through the side slits, pushed sideways and back, so it never blows the trail away and the sniffs come one after another (`unchecked`: Craven et al. 2010, Journal of the Royal Society Interface). Answer: it breathes out to the side, so it can keep smelling without stopping.
  4. **Problem 3 (~28–38 s):** "แต่ ถ้ารอนาน ๆ ล่ะ กลิ่นก็หายหมดสิ" She waits, counting, sure the trail is cold. Answer: the steps keep fading, but they all fade together, so the newest is still the strongest and the order stays the same (`unchecked`: Boat checks this is a true simplification and whether trail age limits it; if not solid, this beat is cut, ER07).
  5. **Payoff flip (~38–48 s):** the dog rounds the tree and licks her face; she laughs. "คุณหวังว่ากลิ่นจะจางจนหมาหาไม่เจอ… แต่กลิ่นที่จางนั่นแหละ คือลูกศร ที่ชี้ไปหาคุณ" ~2 s hold on the faded trail glowing toward her.
- **Ending:** closed on the flip, ~2 s hold; `_A_fade`. No tip.
- **Voice / tone:** Kore, warm smiling storyteller, amused at her scheme ("แผนดีนะ… แต่", "หรอก"). She whispers, giggles, gasps "เจอได้ไง!"; the dog with snuffles and one happy bark.
- **Why it holds:** hide-and-seek with a dog is universal and warm; the hook's "five steps" is concrete and sounds impossible; the glowing-footprint gradient is a painted picture that explains itself; the flip turns her own plan (wait for the smell to fade) into the clue.
- **Research load:** named studies: direction of a human trail from footsteps (Hepper & Wells 2005), dog nose airflow and side slits (Craven, Paterson, Settles 2010); trail ageing (general tracking literature). Medium; the "five steps" number and problem 3 are the risks.
- **Length risk (ER09/ER02):** three problems in ~50 s; comfortable. If problem 3 is cut, the flip lands on problem 1's rule and the Short runs ~40 s.
- **Sound intent (English notes; narration unchanged, sound never replaces a clue):**
  - Beat 1: park ambience (kids far off, birds, light wind in grass); sneaky tiptoe pizzicato bed; quick rhythmic sniff-sniff in time with a soft rising glow tone per footprint, each a step higher in pitch (dim to bright is also on screen).
  - Beat 2: the rising glow tones repeat on the trail; a warm "click" chord on the payoff line.
  - Beat 3: her failed sniff is a comic snort; the inside-view gets an airy whoosh panned out to the sides; rapid steady sniff rhythm.
  - Beat 4: a ticking clock under her counting; the bed thins to a held note, a soft riser as the dog closes in.
  - Flip: the riser resolves into a happy bark and her laugh, bed swells to warm guitar, then hold on the glowing trail; closing sound is Film's choice (UT15).

## Checks for Boat

- Problem 3: does the newest step stay the strongest as a trail ages; any limit (very old trails, rain) that makes the line false. Recommend the cut if not solid.
