# C18 — ถอดหมวกกลางอวกาศ (wild card)

- Status: **made** as `EDU-EP08-SHORT` (published).

## Pitch (Preaw)


- **Sub-genre:** what-if survival / myth-buster (space vacuum), with one named real case.
- **Staging:** a spacewalk in orbit, Earth below; one astronaut's helmet comes off (cartoon, no gore); a 15-second painted timer; a crewmate pulls them into the airlock.
- **Logline:** Films say a body explodes or freezes instantly in space. It does neither: you would stay conscious for only about 10 to 15 seconds, the moisture on your tongue would start to boil, and the one thing that would hurt you most is holding your breath. A 1966 NASA test accident shows what it feels like.
- **Hook (Thai):** "ถ้าคุณถอดหมวกกลางอวกาศ… ตัวคุณจะไม่ระเบิดอย่างในหนัง แต่คุณมีเวลาแค่ 15 วินาที"
- **Hook claim status:** `unchecked`, `settled` for "no explosion"; the 10–15 s consciousness window is `settled` in NASA references but Boat confirms the source and wording.
- **Key reveal image:** the astronaut floating against the blue Earth, helmet drifting away, a fine glittering mist from their breath and tiny bubbles on the tongue, timer at 0:11.
- **Beats:**
  1. Spacewalk; the helmet clasp slips; the helmet drifts off. Timer starts.
  2. Myth-buster: no explosion (skin and body hold together; the body swells somewhat), no instant freezing (vacuum carries heat away slowly). `unchecked`, `settled`
  3. What you would feel: the moisture on your tongue starts to boil. In 1966 a NASA suit test in a vacuum chamber went wrong; the technician later said his last memory was saliva bubbling on his tongue. He was repressurised and recovered. `unchecked`, named real case (documented; Boat checks the name, Jim LeBlanc, and the details)
  4. The rule: breathe out, never hold your breath, or the air in your lungs damages them. Consciousness lasts about 10–15 seconds. `unchecked`, `settled`
  5. Rescue: the crewmate hauls them into the airlock; studies suggest a short exposure can be survivable. Last line: "ในอวกาศ… สิ่งที่อันตรายที่สุดไม่ใช่การระเบิด แต่คือการกลั้นหายใจ" `unchecked` (survival window `model`, from older animal studies; must be hedged)
- **Ending:** `A_fade` (relief beat in the airlock).
- **Voice / tone:** tense but clear narrator; the astronaut gasps once and mouths words silently (no air, no sound); crewmate one muffled line over the radio.
- **Why it holds:** busts a myth every film fan carries; a real case adds "this actually happened"; the countdown holds attention; the breath rule is the sort of strange fact people repeat.
- **Research load:** general knowledge plus a named real case (NASA 1966 vacuum-chamber incident, documented; physiology of vacuum exposure from NASA references and older animal studies).
- **Kit gap (why it is the wild card):** no spacesuit or helmet in `WARDROBE` (and a helmet changes the head silhouette of the 4-head cast), no zero-g float motion in `MOTIONS`, no orbit / Earth-from-space set or airlock, and no vacuum FX (breath mist freezing to glitter, tongue bubbles). Film and Boss would scope these in W0.2. Taste risk: keep it cartoon-safe; no swelling or injury detail on screen.
- **Sound intent:** the big idea is sound itself: radio chatter and breathing inside the helmet, then near-total silence the moment the helmet comes off (no sound in vacuum), with only a felt heartbeat thud and the timer; the airlock hiss and radio voice return as the stinger of rescue.
