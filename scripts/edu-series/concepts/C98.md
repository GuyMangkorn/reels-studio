## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Genre and setup** — Cargo problem in an invented nineteenth-century New England ice shipment.
- **Proposed medium** — Painted 3D ship hold with the viewer’s hands, stacked ice and a recurring drip close-up.
- **Logline** — สมมุติว่าคุณดูแลน้ำแข็งทั้งเรือที่ต้องส่งถึงเมืองร้อน พอเรือออกไปได้ไม่นาน คุณเห็นน้ำหยดจากก้อนที่ใกล้ฝาเรือที่สุด ทางเลือกหนึ่งคือเอาขี้เลื่อยแห้งที่เหลืออยู่ไปอุดช่องตรงนั้น
- **Opening line** — “ถ้าของที่คุณต้องส่งคือน้ำแข็งเต็มเรือ แล้วของที่ช่วยให้มันถึงฝั่งคือขี้เลื่อยล่ะ?”
- **Counter-intuitive premise** — A loose woodworking by-product can help ice survive a long voyage by slowing heat transfer.

## Beats

1. **B01** — Below deck you catch the first drip and look at the chalk mark on the loaded blocks. Someone at the far port is paying for ice that arrives solid.
2. **B02** — You can use your last sack of dry sawdust to floor the wet patch or pack it into a newly exposed gap around the blocks. The opening puts both options in your hands.
3. **B03** — You choose the gap. Introduce dry sawdust as a period insulating material through the visible packed layer. You keep checking the ice as the ship moves into hotter weather and the chalk mark takes on a drip.
4. **B04** — At unloading you scoop away the sawdust and lift a block still solid enough for the buyer to accept. As you pull the block up, the chalk mark shows how much you lost and how much you saved.

- **Ending** — Closed material reveal. Hold on the intact block coming out from under the shavings.
- **Voice and tone** — Kore with suspense that releases into surprised satisfaction.
- **Retention and share hypothesis** — The cold cargo has an obvious timer. The audience sees the size of the problem and the result in the same prop.
- **Research load for Boat** — General history. Check New England ice shipments, actual packing materials and route plausibility. The ship, weather, choice, time compression and amount surviving are dramatized.
