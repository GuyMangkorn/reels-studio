## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Genre:** everyday-physics surprise in slow motion with a flip ending.
- **Medium:** painted 3D camera (project default) for a Thai townhouse kitchen; the signature picture is a painted slow-motion inside-view: the tongue, the water column and the jaw snapping, frozen and scrubbed like a sports replay.
- **Our cast:** a Thai teenage boy (four-head cast) who has just mopped the kitchen floor, and his dog. A neighbour's cat walks in for one comparison beat. He meets each problem on screen (ER02).
- **Logline:** He has just mopped the floor and the dog drinks, splashing everywhere. Slow motion shows it isn't scooping like a spoon: it slaps the water and yanks a column up faster than it falls, then bites it. The splash he hates is the method.
- **Counter-intuitive claim (ER01):** a dog does not scoop water with its tongue like a spoon; it curls the tongue backwards, slaps the water and pulls up a column that it bites off in mid-air (`unchecked`: Gart et al. 2015, PNAS; Crompton & Musinsky 2011).
- **Rule that flips (ER03):** "ยิ่งตีแรง ยิ่งได้น้ำเยอะ". At first it is the cost (harder slap, more mess on his clean floor). The flip: the mess is proof the trick works; a gentle dog would go thirsty.
- **Hook (Thai):** "หมาไม่ได้ใช้ลิ้นตักน้ำเหมือนช้อนนะ… มันตบน้ำ ให้น้ำกระโดดขึ้นมาเอง"
- **Objection by ~7 s:** viewer: "ก็เห็นลิ้นมันงอเป็นช้อนนี่" → narrator: "งอจริง… แต่งอไปข้างหลัง และน้ำในช้อนนั้น แทบไม่ได้เข้าปากเลย" (`unchecked`: how much water the curl itself carries).
- **Twist clue planted:** CL01 the splash rings around the bowl, seen in beat 1 as the burden (his annoyance); CL02 the cat drinking neatly in beat 4. Both return in the flip.
- **Beats (each opens the next with "แต่…"; times estimated):**
  1. **Hook (0–7 s):** he finishes mopping, proud; the dog drinks, water flies; he groans "เพิ่งถูเสร็จ!". The frame freezes on a drop in mid-air.
  2. **Problem 1 (~7–16 s):** "แต่ ทำไมหมาไม่ดูดน้ำเหมือนเรา?" He tries to sip from a glass with his mouth stretched wide open and can't. Answer (one cause): a dog's cheeks open far back, so it can't close its mouth tight enough to suck (`unchecked`; everyday comparison: like drinking through a straw with a hole in it).
  3. **Problem 2 (~16–26 s):** "แต่ แล้วน้ำขึ้นมาได้ยังไง?" Slow-motion inside-view: the tongue curls backwards, hits the water, then shoots up; a column of water follows it. Answer: the tongue pulls up so fast that water clings and rises behind it, like the splash when you yank your hand out of a bucket; the jaw snaps shut on the column at the top. Hook payoff at ~30 %: "ไม่ได้ตักนะ… ตบแล้วงับ"
  4. **Problem 3 (~26–38 s):** "แต่ แมวก็ใช้ลิ้นกินน้ำ… ทำไมไม่เลอะ?" The cat touches the surface with just the tip of its tongue and lifts gently. Answer (one cause): same trick, but the dog drives its tongue in harder and faster to lift a bigger column for its bigger mouth (`unchecked`: Reis et al. 2010 for cats; Gart et al. 2015 for the dog comparison).
  5. **Payoff flip (~38–48 s):** he looks at the splash rings (CL01), then at the neat cat (CL02), then at his dog lapping hard; he puts the mop down and smiles. "หมาไม่ได้กินเลอะเพราะซุ่มซ่ามหรอก… มันกินเลอะ เพราะมันกินเก่ง" ~2 s hold on the dog's wet happy face.
- **Ending:** closed on the flip, ~2 s hold; `_A_fade`. No tip (no "ซื้อชามกันเลอะ").
- **Voice / tone:** Kore, warm smiling storyteller, amused at his mopping ("เพิ่งถูเสร็จเลยนะ…", "หรอก"). He reacts with "โห!", a groan, a slurp attempt and a laugh; the dog with happy lapping.
- **Why it holds:** everyone has watched a dog drink and never seen this; the slow-motion picture is satisfying on its own and only works painted/frozen; the cat beat is a built-in comment war (cats vs dogs); the flip forgives a daily annoyance.
- **Research load:** named studies: dog lapping (Gart, Socha, Vlachos, Jung 2015, PNAS; Crompton & Musinsky 2011, Biology Letters), cat lapping (Reis, Jung, Aristoff, Stocker 2010, Science), dog cheek anatomy (general). Light to medium.
- **Length risk (ER09/ER02):** three problems in ~50 s; comfortable. Problem 2 must stay one cause (the fast pull), the jaw snap is shown, not explained.
- **Sound intent (English notes; narration unchanged, sound never replaces a clue):**
  - Beat 1: mop squeak, bucket clunk; bouncy marimba bed; loud comic lapping and splatter; record-scratch freeze on the mid-air drop.
  - Beat 2: a failed slurp and dribble from the boy; small comic bass "bwomp".
  - Beat 3: everything slows (pitched-down room tone, a whoosh with long tail); a crisp water-slap transient, a rising wet "bloop" as the column climbs, a dry jaw "clack" at the top; the bed returns at normal speed on the payoff line.
  - Beat 4: delicate tiny tongue taps for the cat, near silence, a dainty celesta note.
  - Flip: bed softens to warm guitar, one last happy lap and splash, then hold; closing sound is Film's choice (UT15).

## Checks for Boat

- Dog lapping: tongue curls backward (ventral side), column formation, jaw closure timing; how much water the curled tongue itself carries vs the column.
- Dog cheeks cannot seal for suction: true one-line wording.
- Cat vs dog: cats touch the surface lightly; dogs plunge harder/faster; is "to lift a bigger column for its bigger mouth" a true one-cause line.
- Whether "faster than it falls" / "water clings and rises behind it" is a true simplification (inertia vs gravity).
