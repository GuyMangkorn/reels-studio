## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Genre and setup** — Sports-broadcast comedy that loses its mind. Caribbean Cup qualifier, Barbados vs Grenada, January 1994. A strange rule: a golden goal in extra time counts as two. Barbados need to win by two.
- **Proposed medium** — Painted 3D camera on a sunny 1990s stadium, framed like a TV broadcast with a period scoreboard graphic that keeps changing in ways that make no sense.
- **Logline** — ทีมหนึ่งต้องชนะสองลูก นาทีสุดท้ายเลยยิงประตูตัวเองให้เสมอ เพื่อไปต่อเวลาที่ลูกเดียวนับสอง อีกทีมเลยต้องวิ่งไล่ยิงประตูทั้งสองฝั่ง ส่วนคนดูไม่รู้แล้วว่าจะเชียร์ทางไหน
- **Opening line** — “มีทีมฟุตบอลที่ตั้งใจยิงเข้าประตูตัวเอง… เพื่อจะชนะ”
- **Counter-intuitive premise** — An own goal was the smartest move on the pitch.

## Beats

1. **B01** — The rule, in one breath: “ต่อเวลาแล้วใครยิงก่อน ลูกนั้นนับเป็นสองลูก”. Deadpan: “ตอนเขียนกฎ ไม่มีใครคิดว่าจะมีคนเอาไปใช้แบบนี้”.
2. **B02** — Barbados lead 2–0; then Grenada score late: 2–1 (lead). Barbados now go out unless something strange happens. Grenada's bench celebrates.
3. **B03** — The turn: a few minutes from time, Barbados pass back and score in their own net on purpose: 2–2 (lead). Grenada players freeze. Character line (Grenada crowd of players): “เดี๋ยว… เขายิงเข้าบ้านตัวเองทำไม”.
4. **B04** — But worse: now Grenada want a goal at either end, because an own goal would also send them through (lead). Barbados defend both goals at once. Visual gag: one team attacking left and right, the other defending both nets, the referee spinning.
5. **B05** — Extra time: Barbados score the golden goal, which counts double (lead). Rule of three: score, score yourself, score double.

- **Ending** — The coach's documented complaint, reported as a fact line: the rule-maker belongs in a madhouse (lead: Grenada coach's quote). Fallback irony (lead): after all of that, Barbados went out in the next round. “ยิงประตูตัวเองจนชนะ… แล้วก็ไปตกรอบต่อไปอยู่ดี”.
- **Voice and tone** — Kore as a friend narrating a match she cannot believe, switching sides every beat; the players as overlapping crowd shouts, never named.
- **Why it holds** — HK01 states something every football fan thinks is impossible; the both-ends chase is a picture worth replaying. Share trigger: “ส่งให้เพื่อนที่ดูบอล”.
- **Research load for Boat** — Named real case (Barbados vs Grenada, Shell Caribbean Cup qualifier, 27 Jan 1994). Leads: the golden-goal-counts-double rule and why it existed; the score order and minutes; the own goal and the both-ends defence; the golden goal in extra time; the Grenada coach's quote, its wording and source; Barbados's result in the next round. No player names on screen.
- **Sound intent (proposed)** — Steel-drum stadium groove and crowd bed; a record-scratch and sudden crowd silence at the own goal in B03; the music swings left and right in the stereo field during B04; a big brass hit on the golden goal, then the groove deflates for the ending.
