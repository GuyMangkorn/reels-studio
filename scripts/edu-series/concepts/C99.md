## Pitch (Preaw)

- **Genre and setup** — Deadline chase in an invented nineteenth-century Bristol delivery.
- **Proposed medium** — Painted 2D street-level perspective with a double minute-hand clock and the delivery envelope always in the viewer’s possession.
- **Logline** — สมมุติว่าคุณวิ่งส่งจดหมายที่ต้องขึ้นรถไฟเที่ยวสุดท้ายในบริสตอล คุณเช็กนาฬิกาแล้วคิดว่ายังทัน แต่เข็มยาวอีกอันบนหน้าปัดบอกอีกเวลา
- **Opening line** — “ถ้าคุณวิ่งส่งจดหมายไปขึ้นรถไฟ แล้วนาฬิกาเรือนเดียวบอกว่าคุณทั้งทันและสายล่ะ?”
- **Counter-intuitive premise** — The same public clock can give two valid local conventions, only one of which governs your railway deadline.

## Beats

1. **B01** — The viewer runs to catch the last departure with the sealed letter and checks the public clock.
2. **B02** — You see two minute hands. One leaves time for the usual route. The other says you need to go straight to the station and give up your stop for a paid second delivery.
3. **B03** — You ask which hand the train uses. The reply gives the one fact about standard railway time and the clock. You take the direct street, glancing back once at the hand you had been using.
4. **B04** — At the platform you put the letter in the waiting recipient’s hand as the train is ready to leave. Your paid stop is behind you, but the urgent job goes out on time.

- **Ending** — Closed race with a trade-off. Hold on the empty hand as the train leaves with the letter.
- **Voice and tone** — Kore brisk and close, with urgency rather than a historical lecture.
- **Retention and share hypothesis** — A clock that seems broken is an immediate puzzle, and the choice tests whether the viewer trusts the familiar time. The two hands should be readable in one frame.
