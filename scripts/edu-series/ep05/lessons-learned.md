## 1. Hook: why the first 7 seconds hold

| Time | Line / picture | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0–1.3 s | L01 "ถ้าคุณตัวเล็กเท่ามด…" over the girl on her balcony | The title is the first line: the promise the viewer clicked on is confirmed at once. "คุณ" puts the viewer in the story. A "what if" needs no prior knowledge. |
| 1.3–4.6 s | L02 "ตกจากตึกสิบชั้น… คุณจะแทบไม่เป็นอะไรเลย" over a top-down vertigo POV down the building face (slow tilt, pitch −50→−53) | A **concrete, counter-intuitive claim with life-or-death stakes**: everyone "knows" ten storeys kills. It opens a "prove it" gap. The picture shows the height, so the stakes are felt, not only heard. |
| 4.6–7.2 s | L03 "สมมติว่าหดทั้งตัว… รวมทั้งน้ำหนักด้วย", touch and pop, cut into the grass forest | **Pre-empts the smart objection** ("but what about weight?") before the viewer can raise it, which buys trust. The shrink is a visual event at ~6 s, and the first macro frame (blades as trees, dew as boulders) is the wonder payoff of the premise. |

Comparison with the series: EP04 opens with a question ("คุณเปลี่ยนแถวกี่ครั้ง…"), EP06 with a warning ("จ้องตาแมวด้วยความรัก…"), EP03 with a gentle surprise ("…อาจดูคล้ายหญ้า"). EP05 is the only hook that promises a **spectacle the viewer will see** (the fall) and contradicts a belief held with certainty. Hedge "แทบ" kept it fact-safe without softening the claim.

## 2. Picture and camera

- **Scale is the joke and the camera holds to it.** Macro shots sit at TINY's eye height (eye 0.6–1.0 sm), so blades read as trees, the drop as a glass boulder, the leaf on the ant as a sail. Only two human-scale views exist after the shrink: the vertigo POV (hook) and the speck on the facade (shows the height she fell, at the payoff).
- **Every shot moves a little; two shots move a lot.** Camera rows: nearly every shot is a small yaw arc (6–10°) plus a slow push (r −0.1 to −0.4), eased. The frame is never dead, but the motion never competes with the action. Two signature moves land on the two story events: the tilt down the drop (L02) and the crane up the blades (V01, the scale change). Trucks follow action (drag, tumble), never wander.
- **One subject per frame, readable at thumbnail size.** The look is simple and flat (closer to clean vector than to lush watercolour), yet the video held. Clarity of the one thing to look at mattered more than rendering richness. Contact close-ups (hand in the drop, drop stretching) are the explanation of surface tension; the picture teaches before the narrator does.
- **Light marks the chapters.** Warm balcony → bright air → backlit gold drop → **cool blue shade** (cold, B04) → warm sun returns on the lift. Viewers feel a new chapter without a word.
- **Weak spots seen in the frames:** the first 1.3 s is a near-static medium shot (the vertigo shot is the stronger opening frame); the drift (S01-B02-SH02, 5.3 s) sits on a plain cream background with little to look at, during the most abstract line (L05). Watch whether the retention curve dips there.

## 3. Why viewers stay and do not swipe

1. **A game with levels, each opened by "แต่…".** Fall (0–20 s) → water trap (20–34 s) → cold (34–42 s) → hunger (42–45 s) → the ant and the reversal (45–66 s). A new problem every ~10 s, a new set or light every beat, a cut every ~3.5 s (19 shots / 66 s). The hook is paid at ~18 s ("ราว ๆ กระโดดลงจากเก้าอี้", a body-felt comparison), and the next loop opens in the same breath ("แต่ข้างล่าง… มีหยดน้ำค้างรออยู่"): no gap where the viewer feels "done".
2. **Show, then tell.** The character hits each problem before the narrator explains it (hand stuck → "ทำไมน้ำมันเหนียวแบบนี้!" → surface tension). The viewer forms the question a second before it is answered.
3. **One rule explains everything, then flips.** The refrain "ตัวยิ่งเล็ก…" explains the soft fall, the cold and the strength. B02–B04 teach "small = harder"; the ant reveal turns the same rule into a strength. The crumb planted as a burden (CL01) and the ant glimpsed in the background (CL02) pay off: a twist that rewards having watched.
4. **A character with feelings, not a lecture.** Squeak, "huh?" and giggle, shiver, defeated sigh, triumphant squeak: cheap vocal sounds give an emotional arc (wonder → "ugh" → pity → triumph). The narrator is a friend (Kore, playful "กวน ๆ"; user R1.4 rewrites to spoken Thai: "นะ", "หรอก", "เหลือ ๆ").
5. **Sound shapes attention.** 0.8 s of silence before the ant (music cut on the frame), no hard thud on landing, a sting on the lift, a 2 s hold with the end chord. The last 20 s is the best part of the video (the reversal), so the ending pulls people through instead of releasing them early.

## 4. "This topic is everywhere": why it still worked

- **A crowded topic is a demand signal, not a reason to skip.** "What if you were tiny" is a proven curiosity; the platform already knows who wants it, which helps reach.
- **We competed on execution, not topic.** Most versions are lists or lectures ("10 things if you were ant-sized"), stock footage or 3D, often English. Ours is a Thai story with one protagonist, one rule, a twist and a friend's voice.
- **Self-insertion lowers the cost to start watching.** "คุณ" + an everyday place (a condo balcony at 06:30 with coffee) makes the fantasy feel like it could be the viewer's own morning.
- **The counter-intuitive claim survives familiarity.** Even viewers who have seen the topic rarely know "ten storeys, almost unhurt"; the claim works on them too.
