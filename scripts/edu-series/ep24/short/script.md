## Story core and ending

- **Logline:** ถ้าคุณได้ประมูลแบงก์ร้อยใบเดียว เริ่มที่ห้าบาท โดยคนที่เสนอรองลงมาต้องจ่ายด้วย คุณจะไต่ราคาไปจนเกินร้อยทีละก้าว และทุกก้าวคุ้มจริง ๆ
- **Central question:** why would anyone pay more than 100 baht for a 100-baht note?
- **Intention / obstacle:** you want a cheap 100-baht note; the second-place-pays rule turns every stop into a bigger loss than the next bid.
- **Feeling:** greed and fun (cheap bids) → alarm (the second rule) → trapped by your own good sums → the cold reveal of who was playing whom.
- **Learning takeaway:** in a "second place pays too" auction, each bid only compares this step's loss, so every step is the better choice while the whole path loses money; the person selling the note is the one who gains. This game was published in 1971 and, when played for real, bids often go past the note's value.
- **Ending chosen first:** closed reversal. You win the note at 110, the rival pays 105 for nothing, and the host, who never bid, collects 215. The refrain "ทุกก้าวคุ้ม" flips from your reason into his profit; ~2 s hold.

## Truth inside the story

- What happens: bids 5 → 10 → 20 … → you 90, rival 95 → you 100 → rival 105 → you 110 → rival quits at 105. You pay 110 for a 100 note (lose 10); the rival pays 105 and gets nothing; the host takes 215 for one 100 note. Increments are 5 baht from 90 on.
- Story-world time: one auction, a few minutes. Video time is compressed: the board flips past 20 → 90 without narration.
- Real, checked content is limited to the rule (CL-F02), the arithmetic (CL-F03/F04), the 1971 origin (CL-F01) and "มัก" for real play (CL-F05). No real-play amount, no named researcher, no claim that this baht version was ever held.
