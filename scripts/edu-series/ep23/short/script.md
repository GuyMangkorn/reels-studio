## Story core and ending

At the mouth of a Thai soi, you try to direct a stranger to the market. Someone carrying a door interrupts your line of sight, so the first man slips away and a second man takes his place. You finish giving directions. Just when he looks ready to go, he asks which way to turn, so you move beside him and point straight at the corner. That small interruption gets your attention. The ending asks what else changed and lets the viewer verify it against the opening.

The first pass changes the person. The second pass changes the cap and shop sign while you sort out the turn. The two men both wear red caps in their first appearances, so the later cap change is a separate event. The real study supplies the mid-story answer: researchers swapped someone asking for directions while a door blocked the view, and eight of 15 pedestrians gave no report of the person changing in response to the first, open-ended question. What a given viewer notices on either pass is unknown. The takeaway is that the person we are talking to can change without us catching it.
