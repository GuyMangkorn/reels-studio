### C1 — หัวหอมแก้แค้น

- **Sub-genre:** everyday science (kitchen chemistry).
- **Staging:** one character, set-heavy kitchen; warm window light; macro inserts of the onion's cells as a painted "inside" view.
- **Logline:** A cook keeps crying over an onion and tries every folk trick on camera. We zoom inside the onion to see that the tears are a chemical alarm it fires only once it has been cut, and learn the one trick that actually helps.
- **Hook (Thai):** "หัวหอมไม่ได้ทำให้คุณร้องไห้… จนกว่าคุณจะทำร้ายมันก่อน"
- **Beats:**
  1. The cook (4-head adult, apron) slices an onion and bursts into exaggerated waterfall tears. A whole onion on the counter does nothing; only the cut one "attacks".
  2. Push into the cut: painted cells burst, and two "ingredients" stored in separate rooms meet and mix into a gas (a lilac watercolour mist) that drifts up toward the eyes. `unchecked`
  3. The gas reaches the eye's wet surface and the eye answers by flushing it out with tears. `unchecked`
  4. A trick montage with comic failures: candle, bread in the mouth, goggles (works, looks silly). Then the useful one: a chilled onion and a sharp knife make less gas. `unchecked`
  5. Payoff: the cook, dry-eyed, cuts a cold onion with a sharp knife. Last line hands back to the hook: "เพราะน้ำตาคุณ… คือสัญญาณกันขโมยของหัวหอม"
- **Ending:** `B_loop`.
- **Voice / tone:** brisk female narrator, playful, deadpan over the crying gags; the cook has no lines, only sobs and sniffs (Gemini vocal sounds).
- **Why it holds:** the hook reverses the obvious ("the onion is innocent until you cut it"); tears are a big physical gag the 4-head proportions sell; the takeaway is usable tonight, so people send it to whoever cooks at home.
- **Research load:** general knowledge (onion lachrymatory chemistry, which tricks work).
- **Sound intent:** light pizzicato kitchen bed; a comic "wah-wah" sting on each failed trick; a soft whoosh for the gas rising; music drops for one beat before the payoff line.

### C2 — ตกจากหน้าผาในความฝัน

- **Sub-genre:** body / sleep science.
- **Staging:** one character in a moonlit bedroom that melts into an abstract watercolour dreamscape (clouds, a cliff edge, falling through paint).
- **Logline:** A girl drifts off and suddenly "falls off a cliff", jerking awake. We follow her body falling asleep step by step to show what the jolt is, why it often comes with a falling dream, and why nobody knows the full answer yet.
- **Hook (Thai):** "เคยไหม… กำลังจะหลับ แล้วรู้สึกเหมือนตกจากที่สูงจนสะดุ้งตื่น"
- **Beats:**
  1. Bedroom, blue moonlight. The character's eyes close; the room's walls soften into watercolour clouds.
  2. Her body "switches off" in stages: muscles loosen, shown as painted lights going out along the limbs. `unchecked`
  3. Dream: she walks to a cliff edge, steps off, falls through paint. The body jerks; she bolts upright in bed. Name it: the hypnic jerk. `unchecked`
  4. Why: one leading idea is that the brain misreads the sudden muscle relaxation as falling and jerks to "catch" you; caffeine, stress and tiredness are linked to more of them. Labelled as a theory, not settled. `unchecked`
  5. Reassure: common and usually harmless. She lies back down, eyes close, clouds return.
- **Ending:** `C_seamless`. Hook/last-line pair: the last line "…แล้วคืนนี้ คุณจะลองสังเกตไหม ว่าตอนกำลังจะหลับ" runs straight into the hook "เคยไหม… กำลังจะหลับ…", and the last frame (eyes closing, walls softening) is the first frame.
- **Voice / tone:** soft, slower female narrator close to a whisper, warm and calm; one acted gasp on the jolt.
- **Why it holds:** almost everyone has felt it, so the hook is a recognition hit in under two seconds; the cliff fall is a showpiece for painted weather and light; the loop invites rewatching just before bed; honest "science doesn't fully know" is a shareable surprise.
- **Research load:** general knowledge, with a documented-uncertainty duty (causes are theories).
- **Sound intent:** low ambient pad and a ticking clock; wind rises during the fall; the jolt is a hard silence plus a single thump; the pad returns quietly for the loop seam.

### C3 — 11 วันที่หายไปจากปฏิทิน

- **Sub-genre:** history-as-story.
- **Staging:** ensemble in an 18th-century English market town (period costumes on the 4-head cast), a giant painted wall calendar as the recurring set piece.
- **Logline:** In 1752 a whole country went to bed on 2 September and woke on 14 September. Villagers act out the confusion over birthdays, rent and wages, while we explain why the calendar had drifted and why the famous "give us our eleven days" riots may be more legend than fact.
- **Hook (Thai):** "ปี 1752 คนอังกฤษเข้านอนวันที่ 2 กันยา… แล้วตื่นมาเป็นวันที่ 14"
- **Beats:**
  1. Night: a villager tears off "2" on the wall calendar, blows out the candle. Morning: the calendar says "14". Exaggerated double-take across the whole street.
  2. Why: the old calendar's year was a little too long, so over centuries the dates slid away from the seasons and Easter. Painted sun and season wheel slipping out of line. `unchecked`
  3. The fix: switch to the calendar much of Europe already used, and skip the drifted days in one jump. `unchecked`
  4. Comic ensemble: a landlord demands a full month's rent, a worker asks for full pay, someone's birthday "vanished". Then the turn: the story says people rioted for their lost days, but historians doubt the riots happened as told. `unchecked`, uncertainty kept.
  5. Takeaway: the calendar on your phone today is the result of that jump. Last line: "เวลาไม่ได้หายไปไหน… แค่ปฏิทินเคยนับผิดมาเป็นพันปี"
- **Ending:** `B_loop`.
- **Voice / tone:** energetic male narrator (Orus) telling it like a tall tale, then a quieter honest turn on the riot myth; two or three villagers with short comic lines (Edge TTS is fine).
- **Why it holds:** the hook sounds impossible yet is true; ensemble reaction gags are what the cute cast does best; the "it might be a myth" turn gives a second payoff and comment bait ("so did they riot or not?").
- **Research load:** named real event (Calendar Act; the date jump; the riot story's documented status).
- **Sound intent:** jaunty folk fiddle; a rooster at the morning reveal; a record-scratch stop on the double-take; music thins under the myth turn.

### C4 — ทำไมแถวข้าง ๆ เร็วกว่าเสมอ

- **Sub-genre:** psychology (perception and memory bias), with simple probability.
- **Staging:** ensemble at a supermarket checkout; the camera rises to a top-down view where the queues become a clean painted diagram (dots, arrows, a big "1/3").
- **Logline:** A man switches queues three times and always picks the slow one. We show that the odds were against him from the start, and that his memory keeps the slow days and forgets the fast ones.
- **Hook (Thai):** "คุณเปลี่ยนแถวกี่ครั้ง… แถวที่คุณย้ายไปก็ช้าทุกที"
- **Beats:**
  1. The man joins line 2. Line 1 moves; he switches. Now line 1 stops (a price check) and line 2 flies. Switch again, same result. Big sulking acting.
  2. Top-down diagram: with three lines, the chance that *yours* is the fastest is only one in three, so most of the time some other line wins. `unchecked`
  3. While you wait you watch the other lines, so every overtake counts against you, and nobody notices the times they were overtaking someone else. `unchecked`
  4. Memory: the furious slow days stick and the smooth days are forgotten, so "I'm always unlucky" feels true. `unchecked`
  5. Takeaway and gag: the trick some shops use is one shared line feeding all tills. The man stands in one, finally calm, while a new customer behind him eyes the next shop's queues.
- **Ending:** `B_loop` (the new customer's look sets up the hook again).
- **Voice / tone:** dry, knowing male narrator; the man has a few muttered lines; the cashier has one.
- **Why it holds:** a universal small frustration; the switch-switch-switch rhythm is pure physical comedy; the "1 in 3" diagram is a satisfying aha; very easy to send to a friend who always complains in line.
- **Research load:** general knowledge (queue probability, attention and memory bias, single-queue systems).
- **Sound intent:** supermarket muzak bed; a scanner beep that speeds up in the other line; a sad trombone on each wrong switch; a clean chime when the diagram's "1/3" lands.

### C5 — ลูกบอลสีแดงที่หมามองไม่เห็น

- **Sub-genre:** animals (how dogs see).
- **Staging:** one character and a golden retriever (kit animal `dog-golden`) in a sunny park; the whole painted scene re-tints into the dog's view, which the watercolour palette can do in one move.
- **Logline:** An owner throws a bright red ball onto green grass and the dog runs right past it. We switch the whole painting to dog vision and see why: to a dog, red and green look almost the same, so the "cute" red toy is the hardest one to find.
- **Hook (Thai):** "ลูกบอลสีแดงที่คุณซื้อให้หมา… ในสายตามัน แทบกลืนไปกับหญ้า"
- **Beats:**
  1. Park, morning light. The owner throws a red ball; the dog sprints, overshoots, sniffs around in confusion. Owner points, shouts, mimes, all with exaggerated acting.
  2. The painting "flips" to dog vision: reds and greens wash into similar brownish-yellows, while blues stay strong. `unchecked`
  3. Why: people have three kinds of colour sensors in the eye; dogs have two. They are not seeing in black and white, a common myth. `unchecked`
  4. The dog finds the ball anyway, by nose, and with sharp sense of motion. `unchecked`
  5. Payoff: the owner throws a blue ball. In dog vision it pops off the grass; the dog catches it mid-air. Last line: "ครั้งหน้าที่ซื้อของเล่น… ลองถามตัวเองว่า หมาเห็นมันไหม"
- **Ending:** `B_loop`.
- **Voice / tone:** warm, cheerful female narrator; the dog "speaks" only with barks and whines; the owner has two short lines.
- **Why it holds:** pet owners recognise the scene instantly; the palette flip is a striking visual payoff that only works because the frame is painted; the myth-buster (not black and white) plus a practical tip makes it shareable among dog owners.
- **Research load:** general knowledge (canine dichromacy, the black-and-white myth, motion sensitivity).
- **Sound intent:** bright acoustic guitar; birdsong ambience; a soft "shimmer" riser for the palette flip, which carries the reveal; a happy bark as the stinger on the catch.

### C6 — ถ้าคุณตัวเล็กเท่ามด (wild card)

- **Sub-genre:** "what if" physics (scale).
- **Staging:** one tiny 4-head character in a giant macro world: grass blades like trees, a dew drop like a boulder, a breadcrumb like a sofa; the small-scale-body gags are the whole point.
- **Logline:** A person shrinks to the size of an ant. Falling becomes harmless, water becomes a sticky trap and staying warm becomes hard, because when you get small, the rules of the world change.
- **Hook (Thai):** "ถ้าคุณตัวเล็กเท่ามด… ตกจากตึกสิบชั้น คุณจะไม่เป็นอะไรเลย"
- **Hook claim status:** `unchecked`. The ten-storey survival claim is the hook's promise; if Boat cannot support it at that height, the hook keeps its shape with a smaller verified height.
- **Beats:**
  1. The character shrinks (painted "pop"), looks up at a forest of grass. A breeze blows them off a table edge.
  2. Falling: they drift down slowly and land unhurt, because for something that small the air slows it more than it can speed up. `unchecked`
  3. Water: they touch a dew drop and their hand sticks; the drop clings like jelly. At this size the skin of water is strong enough to trap you. `unchecked`
  4. Cold and hunger: tiny bodies lose heat fast, so they shiver in sunlight and eat a crumb bigger than their head. `unchecked`
  5. Payoff: an ant walks past easily, carrying a huge load. "เพราะโลกใบเดียวกัน… ใช้กฎไม่เหมือนกัน ขึ้นอยู่กับว่าคุณตัวใหญ่แค่ไหน"
- **Ending:** `B_loop`.
- **Voice / tone:** curious, wonder-filled narrator (male or female); the tiny character gasps and squeaks (Gemini vocal sounds), with one line: "ทำไมน้ำมันเหนียวแบบนี้!"
- **Why it holds:** the hook promises a surprising survival fact; every beat is a small-body gag that suits the 4-head cast; the painted macro world (dew, backlit grass, warm sun) is beautiful on its own; "what if I were tiny" is a classic share prompt.
- **Research load:** general knowledge (terminal velocity and size, surface tension at small scale, heat loss and surface-to-volume ratio).
- **Kit gap (why it is the wild card):** no macro-scale set pieces (giant grass, dew drop, crumb), no insects in `ANIMALS`, and a shrink transition does not exist yet. Film and Boss would scope these in W0.2.
- **Sound intent:** delicate celesta and harp; exaggerated macro sounds (a dew drop "boing", grass creaking like trees); wind carries the fall; silence before the ant reveal, then a tiny triumphant sting.
