# EDU-EP07-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP07-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, what-if physics, concept C15 "ถ้าดวงอาทิตย์หายไปตอนนี้", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md`). Research date: 2026-11-08.
- Central question: if the Sun vanished now, what would people on Earth notice, and when (sky, Moon, Jupiter, Earth's orbit)?
- Short answer: (1) Sky goes dark about 8 min 19 s after the vanishing (mean; 8 min 11 s to 8 min 27 s through the year). "8 min 20 s" is the usual rounded figure: `settled`. (2) General relativity says changes in gravity travel at light speed, so Earth would keep curving for the same ~8 min and then move straight. This is a well-supported consequence of GR plus measurements (binary pulsars within 1%; GW170817 within about 1 part in 10^15), but the vanishing itself is physically impossible, so the picture is a thought experiment, not an observation. (3) The concept's GW170817 sentence needs a fix: the light did NOT arrive "together" with the waves; gamma rays came about 1.7 s AFTER the waves, after ~130 million years of travel. (4) The Moon goes dark 0 to 2.6 s after the sky, depending on phase ("about a second" only holds near quarter phase). (5) The Jupiter figure "35 to 50 min" is WRONG for what the viewer would see: it is Jupiter-to-Earth light time only. Jupiter goes dark about 66 to 87 min after the sky does. Must change.
- Tooling limits: pages fetched through a summarising page fetch (a small model returned the quoted text, so quotes are as returned, not read raw). NASA Sun facts page does not state the 8:20 figure; Smithsonian page does. Carlip's PDF could not be text-extracted (binary), so Carlip is `lead-only`. Big Think (Siegel) returned 403. Forum threads seen only as search excerpts. Numbers in the derived table are my own arithmetic (script run, inputs from cited NASA values), not a published source.

## Derived timing table (arithmetic, inputs SRC003/SRC004/SRC005/SRC006)

1 au light time = 499.005 s = 8.317 min. Delay is measured from the moment the Sun vanishes. "After sky" = total minus 8.3 min.

| Event | From vanishing | After the sky goes dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sky and Earth's gravity change | 8 min 19 s (8:11 to 8:27 over the year) | 0 |
| Moon (quarter phase, 384,400 km) | about 8 min 20 s + 1.3 s | about 1.3 s |
| Moon (full, Moon behind Earth) | about 8 min 19 s + 2.6 s | about 2.6 s |
| Sun to Jupiter (light arrives at Jupiter) | 41 to 45 min (mean 43 min) | 33 to 37 min |
| Jupiter at opposition, perihelion to aphelion | about 74 to 83 min | about 66 to 74 min |
| Jupiter at quadrature (mean) | about 86 min | about 77 min |
| Jupiter near conjunction (mean) | about 95 min | about 86 min |

Note: Jupiter itself goes unlit 33 to 37 min after the sky darkens (43 − 8 min), but Earth keeps seeing its reflected light for the extra 33 to 54 min the light needs to come here. The viewer-facing number is the last row group, about an hour or more after the sky. Safe line: "ดาวพฤหัสบดียังสว่างต่ออีกราวชั่วโมงกว่า".
