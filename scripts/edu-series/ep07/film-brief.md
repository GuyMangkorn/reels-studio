## Visual intent

- **Audience knowledge and mood.** The viewer starts on an ordinary hot afternoon and is told the Sun just vanished. The film then plays dramatic irony: the viewer sees (top) that the Sun is gone and the "news" is still travelling, while the street (bottom) carries on, sweating and joking. Feelings in order: a chill (B01), a smile at the irony under a countdown (B02), awe at the 2017 race (B03), a hard silence and a dark that is beautiful rather than black (B04), wonder at a sky full of old light, then a small shiver as the sunny street returns and the narrator says the light on your skin is already 8 minutes old (B05). The vanishing, the stall, the people, the clock, the pop and the dog are **dramatization**; the space panels are **labelled-by-style diagrams** (schematic, not to scale), never documentary images.
- **Story first.** A mid-afternoon side street (soi) in Bangkok, around 15:00, hot and hazy. A somtam cart sits on the footpath in front of a cream boundary wall spilling magenta bougainvillea, under a faded orange-and-cream beach umbrella; a concrete utility pole with its tangle of cables, a rain tree over the wall, a row of three-storey shophouses running off to the right, two hazy condo towers far behind. The **vendor (VEN)** is a cheerful young woman in her mid-twenties with a ponytail, cream short-sleeve shirt and teal apron, pounding papaya in a big clay mortar with a wooden pestle: her rhythm is the street's heartbeat. The **customer (CUS)** is a working man in his mid-thirties on his afternoon break, short black hair, tan skin, grey-blue short-sleeve work shirt, sitting on a red plastic stool at the sunny edge of the umbrella's shade, his bare forearm resting on his knee in full sun (CL03). He is hot and a little whiny; she is breezy. A pale first-quarter Moon hangs in the afternoon sky above the shophouse roofs (planted for L10). Up above, in space, there is no street: a dark, deep-blue star field, a small blue Earth on a thin cream orbit line, and the Sun, then the place where the Sun was.
- **Medium.** Painted 2D, p5.brush, [painted 3D camera](../../../docs/film-visual-brief-workflow.md#painted-3d-camera) (project default, user 5 Nov 2026). The soi is one world-metre set (SET-SOI, day and night light states) seen through the kit yaw camera (`kit/ycam.ts`), the camera moving inside the set every shot; VEN and CUS are 4-head kit rigs as flat cards squared to the lens (`squareToCam`). Space is its own small set in **schematic units** (SET-SPACE, 1 su = Earth's orbit radius, sizes stylised), seen through the same yaw camera so the orbit is a tilted ellipse and the camera can drift and push. The 2017 cutaway is a third schematic set (SET-GW). The split frame (VD05) paints two cameras into one plate. Backgrounds are washes without outlines; characters, cart and hero props get one thin ink line and one cel shade. Thai text and digits (the clock card) are rendered by Remotion in screen space, never painted.
- **Two worlds, one news front.** The film's argument is drawn as one shape: after the Sun vanishes, a thin pale ring (the **news front**) leaves the empty spot and grows at a steady rate keyed to the clock; inside the ring the sunlight has stopped, outside it the last sunlight is still flowing toward Earth as a stream of small warm dashes. Earth stays lit and keeps curving until the ring reaches it at 00:00; then Earth's day side goes dark and Earth goes straight (L09). The ring stands for "the change", light and gravity together (L05: both at light speed); it carries no number and no label. In L12 the same dashes show the two-leg trip to Jupiter and back.

- **Light arc.** B01 bright warm afternoon (the warmest, most saturated frames), then a cool space cut. B02–B03 the street stays exactly as sunny (that is the point); the space panel is cool. B04 the sky darkens over ~1 s to a starry night with a cool moon rim, the street goes blue-grey and still; after the Moon fades only starlight remains, readable, cool. B05 the star field is the most beautiful dark of the film (wonder), then a hard cut back to the warm afternoon at full B01 values. Light carries feeling; the only "fact" in the light is its order (street stays lit until 00:00, Moon a few seconds later, Jupiter later still).
- **Camera language.** Vertical 1080×1920. Street at standing/seated eye level (eye 1.05–1.35 m), small slow moves: pushes, ≤ 6° arcs, one tilt-up into the sky (B01-SH01) and one tilt-up into the stars (B05-SH01). Space shots drift or push slowly; no shake, no whip, no zoom across scales (street ↔ space is always a cut). Close on faces for the dialogue lines (D01 r ~1.5 m, D02 r ~1.6 m; [UT13](../../../docs/user-taste.md#ut-close-camera)); the establishing L01 framing is a medium-wide two-shot at r ~2.7 m because it must read the whole stall and the sky (UT01) and it is the loop frame.
- **Frame zones.** Captions white with the edu stroke, no overlay band, `captionStyle.bottomEdgeY` 1344, ≤ 2 lines, band y 1176–1344; faces, the mortar, the Moon, Earth and every key diagram event stay above y 1160 or below y 1360. `@TamMaiLa` channel mark top-left (`video.chrome` default); nothing important above y 140. **Clock card zone** (B02–B04 only): fixed screen box x 700–1020, y 160–280, top right; nothing important there in any shot that shows the card. **Split frame** (VD05): space panel y 0–760, a thin cream seam line at y 760, street panel y 760–1920.
- **Conceal / reveal.** CL03 (the sunlit forearm) is visible in B01-SH01 and B02-SH02 but never featured until the B05-SH02 insert. CL01 (the clock) appears only after the vanish, on L03. CL02 (Jupiter) is not singled out before the "+1 ชม." card (fact-check d: Jupiter's dot appears after the time jump). The pale daytime Moon is in the L01 frame but small and unlit by any emphasis; it pays off in L10.
- **Truth limits on picture** (fact-check and research N3, binding): the clock runs **08:19 → 00:00**, visibly fast, and lands on 00:00 on L08's "ศูนย์"; Earth leaves along the **tangent** (straight on in its direction of motion), never outward from the Sun; the Moon is a **first-quarter** Moon, its lit half facing the Sun's direction; Jupiter's size is stylised; the 2017 cutaway shows the **ripple first, the flash a hair later** (small gap, no number), never "together"; the blackout is a **darkening to a starry night**, not a flat black frame; no frost, no temperature cue, no electric light switching on or off (no street lamps, no stall bulb; D03 is kept); "ยังสว่างต่อ" is never drawn as Jupiter glowing by itself (L12 diagram: Jupiter lit only on its Sun-facing side by arriving sunlight, then the reflected light still in flight); no on-screen numbers except the clock digits and "+1 ชม.", no "2017" or "130" text (subtitles carry them), no Moon or 2017 gap number; the orbital motion and the Earth departure in the space panels are **time-compressed** (stylised like the clock), not real speed; the pop, ripple "whomp" and ping are stylised sound.
