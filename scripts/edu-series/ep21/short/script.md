## Story core and ending

Logline: an occupant credits rubber tires for protection until the tire damage exposes the role of the conducting metal enclosure.
Central question: how can metal that conducts electricity help protect the occupant? Intention: understand why this enclosed car offers protection. Obstacle: confusing insulation with exterior routing, then confusing occupant protection with an undamaged vehicle.
Feeling: surprise → apparent contradiction → a new reading of the same metal shell.
Takeaway: an enclosed hard-top metal vehicle with closed windows helps protect occupants through its conducting exterior; rubber tires do not explain that protection, and the vehicle can still be damaged.
Ending chosen first: the threatening conductor becomes the protective route. This is bounded general knowledge, not a survival guarantee or a real strike reenactment.

- Frame 0 must already establish an enclosed metal hard-top car, closed windows and a passenger inside. L02's whole enclosure matters: roof alone does not satisfy the claim. Do not depict an open convertible, motorcycle or person leaning on the exterior as protected.
- B02's route graphic is labelled explanatory, not a measured path. No single exclusive line, exact roof entry, four-wheel exit or magical sealed bubble. B04 must remain compatible with some discharge reaching components.
- Tire damage and dashboard failure are selected possible consequences in a hypothetical story. Do not accumulate glass explosion, fire and every possible failure or imply all struck vehicles share this sequence.
- Do not prove protection by an unharmed grin, a stunt invitation or a guaranteed-survival caption. L11 preserves non-guarantee; keep the final line anchored to this same scoped example.
