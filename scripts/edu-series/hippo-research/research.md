# Research packet: "Hippos kill the most people?" (edu)

**Author:** Boat (research stage only; no script, no fact-check of wording yet). **Research date:** 2026-11-15. **Channel:** edu (Thai Short). **Revision:** R1.
**Story type:** edu, claim-checking twist. **Central question:** is "the hippo is the animal that kills the most people" true? **Learning takeaway candidate:** a number repeated everywhere can have no data behind it; the real danger ranking depends on what you count.
**Access note:** many primary pages blocked or empty to the fetch tool (CNN 451, Africa Check 403, Britannica 403, Cambridge reader partial, Rajya Sabha redirect, Frontiers PDF unreadable). Where a number reaches me only through a secondary summary it is labelled `via`. Confidence is qualitative.

Classification tags: `documented fact` / `reported` / `folklore` / `hypothesis`.

## 3. Supported story angles and twist directions

**Evidence supports**
1. **"The famous number has no data behind it."** F001-F005, F016. Hook: everyone "knows" the hippo kills the most; ask who counted. Strongest, true, and about how we know things. Twist-fit: the rule "a figure repeated is not a figure measured".
2. **"Even if you believe 500, hippo is nowhere near the top."** F020-F022, F028. Mosquito, snake, dog are hundreds to thousands of times bigger. Safe, order-of-magnitude, with WHO sources. Counters the ask "so how many?" without needing the unknowable hippo number. Careful: avoid the claim "only 50" (F014 is hypothesis).
3. **"Not even the deadliest big wild animal in the records we have."** F015, F030. Crocodiles and elephants beat hippos in three national datasets. Must be scoped ("ในข้อมูลที่มีจากบางประเทศ" is a source phrase, ER10 forbids; instead word narrowly: "ในหลายประเทศที่มีสถิติ จระเข้กับช้างฆ่าคนมากกว่า" is a claim, not a source phrase, but check with Boat).
4. **Flip: "Rarely deadly in total, but when it attacks it often kills."** F013, F031. Supported only as hypothesis (site-specific); acceptable as a flip if kept narrow ("มักถึงตาย") or cut (ER07).
5. **Where the danger really is: on the water and at night.** F040-F041, F044. Boats, nets, crop edges; speed on land. Good "so what do people really face" beat.

**Evidence does not support (avoid or label)**
- "Hippos kill ~50 a year" as fact (extrapolation only).
- "Hippos kill 500/year" as fact; "deadliest large land mammal" in any form.
- "Mothers with calves cause attacks" (F043), "bite force 1,800 psi" (F045), "most territorial animal in Africa", any quote from Lewison or Templer on screen until SRC011/SRC017 are opened.
- "Crocodiles kill 1,000/year" as current fact (conflicts with database ~150-160/yr).
- "Hippo vs lion" ranking claim.
- "Mosquitoes kill X" as bites; the cause is a parasite/virus carried by them (needs a plain line).

## 4. Gaps and uncertainties

1. Africa-wide hippo deaths unknown. Best proxy: Zambia ~11/yr (F010, secondary), Zimbabwe ~3/yr per OWID (unverified in primary). No Mozambique per-species counts seen; Table 1 of SRC005 should be pulled.
2. 500's true origin untraced (BBC and Nat Geo statements; Fenner 1987 lead unverified). Africa Check and CNN not read.
3. Crocodile 1,000 (Gates 2014) vs ~150-160 (CrocBITE 2010s) is unreconciled; CrocBITE not opened.
4. Elephant and lion global numbers rest on one OWID synthesis plus Indian Parliament answers read second-hand; Packer 2005 not opened.
5. Hippo territory/calves/bite need a primary (IUCN assessment, San Diego Zoo, hippo literature); only pop sources found.
6. Dunham: lion+hippo 55% vs Frontiers saying hippo 55%; confirm in Table 1.
7. The Frontiers 2022 reference list (last 36k chars) and the 2024 PeerJ gait paper unread.
8. Wording risk for mosquito: say "ยุงนำโรค" not "ยุงฆ่า".
