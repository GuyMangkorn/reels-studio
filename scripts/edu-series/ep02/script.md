## Story core

- **Logline:** A cook cries over every onion she cuts. We go inside the cut to see that the onion's chemistry only switches on when its cells break, watch the eye wash the gas away, grade the folk tricks honestly, and land on the silly one that works best: swim goggles.
- **Central question:** Why does cutting an onion make you cry, and what actually helps?
- **Character intention / obstacle:** she wants to chop an onion with dry eyes; the onion's chemistry (and bad advice) keeps beating her.
- **Intended feeling:** comic sympathy at the waterfall tears, a small "oh" at the inside view, laughs at the trick montage, a satisfied deadpan payoff.
- **Learning takeaway:** the tears start only when you cut: breaking the cells lets a stored enzyme meet a sulfur compound, and a second enzyme turns the result into a tear-making gas; nerve endings on the eye's surface sense it and the eye flushes it with tears. Mouth tricks do nothing for the eye; candles and chilling are unproven; goggles block the gas best (informal tests); a sharp knife and slow cutting reduce the spray from the cut.

## Truth and ending

- **What happens in the story:** a whole onion sits harmless on the counter; the cook cuts it and bursts into tears. We push into the cut, see two things stored apart meet, and a second enzyme make a (illustrated, lilac) gas that drifts up to her eyes; her eyes flood. She tries bread in her mouth, a candle, a chilled onion: still crying. She puts on swim goggles, looks ridiculous, and cuts on dry-eyed, slowly, with a sharp knife. The closing image makes the "alarm" a figure of speech; hold, then fade.
- **Verified (per research, pending W1.3 on this wording):** reaction starts on damage, whole onion "แทบไม่มีอะไร" (F005, F001, F004); enzyme + sulfur compound stored apart (F001, F004); a second enzyme makes the tear gas (F002, F003); corneal nerve endings trigger reflex tearing to wash it off (F006); mouth tricks irrelevant to the eye (F016); candle evidence conflicts (F015); chilling not supported, said only as "หลักฐานยังไม่ชัด" (F011); goggles best-supported in informal tests (F012); sharp knife and slow cutting reduce droplets (F009, F010); no intent (F020).
- **Remaining uncertainty kept in VO:** candle "ยังออกมาไม่ตรงกัน" (L12); chilling "หลักฐานก็ยังไม่ชัด" (L13); goggles "ในการลองแบบไม่เป็นทางการ" (L14); knife/slow cut limited to "ลดละอองที่กระเด็น", never "ไม่ร้องไห้" (L16); alarm is "เหมือน" (L18), with "ไม่ได้ตั้งใจ" said first (L17).
- **Deliberately not used (rules 1–8):** TRPA1; "sulfuric acid"; any percentage or fold number; peeling order, root end; "everyone cries"; Smile Ball / Sunions / House Foods coda (user); the defence-against-pests hypothesis (held as optional ALT-A); fan, running water (time; weak evidence); organelle names; "cold + sharp knife = dry eyes".
- **Twist / reveal logic:** the reversal is in the hook: the onion is "innocent" until cut. It is fair because L04 shows the whole onion doing nothing and L06–L07 show why (the parts are kept apart until the cells break). The comic reversal of the trick montage is that the winner is the silliest-looking one; it is fair because L08 has already placed the trigger at the eye, so L11 (mouth is irrelevant) and L15 (goggles keep the gas off the eye) follow from what the viewer saw.
- **Ending:** `A_fade` (user, 2026-11-05). L18 pairs with the hook: hook "…จนกว่าคุณจะทำร้ายมันก่อน" ↔ last line "…ที่ดังเฉพาะตอนโดนบุก" (only when broken into). After L18: hold ~1.0 s on the goggled, dry-eyed cook mid-cut, then a ~0.5–1.0 s fade to black. Not a loop seam; no match to the first frame required.
