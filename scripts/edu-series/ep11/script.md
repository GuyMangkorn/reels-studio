## Story core

- **Logline:** if a lift's cables snapped and it fell from the 20th floor, would jumping at the last moment save you? From that height it barely helps: you are falling as fast as the car, about 120 km/h near the bottom, and the best jump takes off about 10, so a 20-floor fall becomes a 17-floor fall; and you cannot see the floor to time it. But can a lift even fall like that? In 1854 Otis stood on a raised platform and ordered the rope cut; it dropped a little and his brake caught the rails. Today several cables each hold the car alone, and if all of them broke a brake grabs the rails. The lift stops, so you stop.
- **Central question:** does a last-second jump save you in a falling lift?
- **Intention / obstacle:** WOMAN wants to survive the fall with the famous jump; the obstacle is that she shares the car's speed and cannot see the bottom.
- **Feeling:** dread (the snap, the float); a laugh at "ชั้นสิบเจ็ด"; frustration (ceiling bump, "too late"); curiosity at "แต่เดี๋ยวนะ"; the crowd's gasp in 1854; relief when the jaws grab and her hair settles; calm at the alarm button.
- **Takeaway:** in a falling lift you move as fast as the car, so a jump from high up barely changes anything; the real protection is a brake that grabs the rails, an idea Otis showed in 1854, plus several cables that each can hold the car, so a modern lift almost cannot fall that way at all.
- **The one rule (ER03 refrain):** "ลิฟต์เร็วเท่าไหร่ คุณก็เร็วเท่านั้น" (you are as fast as the lift). Cost: the jump only subtracts a little from the lift's speed (L03–L05) and a mistimed jump leaves you floating (L07). Flip: the same rule means a brake that stops the lift stops you (L15 "ลิฟต์หยุด… คุณก็หยุดไปด้วย").
