# EDU-EP11-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP11-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, concept C32 "ลิฟต์ขาด กระโดดตอนท้ายรอดมั้ย", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md` `### C32`). Research date: 2026-11-08.
- Central question: does a last-second jump save you in a falling lift, can a modern lift fall at all, and what did Otis show in 1854?
- Short answer: (1) A last-second jump removes only a few m/s from an impact speed of about 34 m/s after a 60 m fall: `settled` arithmetic. (2) A modern lift almost cannot free-fall: several ropes that each can carry the car, plus a governor and safety gear, plus pit buffers: `settled` as design, `hedge` as "แทบ". (3) Otis's public demonstration at the New York Crystal Palace was 1854 and the first passenger safety lift was 1857 at Haughwout: `settled` for years, month and day differ by source.
- Tooling limits: web search excerpts plus fetched pages. Britannica and Elevator World pages returned 403; HowStuffWorks returned empty. No standards text (EN 81-20, ASME A17.1) was opened, so the safety claim rests on industry and trade sources (`secondary`). No physical test was run. Everything here is desk research.

## Corrections and recommendations to the C32 entry (for Preaw)

1. **Hook.** The hook "คุณกระโดดตอนจะถึงพื้น… รอดมั้ย" asks a question, not advice, and the answer is "no". Make sure the hook's frame is "ถ้าลิฟต์ร่วงจริง" (hypothetical) and that the payoff clearly says do not rely on this. Add a short audience-facing safety note: lifts do not work this way; if a lift stops or shakes, press the alarm or call button and wait. Do not add any survival instruction. The viewer-safety risk is a viewer taking "do the jump" as a plan, or a child trying to jump in a lift (jumping in a working lift can also trip safety stops); the script avoids both if beat 2 says the jump fails and beat 5 says you will not need it.
2. **Beat 2 numbers.** "ราว 120 จาก ~10": use "ประมาณ 120 กม./ชม." and "ประมาณ 10 กม./ชม." (F001, F002). Say "ร่วงจากชั้น 20" is "ประมาณ 60 เมตร" only as an estimate. Preaw's concept said "a hundred or more" in the logline: 120 is correct from 20 floors; for other floors it differs (sqrt(2gh)).
3. **Beat 2 wording risk (F003).** "barely changes anything" is right for a fall from 60 m (speed down 7–9%). It is not true for a low drop, and SRC001 says a very well-timed jump helps in a 1–3 storey fall. Keep "จากความสูงขนาดนี้" so the claim does not become "a jump never helps".
4. **Beat 4 (F012).** Do not say he cut the rope himself. "สั่งตัดเชือก" or "เชือกถูกตัด". The Preaw logline "cutting the rope under himself in public" and the staging line "a man on a platform... cuts the rope himself" should change in the script and the scene description. The visual of the cutter is a staging choice; either is fine if narration does not claim who cut.
5. **Beat 5 last line (F015).** "ก่อนจะมีลิฟต์โดยสารตัวแรกในโลกซะอีก" is `unsupported`. Suggested: "สิ่งที่คุณกลัวที่สุด… ถูกแก้ไว้ตั้งแต่ปี 1854 ก่อนลิฟต์โดยสารที่มีเบรกนิรภัยตัวแรกจะเปิดใช้ด้วยซ้ำ" or shorter "…ถูกแก้ไว้ตั้งแต่ปี 1854 ก่อนจะมีลิฟต์โดยสารที่มีเบรกนิรภัยตัวแรกอีก". Shorter fits a Short; the "safety" word is the fix. Premise/reveal impact: none for the plot; the line loses a little punch and keeps the same turn.
6. **Beat 5 mechanism (F007, F013).** "Jaws grab" is a visual of the principle; say "เบรกจับราง". Do not claim the governor or modern gear is the 1854 device: Otis's device is the ancestor of the idea, not of the exact modern part. Narration should not say "the same brake you ride today".
7. **Not to say:** "ลิฟต์ไม่มีวันตก", "ไม่เคยมีอุบัติเหตุ", rope counts as a rule, 115% or other standards numbers, "lie flat", "jump at X floor to survive", any survival tip, "Otis invented the elevator" (he did not; he invented the safety brake), the "All safe, gentlemen" quote.
8. **Visuals.** Floor counter and speed gauge are dramatization of a hypothetical (label "ภาพจำลอง" or let the opening frame carry "ถ้า…"); the 1850s fair insert is a reconstruction, no period photograph is claimed. Cut away before any impact (per task). No casualty image.
9. **Sound.** Cable-snap stinger, fall whoosh and metal clamp are fictional or reconstructed effects (no real recording needed); a crowd gasp for 1854 is reconstructed, label with the picture. Sound must not imply a real accident or a casualty. No source needed for the chime.
