## Story core and ending

Logline: you are the eunuch who must taste every dish before the emperor eats, yet the last emperor never touches that long table; he eats what the dowager's side sends, and the silver check on each dish catches less than it seems.
Central question: if the emperor does not eat the long table, why is it set at all? Intention: understand what the famous poison-tasting ritual looked like at the real Qing table. Obstacle: the viewer's picture (eunuch tastes, silver warns, emperor eats) fits only part of what Puyi described.
Feeling: role-play curiosity → surprise (he doesn't touch it) → comic relief (he still eats about twenty dishes) → doubt (silver) → the table reread as a display, and the eye moved to the dishes he actually ate.
One rule that flips (ER03): **"ยิ่งด่านเยอะ ยิ่งดูปลอดภัย"** (the more checkpoints, the safer it looks). Planted as the viewer's comfort (L08) and flipped at the silver beat (L13 "ด่านนี้ ดูปลอดภัยกว่าที่เป็นนะ": looks safer than it is). R3 no longer repeats it in L16: the user found the "ดู" play (ดูปลอดภัย / ไว้ให้ดู) confusing, so the refrain closes at L13, where the flip is clear on first hearing because it echoes L08's words five lines earlier, and the last line is the payoff (ER03 allows "the flip or the payoff").
Knowledge takeaway: in the last Qing court each dish carried a silver tag and a eunuch tasted before serving, but Puyi did not eat the imperial kitchen's long table, which he said was only for show; he ate the dishes from the dowager's side, set in front of him; and silver darkens on sulfur (a boiled egg has it), not on the poison itself.
Ending chosen first (R3, user option A): closed, concrete ending without wordplay, then a ~3 s hold. L16 sets the two tables side by side: the long table was for looking at, the food he really ate was the dishes in front of him. It names where the food was (the memoir: the dowager's dishes "放在我面前", the kitchen's "远远摆在一边") and says nothing about whether either was tasted, safe or hidden, and nothing that the tasting was fake; it never says the eunuch tasted food nobody ate (C06).

## Film constraints and revision scope

- Frame 0: the child emperor and the long silver-lidded table together. Keep him a child; no adult Puyi.
- The child meets each problem before the narrator names it (ER02): he looks past the long table before L02, reaches for the small tray before L04, dips the silver strip in the egg before L11.
- The eunuch tasting is shown plainly and calmly; no choking, collapsing or "poisoned taster" drama (no poisoning is claimed in this story). Do not show eunuchs tasting the dowager's tray or pointedly skipping it (neither is documented).
- Do not label or stage the long table as untouched by everyone, thrown away or tasted "for nobody". No "bland" imagery or caption for the long table (FACT-R1 F01); "made for show, not for eating" is the only claim about it.
- No Ming, Tang or Korean imagery; no costume or set lifted from palace dramas as if documentary.
- Film owns camera, medium and staging. No pictures, sound or timing have been produced or validated here.
