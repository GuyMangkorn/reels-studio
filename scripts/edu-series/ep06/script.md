## Story core

- **Logline:** a cat lover stares at her cat with wide, loving, unblinking eyes; the cat flattens its ears, turns away and leaves. Seen from the cat's side, that stare may read as a challenge. When she instead narrows her eyes and closes them slowly, the cat narrows its eyes back and walks over to her hand.
- **Central question:** why does my cat look away when I gaze at it lovingly, and what can I do instead?
- **Intention / obstacle:** OWN wants her cat close; her own way of showing love (a long, wide-eyed stare) is the obstacle.
- **Feeling:** a laugh of recognition at the rejected stare, "oh no" at the glare repaint, calm during the counted blink, a warm small win when the cat answers, and a quiet glow at the end when the cat blinks back at her and she smiles.
- **Takeaway:** a long, fixed, unblinking stare may come across to many cats as pressure rather than love; a slow blink is a softer signal, and in a small study cats tended to narrow their eyes back and approach a little more. Not every cat answers; let the cat come to you.

## Truth and ending

- **Spoken as hedged knowledge:** C03/C04 stare may read as a challenge/threat ("อาจ", "หลายตัว"), never "every stare" and never what the cat "thinks" (L02, L06). C05 "ภาษากายแมว / ภาษาแมว" is a figure of speech for body signals.
- **Spoken as a study finding, with limits:** C07 cats tend to narrow their eyes or slow-blink back (L09); C08 they approached an unfamiliar person's hand a little more than when the person looked away (L10). "การวิจัยเล็ก ๆ" signals the small sample (18 + 18 cats); "มีแนวโน้ม" and "นิดนึง" signal a tendency and a modest effect.
- **Practice tip (not a study claim):** C14 sit about a metre away (study protocol); "หนึ่ง… สอง" count = a hedged practice tip (paper: closure over 0.5 s; "a couple of seconds" is the press-release quote, SRC006). C12/C15 "not every cat answers, let it come to you" spoken in L12.
- **Closing (C10):** not stated as fact that the blink means love. L13 says it is a good signal that "many cat people translate as love" (attribution to people, not to the cat or the study).
- **Dramatization only (no narrator fact):** the cat's ears flat and turn-away (C02), the cat-read glare repaint (C06; narration frames it with "ลองนึก…"), the cat's eye answer (drawn as narrowing to a half-close, matching C07; a full slow close would be extra), the glow, the chime, the purr (not "purr = happy"), the head-rub into her hand.
- **Not used:** C09 "slow blink interrupts a threatening stare" origin (length; available for a Long), C11, C13 shelter/stray cats, C16 "cat smile" (L08's "เหมือนยิ้ม" describes the human's eyes), C17 cats blinking at each other.
- **Knowledge twist (myth reversal):** B01 sets up the viewer's assumption (a loving gaze = love). The cat's reaction (CL01) plants the doubt; B02 flips it: the wide stare is the problem. The fix is the opposite of what feels loving (close your eyes). Fair: the wide unblinking eyes (CL02) are shown in the first second, and on rewatch the cat's half-closed eyes before the stare (CL03) were already the "answer" she missed.
- **Ending `A_fade` (user decision 2026-11-07, option 1):** the spoken script ends on L13 "…ที่ทาสแมวหลายคนแปลว่า… รัก" (unchanged). No loop line, no hold, no seam. Last visual, after L13: cat ECU, its eyes in a slow half-close toward her (the slow-blink back); cut to her, who smiles softly; purr under; fade out. The ending closes the story's question (she learned the softer signal and the cat answers it) rather than restarting the gag. The closing blink is dramatization, drawn as narrowing to a half-close like B04 (C07).
