## Answers to Preaw's N4

- **(a) L02 "แมวหลายตัว… อาจอ่านสายตาแบบนี้ว่า… เป็นการท้าทาย" (Q1 confirmed): pass.** Supports: SRC001 Methods pilot note (cats "may perceive direct eye contact from humans as threatening"), SRC001 Discussion (an "unbroken stare … potentially threatening in social interactions", citing Emery 2000, SRC003, unopened), SRC002 (do not stare). Hedges present and sufficient: "หลายตัว" (not all), "อาจ" (may), "อ่าน…ว่า" (a reading of a signal, not what the cat thinks). "ท้าทาย" (challenge) sits at the level of the research's accepted "แมวอาจเข้าใจว่าคุณกำลังท้าทาย" and is milder than "ท้าตี". Limits to keep, none needing a word change: (1) "หลายตัว" is a quantity no source counted; it is acceptable only because "อาจ" travels with it. (2) "สายตาแบบนี้" must point at the long, wide, unblinking stare shown in L01–L02; it does, by the cue. (3) The pilot is undocumented (no numbers), so do not add a number or "งานวิจัยพบว่า" to L02. **No change.**
- **(b) L13 "แต่ถ้ามันกะพริบกลับมา… นั่นคือสัญญาณที่ดี… ที่ทาสแมวหลายคนแปลว่า… รัก" (Q2 confirmed): pass with one delivery condition.** Structure: "สัญญาณที่ดี" = SRC001's "positive emotional communication / indicator of positive emotion" (authors' own hedge: "further research would be necessary before coming to firm conclusions about the emotional state"); "รัก" is attributed to "ทาสแมวหลายคน", not to the cat or to the study. That is the research's approved shape ("สัญญาณเชิงบวก ที่คนชอบแปลว่ารัก"). The unsupported part, "the blink means love", is not said as narrator fact. Remaining risks, all delivery not wording: (1) a listener can drop the attributing clause if the pause lands before "ที่ทาสแมวหลายคนแปลว่า" or the music resolve (sound intent B05, "resolves warmly on L13 '…รัก'") lands on "รัก" and sells it as truth. Condition for Editor/voice lock: read "สัญญาณที่ดี ที่ทาสแมวหลายคนแปลว่ารัก" as one phrase with the pause before "รัก" only; the musical resolve is a mood cue, not a statement. (2) "ที่ทาสแมวหลายคนแปลว่า… รัก" as a social claim rests on commercial pages (SRC005, `lead-only`); it is low-risk, popular and unmeasured; do not add "ทุกคน" or a percentage. (3) Optional softening, not required: "นั่นคือสัญญาณที่ดี" to "นั่นก็ถือเป็นสัญญาณที่ดี". (4) "ถ้ามันกะพริบกลับมา" is a conditional and the cue shows the half-close the paper measured; fine. Consistent with L12 ("ไม่ใช่ทุกตัวจะตอบ… ให้มันเดินมาหาเอง"; SRC002: a slow blink is not necessarily an invitation to touch). The "จูบแมว" fallback is not needed. **No change.**
- **(c) L04 "ลองนึกจากฝั่งแมวดู…" as the only imagined-view label: pass, with a picture condition.** The spoken line names the shift as imagination and is attached to the repaint (research C06: "mark as imagined view, not 'this is what cats see'"). An on-screen tag is not required. Conditions for Film: start the repaint on or after "ลองนึก…"; keep it painterly (cool key, hard shadow, hard-glare eyes), not a cat-vision filter (no colour/blur "cat eyesight" look, which would assert how cats see a human face, unknown); L05 "ตาโต… ไม่กะพริบ… จ้องไม่วาง" describes the stare, not the cat's sight. The glare is the owner's eyes dramatized as a threat signal, consistent with SRC002 (wide eyes, fixed stare as cat-side tension signs).
- **(d) L08 "หรี่ตาเหมือนยิ้ม… ค่อย ๆ หลับ… หนึ่ง… สอง… แล้วค่อย ๆ ลืม": pass.** Paper: the slow blink is defined as an eye closure lasting more than 0.5 s (read in Methods and Table 1). A calm "หนึ่ง… สอง" is at least about 1–2 s, so it is inside the protocol, and the Sussex press quote ("narrowing eyes … then closing for a couple of seconds", SRC006, `lead-only`) agrees. The script does not say "the study used two seconds", and "เหมือนยิ้ม" describes her eyes, not a cat claim (C16 unused). Two notes: (1) the study's owners repeated the slow blink about 14.6 times per minute (range 3–30.6) for up to 2 minutes, and waited for cat eye contact before each; the picture shows one blink and a reply within about 2 s. That is dramatization and compresses the study, and the narration never says one blink works, so keep it. Film should not caption or voice it as "one blink = a reply". (2) L11's "แล้วกะพริบช้า ๆ" repeats the blink; fine, and L12 carries the limit.
- **(e) L09, L10 "ในอังกฤษ", "เล็ก ๆ", "นิดนึง", and the stranger/owner picture:**
  - **"ในอังกฤษ" without naming Sussex: pass.** University of Sussex (Brighton); owners "in and around Sussex". "Britain/England" is accurate at that level.
  - **"งานวิจัยเล็ก ๆ" as the sample-size hedge: pass.** 18 pairs (Exp. 1) and 18 cats from 8 households (Exp. 2), after excluding 3 and 6 outliers; one research group; home cats only. "เล็ก ๆ" says small without a number, and L12 "ไม่ใช่ทุกตัวจะตอบ" backs it. Also true and not spoken (no change needed): in Exp. 1 the half-blink rise came from male cats; females showed no strong effect (interaction p = 0.02).
  - **L09 "แมวมีแนวโน้มหรี่ตา หรือกะพริบช้า ๆ ตอบ": pass.** Paper: eye narrowing and half-blinks were higher after slow blinking (Exp. 1: narrowing p = 0.017, half-blinks p = 0.003; Exp. 2: half-blinks p = 0.007, narrowing p = 0.002). Full eye closures were too rare to analyse in Exp. 1 (4 vs 3 cats) and followed age, not condition, in Exp. 2. "หรี่ตา หรือกะพริบช้า ๆ" matches the paper's slow-blink sequence (half-blinks then narrowing or closure) and my safe line. "ตอบ" slightly implies an intended reply; acceptable as everyday wording (the authors themselves frame it as communication); optional "กลับ" if Preaw wants it stricter.
  - **L10 "นิดนึง": pass.** Approach score 2.78 vs 2.39 on a 1–3 scale (z = 2.11, p = 0.035): modest. Matches.
  - **L10 comparison "มากกว่าตอนคนหันหน้าหนี": pass.** The control was a neutral face, experimenter looking slightly to the side, no eye contact. It is correctly not "versus a stare"; the paper says the gaze difference "may potentially have affected" approach. "หันหน้าหนี" is slightly stronger than "มองเลี่ยงไปด้านข้าง" but is the research-approved phrase; optional "ไม่มองตา" is more exact.
  - **L10 "คนแปลกหน้า" vs the owner picture: fix the picture.** The finding is Exp. 2: an unfamiliar experimenter offered a flat palm, then 1 minute of slow blinking, then the palm again for about 3.7 s. Exp. 1 (owners) measured cat eye movements only; no approach. The cue "She offers a flat open hand… the cat sniffs it, then rubs its head" shows the owner. Narration says stranger, picture says owner, so a viewer hears one claim and sees another and may leave thinking the study showed cats approach their owners after a slow blink. That is knowledge-relevant but not premise-breaking. **Required fix (picture only, no line change):** on L10 show the offered hand as an unfamiliar person's (a visitor or neighbour, hand only or a figure seen from behind, a different sleeve or wrist from the owner's, no face needed); the cat approaches that hand; then back to the owner for V02/L11. If a second hand or character is not wanted, the fallback is Preaw switching L10 to an owner-safe result (the Exp. 1 eye response) and dropping approach and the stranger. Preaw/Film choose; wording stays as written. The head-rub is dramatization; the paper's approach score is any head/body movement toward the hand, not rubbing.
  - **L10 "ยอมเดินเข้าหา": recommended tweak.** "Approach" in the paper = any head or body movement toward the offered hand (a lean or a step), coded avoid/neutral/approach. "เดินเข้าหา" narrows it to walking. Suggested L10: **"และยอมขยับเข้าหามือคนแปลกหน้า… มากกว่าตอนคนหันหน้าหนี นิดนึง"** (+1 syllable; picture of walking over stays as dramatization). Low severity: the packet's safe line used "เดินเข้าหา", so this is my correction to my own research wording (see below).
