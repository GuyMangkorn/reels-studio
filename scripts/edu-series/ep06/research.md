# EDU-EP06-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP06-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, cats, concept C8 "กะพริบตาช้า ๆ ใส่แมว", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md`). Research date: 2026-11-07.
- Central question: is a long direct stare a threat among cats, and does a human slow blink make cats slow-blink back and approach?
- Short answer: (1) the stare-as-threat claim is widely held in cat-behaviour practice and is a mechanism the slow-blink authors themselves propose, but no source I inspected measured it directly in cats; "challenge among cats" is `hedge`. (2) The slow-blink effect is a real, peer-reviewed, small-sample result (Humphrey et al. 2020): more narrowed eyes and half-blinks, and a modest rise in approach to an unfamiliar person's hand. It is not proof of "love", of a universal response, or of a full blink back.
- Tooling limits: full text of the primary paper read through a page fetch of the PMC copy (raw article text, not a snippet). Crowell-Davis 2004 PDF downloaded but no PDF text extractor was available, so it is `lead-only` (search excerpt). Everything else is a search excerpt or one fetched web page, as labelled. Experiments on stare vs slow blink inside one design, and shelter-cat gaze studies, were searched but not found (see gaps).

## What SRC001 actually did (read from the paper)

- Experiment 1 (home): 21 cats from 14 households recruited; 3 outliers (>2 SD) excluded, so 18 cat–owner pairs analysed. Owners sat about 1 m away and gave slow blinks (closure over 0.5 s) for up to 2 minutes; control = owner in the room, no interaction. Trial about 63 s on average. Results: cat eye narrowing rate higher with slow blinking (mean 0.06 vs 0.03, p = 0.017); half-blinks higher (0.21 vs 0.15, p = 0.003), driven by male cats (interaction p = 0.02; female cats showed no strong effect). Normal blinks not affected. Full eye closures: only 4 cats in the slow-blink condition and 3 in control, so no analysis was possible.
- Experiment 2 (home, unfamiliar experimenter): 24 cats from local online adverts; 6 outliers excluded, 18 analysed, from 8 households. Experimenter first offered a flat palm, then 1 minute of slow blinking or a neutral face looking slightly away (no eye contact), then offered the hand again. Cats' half-blinks (5.33 vs 1.94, p = 0.007) and eye narrowing (2.78 vs 0.33, p = 0.002) were higher after slow blinking. Approach score (1 avoid, 2 neutral, 3 approach) was higher: mean 2.78 vs 2.39, z = 2.11, p = 0.035. Eye closures in Experiment 2 were related to cat age (older cats more), not to condition.
- Pilot (stated in Methods, no data shown): a control with a neutral face and direct eye contact "indicated that in cats, as in some other species ... individuals may perceive direct eye contact from humans as threatening", so the control was changed to a neutral face without eye contact.
- Authors' own caveats: owner variation in performing the blink; distance not controlled; the control had no eye contact, so "the experimenter's gaze direction or attentional state may potentially have affected cats approach responses"; the approach could reflect an inherently positive state or learned association with humans; unknown whether the behaviour is evolved or learned; replication in a lab suggested; the result concerns cats in home environments (shelter use is only proposed as "could potentially be used to assess welfare").
- Not in the paper: any test of a long stare against a slow blink; any tracking of cat–cat staring; shelter or feral cats; a sign that cats "love" the person.

## Hook overclaim ("คุณอาจกำลังท้าตีมันอยู่")

1. "ท้าตี" = "challenging it to a fight" asserts intent to fight. Evidence supports "may feel threatening / may be read as a challenge" (C03, C04), not an invitation to physical combat.
2. "คุณ" (you) generalizes to all viewers and all stares. The claim concerns a long, fixed, unblinking stare (wide eyes, no break), not every moment of looking. Soft eye contact is common between bonded cats and people.
3. "ในภาษาแมว" suggests an established vocabulary. Safe as figure of speech, but "คำว่ารัก" in the last line leans on the same framing (C10).
4. Hedge verbs: "อาจ" is already present ("คุณอาจ...") which helps. Replace "ท้าตี" with a softer term or leave the hook as dramatization signalled by tone.
5. Hook "จ้องตาแมวด้วยความรัก" plus later "แมวหนีเพราะกลัว" must not say the cat is afraid in a specific cat; it may also just not want attention (C02).
6. Verdict: the hook is acceptable only with "อาจ" and with a softer word than "ท้าตี", e.g. "…แมวอาจมองว่าคุณกำลังข่มมันอยู่" / "…แมวอาจอ่านว่าเป็นการข่มขู่" / "…แมวอาจเข้าใจว่าคุณกำลังท้าทาย". "ท้าตี" alone is `unsupported` as a narrator assertion; it can stay only as an explicitly playful dramatization that the fact-check must still approve.

## Limits summary (for Preaw and fact-check)

- Sample: 18 pairs (Exp. 1) and 18 cats from 8 households (Exp. 2), after excluding 3 and 6 outliers; the Exp. 2 approach p = 0.035 is a modest effect. One research group. Replication not searched.
- Population: owned, indoor cats with outdoor access in the Sussex area; not shelter, feral or stray cats; no breed breakdown.
- Design: Exp. 2 control was looking away, so a stare vs slow-blink comparison was not tested; the Exp. 1 control was no interaction; the owner effect was noisy.
- Interpretation: approach and half-blinks suggest a positive reaction. They do not show affection, intent to reply, or "love". The authors stress more work is needed on the emotional state.
- Stare-as-threat: supported by authors' reasoning, a pilot comment, practitioner advice and one stare-down photo (lead). A cat–cat quantified study was not located. Treat as common behaviour knowledge with a hedge.
- Shelter vs home: shelter use is a proposal only (SRC001). The first-time-meeting result (Exp. 2 stranger) is the nearest evidence that a stranger can get an approach, in home settings.

## Sound implications

- Beat 2's low tense drone is non-factual score, not a recording. Present purr as part of the story's mood; narration should not claim "purr = happy".
