# EDU-EP12-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP12-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, concept C35 "ตอนนี้คุณเตี้ยกว่าตอนเช้า", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md` `### C35`). Research date: 2026-11-08. Mode: documented-fact myth-buster (height is not fixed) plus a spaceflight scale jump. No script is written here.
- Short answer:
  1. Daily loss: `settled` that stature falls during the day and recovers overnight. The sourced figure is **larger than the hook says**: Tyrrell et al. 1985 mean 19.3 mm (1.1 % of stature, 8 young adults); Leatt et al. 1986 mean 14.4 mm. So "เกือบหนึ่งเซน" **understates and its "เกือบ" points the wrong way** (both means are above 1 cm). Recommend "ราว ๆ หนึ่งถึงสองเซน". Also the concept's "about 1 % = about 1 cm" is an arithmetic slip (1 % of 170 cm = 1.7 cm).
  2. Lying down restores it: `settled` as phenomenon (height recovers overnight, Leatt 1986); the disc fluid mechanism is `settled in reviews` (read via search excerpts only). "Wet sponge under a book" is a fair analogy for water squeezed out and back in.
  3. NASA: about 3 % of stature, in the first 3–4 days, mostly spine: `settled`, with a range (see F009). **Do not say or show the discs swelling in space**: NASA's own spine workshop report says the old "discs expand" belief was disproved (F011).
  4. Back pain: `hedge`. The well-documented pain is **in flight** (Space Adaptation Back Pain, 53–68 % of astronauts in the first days, mechanism unknown), not a documented "lands, spine settles, back hurts" chain. Post-flight pain exists but is thinner in the sources; herniated-disc risk is 4.3x higher and highest in the year after a mission. Beat 5 "she lands, the spine settles back, she winces" needs rewording or a dramatization label.
  5. Closing line: `hedge`, fails as a literal household claim (compares different people). Reword to "each person is tallest right after waking".
- Tooling limits: web search excerpts plus fetched pages. The Tyrrell 1985 abstract (Spine 10(2):161–164) came through search excerpts of Scilit; Scilit, Southampton eprints and PubMed returned 403 or a cookie wall, so the abstract text itself was **not opened**. NASA PDFs (TM-20205004306, Mission X handout) were read locally through macOS PDFKit; Chang 2016 via the eScholarship author manuscript (PDFKit). Full text of Johnston 2010 and Vergroesen 2016 not opened.

## What the evidence supports for a 40–60 s Short

- Safe, strong beats: the line drops by about a centimetre or two across a day; bones do not shrink; the soft pads lose water under your weight, like a sponge under a book; lying flat lets them refill; astronauts grow about 2–4 cm in a few days because nothing presses the spine; seats and suits fit differently.
- Unsafe as written: discs swelling in space (F011); back pain caused by the spine settling on landing (F016); "one centimetre" or "almost" (F002); the household-tallest line as literal (F017).
- Honest version of the flip (for Preaw to choose; I do not rewrite the plot): the 3 % gain is real and the pain is real, but science does not yet know why the pain happens; a pad that "refills" at night for you does not "refill" in space (the spine just lengthens). A reading that keeps beat 5's emotion: she lands, and many astronauts' backs hurt; cut to the boy falling into bed.
- Clarity pass (ER05): "หมอนรองกระดูก" at most once after the sponge (already planned). Avoid "microgravity" and "intervertebral"; "ไร้น้ำหนัก" is plain. `misleading-analogy` check: the sponge is fair for water but would imply the pad refills in space (it does not); do not re-use the sponge in the space beat.

## Hedges the Thai narration must carry

- Daily loss: "ราว ๆ หนึ่งถึงสองเซน" and "โดยเฉลี่ย"; not "เกือบหนึ่งเซน".
- Space growth: "สูงขึ้นได้ถึงราว 3 %" / "สองสามเซน"; "ใน 3–4 วันแรก" optional.
- Fit: "ชุดอาจคับ".
- Pain: "นักบินอวกาศหลายคนปวดหลัง" with no cause stated, no "เพราะ".
- Closing: "คุณสูงที่สุดของวัน คือตอนเพิ่งตื่นนอน" (reworded; Preaw owns the final).
- Sound and visuals: a squish per disc squeeze is a stylised sound, fiction sound; the see-through spine is a diagram (label fiction/dramatisation); the landing thump and wince are dramatised, not recorded events; station hum is a generic cue, no claim.

- Whether the loss depends on height, age or sex: not found. Do not give a rule.
