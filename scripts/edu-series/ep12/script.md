## Story core

- **Logline:** a boy marks his height on the doorframe at night and the line lands below the morning mark. His bones did not shrink: the soft pads between his spine bones get squeezed flat by his own weight all day, like a wet sponge under books, and soak water back while he lies down at night. Take the weight away for good, in space, and the spine stretches; astronauts come home a few centimetres taller, and many of them have sore backs. Back on Earth the boy just goes to bed and gets his height back.
- **Central question:** why are you shorter tonight than this morning, and where does the height go?
- **Intention / obstacle:** BOY wants to know if he really shrank; the obstacle is his own weight pressing down all day.
- **Feeling:** "eh?" at the lower line; relief that bones do not shrink; a small "oh" when the line is back in the morning; wonder at the astronaut growing; a wince of sympathy; a warm smile at the boy asleep.
- **Takeaway:** your height changes during the day, by about one to two centimetres on average, mostly because your body weight squeezes water out of the soft pads (discs) in your spine; lying down lets them soak it back, so you are tallest right after waking. In space nothing presses, so the spine stretches and astronauts grow a few centimetres.
- **The one rule (ER03 refrain):** "น้ำหนักตัวกด" (your own weight presses you down) and its switch-off "ไม่มีอะไรกดตัว". Cost by day (L04); switched off in bed (L07) and in space (L08, L09); the flip is that the same switch-off works for you every night for free (L12, L13).
