## Recommendation for Preaw

**Start with P01 helium balloon for the first hook-only pilot.** It offers an instantly visible wrong prediction, an everyday passenger scene, a reversal under braking, and a stable body that both a question opening and a counter-intuitive-result opening can honestly lead into. This is an editorial judgment, not measured audience demand or a retention forecast.

P02 lightning car is the strongest personal-stakes alternative, provided the vehicle conditions survive into both hooks. P05 chilli offers an accessible food/biology contrast. P03 liquefaction and P08 microgravity flame offer visual spectacle, with larger visual explanation demands. P04 astronaut height offers a body/space alternative with a clean temporary-versus-permanent reframe. P06/P07 are additional animal options; their curiosity is more animal-specific and their mechanism needs careful simplification.

The [channel analytics](../../../channels/edu/analytics/2026-11-15.md) supports testing a personal situation, connected questions and a final reframe as hypotheses. It does not establish that these new subjects are popular, that a danger hook is required, or that any particular topic will win.

## Candidates and evidence boundaries

### P01 — helium balloon: the passenger goes back, the balloon goes forward

- Working title: **รถออกตัว ทำไมลูกโป่งฮีเลียมพุ่งไปข้างหน้า ทั้งที่คุณเอนไปข้างหลัง**.
- Central question/takeaway: why can two things inside the same accelerating vehicle move in opposite directions? The surrounding air also responds to acceleration; buoyancy depends on the air around the balloon.
- Supported premise: a helium balloon tethered inside an enclosed accelerating box moves in the direction of acceleration. Stony Brook's demonstration states this explicitly; NASA's archived physics explanation applies it to a car and explains the denser air's role. `F001`, SRC001–SRC002.
- Usable factual progression for Preaw: passenger's prediction → balloon leans forward → ordinary hanging object provides a contrast → reveal the invisible air → braking reverses the acceleration and the predicted balloon lean. The final braking prediction is the same mechanism applied in reverse, not a separately measured car result in the sources inspected.
- Hook boundary: **name helium**, not merely “a balloon.” Use a closed passenger compartment with no strong ventilation dominating the effect; movement is relative to the car/box and occurs under acceleration, not steady speed. A loose, heavy, ordinary air-filled balloon does not establish this result. Keep any demonstration fictional/controlled; no driver-distraction challenge.
- A/B suitability: two genuinely different openings can share the identical prediction/reveal sequence. No new fact is needed solely to strengthen one hook.

### P02 — lightning car: the conductor protects, not the rubber

- Working title: **ฟ้าผ่ารถ ทำไมโลหะที่นำไฟกลับช่วยป้องกันคนข้างใน**.
- Central question/takeaway: why can a conducting shell offer protection from lightning? The metal exterior provides a route around the enclosed occupants.
- Supported premise: NWS identifies protection for occupants of a **hard-topped metal vehicle with closed windows** and says rubber tires are not the reason. A strike can pass through the exterior shell and tires to ground while damaging electronics, glass and tires; fire is possible. `F002`, SRC003.
- Usable progression: fictional strike → viewer guesses rubber → route around metal exterior → damaged vehicle challenges “safe car means undamaged car” → reframe: protection can come from conducting a discharge around someone, rather than blocking it with rubber.
- Hook boundary: no “รอดแน่นอน,” “รถทุกคัน,” “รถไม่เป็นอะไร,” or suggestion that touching a vehicle outside offers the same protection. A convertible/open vehicle is outside the cited shelter condition. Both hooks need the same vehicle scope; a footnote cannot repair an unconditional safety promise.
- A/B suitability: strong bodily relevance; slightly harder to keep the opening short and fact-safe. A reconstruction must not imply a sourced real accident or casualty. This is an explanation, not personalized lightning-safety advice.

### P03 — liquefaction: firm ground loses its support

- Working title: **ทำไมพื้นดินที่ดูแข็ง กลับทำให้ตึกเอียงตอนแผ่นดินไหว**.
- Central question/takeaway: a building's trouble can begin in the ground, not only the building; some loose, water-saturated sediment loses strength during strong shaking.
- Supported premise: USGS documents the conditions and damage. University of Washington's geotechnical explanation connects rapid loading, trapped pore water, reduced grain contact forces and fluid-like behavior. `F003`, SRC004–SRC005.
- Usable progression: apparently firm ground → shaking → grain-and-water close-up → support lost → building tilts even without a cinematic open chasm. Final reframe: what looked like a solid base was a load-bearing arrangement of grains.
- Hook boundary: say **behaves more like a fluid/loses strength**, not “turns into water” or “melts.” Not all ground liquefies and not every earthquake causes it. Show loose saturated sediment, not solid bedrock; exact damage depends on site and structure. A fictional site is not a hazard diagnosis of Bangkok or the user's home.
- A/B suitability: stable shared mechanism and consequence; a giant collapse would overstate the inspected evidence and distract from the intended test.

### P04 — astronaut height: getting taller without growing new bone

- Working title: **ไปอวกาศแล้วทำไมสูงขึ้น แต่กลับโลกแล้วเตี้ยลงอีก**.
- Central question/takeaway: apparent growth can be a change in spinal loading and geometry, not ordinary permanent growth.
- Supported premise: NASA reports spinal straightening/elongation and height increases **up to about 3%** in space. Its LSAH page says crew members return to normal height on Earth. `F004`, SRC006–SRC007.
- Usable progression: fictional astronaut measures taller → guess ordinary growth → spine under different loading → return to Earth → height advantage goes away. Keep bone-density loss separate from the cause of increased measured height if a later script uses it.
- Hook boundary: “up to” is not an average or a promise for every person. Do not promise a specific number of centimeters, a particular recovery time, immediate growth, or a height-improvement method. Microgravity is not an absence of Earth's gravity. No exercise or medical advice is established here.
- A/B suitability: body relevance and an easy permanent-versus-temporary flip. A future hook should not rely on an unverified extreme astronaut anecdote.

### P05 — chilli: the same fruit deters one eater and recruits another

- Working title: **ทำไมพริกเผ็ดกับเรา แต่นกกินได้หน้าตาเฉย**.
- Central question/takeaway: the familiar burn is a response of a sensory system, not a literal temperature common to every animal.
- Supported premise: the original Cell paper's abstract, hosted by its author's university, contrasts capsaicin-sensitive mammalian receptors with the normally insensitive chicken counterpart and links birds with seed dispersal. Birds still detect noxious heat. `F005`, SRC008.
- Usable progression: human reacts to chilli → bird eats → “hot fruit means hot temperature” expectation fails → different sensitivity → plant's fruit can deter some consumers while still reaching dispersers. Final contrast must be about capsaicin, not birds being immune to all pain.
- Hook boundary: no “นกไม่มีตัวรับความเจ็บ,” no “all birds are immune to everything spicy,” no pet-feeding recommendation. Do not say deterring mammals is the **only** evolutionary reason pungency exists; a later original PNAS study is a lead for a microbial-defense alternative, not inspected full text here (SRC009).
- A/B suitability: familiar object and little technical vocabulary needed. The final hook wording still needs review against the actual species/scope selected.

### P06 — flamingo: one-legged support need not be a strength contest

- Working title: **ทำไมฟลามิงโกยืนขาเดียวได้สบายกว่าที่คุณคิด**.
- Central question/takeaway: body geometry can support a stance with little active muscular force.
- Supported premise: the research-partner zoo describes the experimental positioning of cadaveric flamingos and observation of live birds; the original paper title/abstract reports passive one-leg support and reduced sway during quiescent live behavior. `F006`, SRC010–SRC011.
- Usable progression: human's one-leg wobble as a dramatized comparison → bird rests → body-weight/leg alignment → mechanical support, rather than exceptional constant muscular effort, explains the contrast.
- Hook boundary: the cadaver result required **experimental positioning**; never “dead flamingos naturally stand up.” Avoid “zero energy” as a whole-animal metabolic claim even though the zoo's popular summary uses it. This supports a mechanical explanation; it does not prove the sole evolutionary reason for all one-leg resting. Do not extrapolate to any bird, any pose or an unmeasured exact energy saving.
- A/B suitability: visually clean animal option; narrower self-insertion than the passenger/body topics. The strongest spectacular phrasing is also the largest overstatement risk.

### P07 — wombat: a soft tube can make a cube

- Working title: **ทำไมวอมแบตถ่ายเป็นก้อนเหลี่ยม ทั้งที่ลำไส้ไม่ได้เป็นกล่อง**.
- Central question/takeaway: a cube need not be made by a square opening; differing stiffness and contractions can shape material within soft tissue.
- Supported premise: the research team's university reports cube formation **inside the distal intestine**, with stiff/flexible regions, drying and muscular contractions. The study concerns bare-nosed/common wombats. `F007`, SRC012.
- Usable progression: cubes → guess square exit → cube is already formed inside → alternating tissue properties → reframe a soft intestine as a shaping process. Any “factory” comparison is an analogy, not a literal rigid mold.
- Hook boundary: do not say perfect mathematical cubes, all feces in all conditions, or that cubes' sole proven evolutionary purpose is not rolling away. The researchers explicitly leave evolutionary behavior questions open. No anatomy gag should misidentify the exit as square.
- A/B suitability: immediate unusual picture and a self-contained reveal; greater animal/novelty dependence and a risk of a diagram-heavy middle. It is a useful alternate rather than the first pilot recommendation.

### P08 — microgravity fire: gravity helps draw the familiar flame

- Working title: **ทำไมไฟในสถานีอวกาศ ถึงกลมแทนที่จะชี้ขึ้น**.
- Central question/takeaway: the familiar teardrop flame is influenced by buoyancy-driven flows; combustion itself does not require that familiar shape.
- Supported premise: NASA says low-momentum flames in station microgravity tend to be rounded or spherical because the usual hot-gas rise/cool-air inflow is absent; it documents controlled combustion chambers. The candle comparison shows a blue, rounded example. `F008`, SRC013–SRC014.
- Usable progression: familiar candle → prediction of upward flicker → controlled microgravity comparison → heat has no gravity-defined upward convection here → familiar Earth flame becomes the case needing explanation.
- Hook boundary: **inside an oxygen-containing experimental environment**, not fire burning in open vacuum. Ventilation, fuel and flow matter: not every space fire is a perfect blue sphere. Blue does not establish harmless or cold. Do not add an astronaut casually lighting a candle in the cabin as though it is real procedure.
- A/B suitability: striking matched opening images; a good visual wildcard, with appearance validation for Film before claiming runtime feasibility.

## Sound and image implications

All suggested scenes are dramatization. Lightning thunder, earthquake rumble, astronaut ambience and animal reactions would be newly designed scene sounds, not authentic recordings of a named event. A reaction must not imply a sourced casualty or a natural behavior beyond the claims above. For P08, a conventional rising flame/roar can imply a gravity/flow condition absent from the premise; Film/Ton should align sound with the controlled experiment. No library selection, audio generation, images or renders were performed.
