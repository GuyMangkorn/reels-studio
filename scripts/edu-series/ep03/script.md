## Story core

- **Logline:** An owner throws a bright red ball onto green grass and the dog runs right past it. We flip the whole painting into a simulated dog view and see why: red and green are hard for a dog to tell apart. The dog finds it anyway with its nose, and a blue ball, which a dog likely sees more clearly on grass, ends the game in one run.
- **Central question:** Why can't the dog find a bright red ball that is right in front of it?
- **Character intention / obstacle:** the dog wants the ball; its eyes cannot easily separate red from green. The owner wants to help but points at something the dog cannot pick out.
- **Intended feeling:** affectionate laughter at the overshoot, a "whoa" at the palette flip, a small myth-busting surprise (not black-and-white), a useful take-home tip.
- **Learning takeaway:** dogs are not colour-blind in the black-and-white sense. People have three kinds of colour-sensing cells in the eye, dogs have two, so red and green are hard for them to tell apart while blue and yellow are easy. Dogs also use their nose. For a game on grass, a blue toy is likely easier for a dog to see than a red one.

## Truth and ending

- **What happens in the story:** the owner throws a red ball; the dog sprints, overshoots and sniffs around while the owner points and shouts. The painting re-tints to a simulated dog view: the red ball and the grass sit much closer together, blue and yellow stay strong. A diagram shows three cell types for a person, two for a dog, and strikes out "black and white". Back in normal colour the dog noses its way to the red ball. The owner throws a blue ball; in the dog view it stands out and the dog runs straight to it. Closing question, hold, loop seam.
- **Verified (per research, pending W1.3 on this wording):** two cone types vs three (F001, supported); not black-and-white (F002, supported); red/green hard to tell apart (F003, partly supported, worded "แยกยาก"); blue and yellow seen best (F005, supported); blue on grass likely clearer than red (F006, inference, hedged "น่าจะ"); nose helps (F007, worded "ใช้จมูกช่วยด้วย").
- **Remaining uncertainty kept in VO:** hook says "อาจดูคล้ายหญ้า" (may look like grass), never "invisible" or "blends in"; L10 says "น่าจะเห็นชัดกว่า"; L09 says the nose "helps", not "mainly"; the flip carries an on-screen `ภาพจำลอง` tag.
- **Deliberately not used:** "brownish-yellow" or any named colour a dog "sees" for red/green (F004 unresolved); "แทบกลืนไปกับหญ้า"; "mainly by nose"; acuity 20/75 (F009) and low light (F010), not needed for a sunny daytime story; motion sensitivity (F008) held as optional ALT-A; any study numbers or wavelengths.
- **Twist / reveal logic:** the reversal is that the "eye-catching" red toy is the one a dog finds hard to see, and the problem is not the dog's intelligence. It is fair because B02 shows the dog looking straight at the ball area and missing it, B03 shows the cause (red and green close together in the simulated view), and B04 gives the mechanism (two cell types). The payoff (B06) only changes the ball colour, so the viewer can attribute the difference to colour.
- **Ending:** `B_loop`. L11 asks "หมาเห็นมันไหม"; the loop restarts on L01–L02, which answer it for the red ball ("…อาจดูคล้ายหญ้า มากกว่าที่คุณคิด"). After L11: hold ~3 s on the dog with the blue ball, then the shared loop seam back to the owner holding the red ball.

## Film brief → scene sheets (story constraints)

- Cast: one adult 4-head owner (two lines) and a golden retriever (kit `dog-golden` is the concept's suggestion; Film owns the casting line). Sunny park, morning light, green grass.
- **Must be seen:** the red ball clearly visible to the viewer in normal colour while the dog misses it (B02); the whole painting re-tinting to the simulated view, with the red ball much closer to the grass but **not vanished** (research: brightness still differs) and blues/yellows kept strong (B03); a simple 3-vs-2 diagram and a struck-out `ขาวดำ` (B04); the dog nosing to the red ball in normal colour (B05); the blue ball in the simulated view standing out from the grass, the dog running straight to it on the ground (B06).
- **Simulation look:** a gentle shift that reduces red-green and keeps blue-yellow (research: desaturated yellow-olive / grey-blue is more defensible); do not paint reds as confidently "brown". Tag `ภาพจำลอง (โดยประมาณ)` whenever the dog view is on screen (B03, B06).
- **Payoff staging:** the dog reaches the blue ball after it lands, not a mid-air catch (concept had a catch; changed so motion cannot explain the payoff instead of colour).
- **Must stay hidden until its beat:** the dog-view palette until L03/L04; the blue ball as the payoff until B06 (a glimpse in B03 as CL02 is allowed).
- **Ending:** hold ~3 s on the happy dog with the blue ball; shared `B_loop` seam back to the owner holding the red ball (first frame).
