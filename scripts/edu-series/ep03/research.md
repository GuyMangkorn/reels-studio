# EDU-EP03-SHORT — Research packet (Boat, W1.1)

- **Deliverable:** EDU-EP03-SHORT (Short, Thai audience, educational explainer, painted 2D, 4-head cast). Concept C5, `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md`.
- **Research date:** 2026-11-05. Story mode: `fiction-with-factual-knowledge` (scripted park scene, real canine vision science as narrator assertions).
- **Central question:** How do dogs see colour, and is a red ball hard for a dog to spot on green grass while a blue ball is easier?
- **Learning takeaway (proposed):** Dogs are not black-and-white; they have two cone types, so reds and greens are hard for them to tell apart while blues and yellows are easy. Choose a blue (or yellow) toy for grass.
- **Status:** research only. No script, no fact-check. Search mode: standard only (enough for core claims; primary full texts were mostly behind paywalls or bot checks, see gaps).

## How close can a dog-vision simulation be?

- What is documented: two cone pigments near 429 nm and 555 nm (SRC001) and behavioural red-green weakness (SRC003, SRC004 lead). A simulation can reasonably reduce the red-green axis and keep blue-yellow.
- What is not: the actual subjective percept (no one can ask the dog), exact hue names, the effect of brightness cues, individual and breed variation, and lower acuity (about 20/75, SRC003) which the colour-only filter ignores. Public simulators are modelled on human dichromat conversions.
- Production wording: call it "ภาพจำลองที่ประมาณใกล้เคียง", not "สิ่งที่หมาเห็นจริง". Avoid showing reds as clearly "brown" with confidence; a gentle desaturated yellow-olive/grey-blue shift is more defensible.
- A sharp bright red against bright green still differs in brightness, so the ball in dog vision should not vanish completely; "blends in" is dramatization.

## Supported story angles

1. Myth-bust: dogs see colour, just a different set (two cones), blue-yellow world. Solid.
2. Practical tip: for grass, a blue ball is a better choice than red/orange. Hedge as "likely easier".
3. Honest reframe of the hook: the dog does not necessarily fail because of the colour; scent and motion help, so red is "harder for the eyes" not "invisible".
4. Sensory-world framing: colour is the weaker sense; smell and motion carry the game (Beat 4), consistent with the concept.
