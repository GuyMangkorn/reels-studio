## Story core

- **Logline.** On his first day in a Roman laundry, Marcus vows to make a shirt the whitest in Rome "with these two hands". He discovers the shop washes with urine, and his hands get swapped for his bare feet in the tub. He survives the job, bluffs about a "secret recipe", and sniffs his first coin with relief. Then the story flips to a true one: Emperor Vespasian taxed urine and held the money to his complaining son's nose.
- **Central question.** How bad can Marcus's first day get, and will the shirt come out white?
- **Intention and obstacle.** Marcus wants to impress the shop with his two hands. The urine jar, the tread tub and the smell he takes home keep making things worse (ER02).
- **Feeling.** Disgusted laughter at Marcus's expense, then the "จริงดิ" of the emperor doing the same coin sniff for real.
- **Takeaway.** Roman fullers really washed cloth with urine, treading it in tubs. Emperor Vespasian really taxed urine. When his son complained, he held the coins to his son's nose: "and yet it comes from urine".

## Truth and ending

- **What happens in the story.** It covers one invented working day, morning to evening. Marcus receives the jar, treads the cloth in a tub of water mixed with urine, hands the dry white shirt to the customer in the evening and is paid one coin. The rest of the wash (including the rinse) happens off screen in the cut from B03 to the evening (R3). The story then cuts to Vespasian and Titus, an undated real episode. Marcus's day is not tied to Vespasian's reign.
- **Verified facts used.** F001 (urine as a cleaning agent in the trade), F005 (foot-treading in tubs of water and agents, hands also used), F006 (Vespasian's urine tax), F007 (the coin anecdote as Suetonius reports it). F002 (the rinse stage) is no longer narrated or shown since L11 is cut (R3); with F003 it still bounds the picture: the shirt is white only after the whole wash, never instantly.
- **Remaining uncertainty, kept off narration.** Urine was one agent among several, and its importance is disputed (SRC003). Bare feet are plausible but not stated. Nobody says who paid the tax. The anecdote is reported decades later and cannot be confirmed as an event. Dio's version differs in detail.
- **Ending.** L20 is the narrator's callback punchline, “เงินน่ะไม่มีกลิ่นหรอก… แต่มาร์คุสยังมี”, then a 2.0 s hold (ER03). The line is the narrator's joke and is not presented as Vespasian's words (F008). The ending is chosen first and every clue below feeds it.
