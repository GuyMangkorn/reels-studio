## Story core and ending

Logline: a passenger predicts that a floating balloon will lean the same way as they do; acceleration, a settled straight-speed interval and braking expose the missing character: the air.
Central question: which way does the balloon lean when the car accelerates? Intention: predict the next movement. Obstacle: the passenger treats the balloon as though surrounding air did not matter.
Feeling: playful certainty → surprise → a prediction the viewer can finally make.
Takeaway: acceleration changes the surrounding air's pressure balance; a buoyant, tethered helium balloon tends toward the car's acceleration, opposite the passenger's relative lag.
Ending chosen first: braking flips the contrast. No driving challenge, accident or claim of instant response.

## Film constraints and revision scope

- Keep front/rear readable and consistent. Establish a floating balloon tethered to a low anchor, closed windows, restrained passenger and level straight travel. The story is an observation, never a stunt invitation.
- The forward result must be visible by B02, before the air explanation. Do not reveal the braking result before B05. Film chooses camera/medium/staging; these are narrative constraints, not a timed shot sheet.
- Air graphics are labelled explanatory. No vacuum at the front, visible wall of densely packed air, huge draft, helium without inertia or free-flying balloon outrunning the car.
- L09 changes story time: allow an unspecified settled interval. No instant upright snap, exact lean angle or numerical acceleration. During braking, show the tendency rather than asserting an exact overshoot trace.
- User approval, calibrated W1.4 projection, audio and visual checks remain separate. Boat checks this revision and the matching Guide before factual readiness.
