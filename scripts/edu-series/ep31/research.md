### The case

- **F001** Date and crime: **6 Jan 1995**, McArthur Wheeler and Clifton Earl Johnson robbed two banks in the Greater Pittsburgh area, no mask or disguise (SRC001 citing SRC002, SRC005; SRC008). Name also appears as "MacArthur" and "Macarthur J." Confidence: **medium-high**. Safe wording: "ปี 1995 พิตต์สเบิร์ก ผู้ชายคนหนึ่งปล้นธนาคารสองแห่งในวันเดียว ไม่ใส่หน้ากาก".
  - **Correction to the pitch:** it was **two men**, Wheeler and Johnson. The script may focus on Wheeler but must not say he acted alone.
- **F002** The banks: (1) **Mellon Bank, Swissvale branch**, 2:47 p.m., one robber held the teller at gunpoint with a semi-automatic handgun while the other waited in line, **US$5,200** taken (SRC001 citing SRC002 and SRC005); (2) **Fidelity Savings Bank, Brighton Heights** (SRC001 citing Morris 2010). Confidence: **medium** (time, amount and gun detail via SRC001 only). Charges for the Brighton Heights robbery were **dropped** (SRC001 citing SRC003); Wheeler was sentenced for Swissvale only (F012).

### The Dunning–Kruger link

- **F013** Dunning's account (through Morris 2010 and SRC001): a brief account of the robberies was in the **1996 *World Almanac***; **David Dunning** (Cornell social psychologist) found it, then the longer Post-Gazette article (SRC003); with graduate student **Justin Kruger** he studied whether people's felt competence matches real competence. Confidence: **medium** (via Wikipedia's summary of Morris; Morris unopened). Pitch correction: it was **Dunning** who read the story; Kruger was his student. "นักจิตวิทยาสองคนอ่านเรื่องเขา" should be "นักจิตวิทยาคนหนึ่ง".
- **F014** The paper: Kruger & Dunning, JPSP 77(6):1121–1134 (1999), "Unskilled and Unaware of It: How Difficulties in Recognizing One's Own Incompetence Lead to Inflated Self-Assessments". Four studies on Cornell undergraduates (humor, grammar, logic); the bottom quartile scored at about the 12th percentile and ranked themselves about the 62nd (via SRC011). Confidence: **high** on the paper and its finding, **medium** on exact percentile figures (secondary summary). **Whether the paper itself mentions Wheeler: unverified here** (the full text could not be read). It is widely reported that the paper opens with Wheeler's story as an anecdote, from memory of the paper and several popular pages; do not rely on that until a reader opens the paper.
- **F015** Folklore vs. documented:
  - **True:** the case is credited as the **inspiration** (SRC001, SRC009, Dunning via Morris).
  - **Not true / not shown:** Wheeler was **not a subject** of the study; nobody tested his competence; the paper does not "diagnose" him. Morris and later retellings state that his confidence "proves" the effect: that is a **hypothesis**, not a finding about him.
  - **Misreading:** the effect is about **specific skills**, not "stupid people are confident" (SRC011).
  - **Dispute:** some researchers argue the pattern is largely a statistical artifact (regression to the mean plus better-than-average thinking; Gignac & Zajenkowski 2020), and Dunning disputes that (SRC011). Mention only in the fact-check file.
  - Safe wording: "นักจิตวิทยาคนหนึ่งอ่านเรื่องเขาแล้วสงสัยว่า คนที่ไม่เก่ง อาจไม่รู้ตัวด้วยซ้ำว่าไม่เก่ง เลยทำวิจัยจริงจัง ผลที่ออกมาคือ Dunning–Kruger effect". Not safe: "เขาคือเหตุผลที่โลกรู้จัก Dunning–Kruger" as proof about him, or "เขาคือคนโง่ที่สุดในโลก".
