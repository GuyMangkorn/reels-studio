# EDU-EP08-SHORT — research packet (W1.1, Boat)

- Deliverable: `EDU-EP08-SHORT` (Short, Thai, educational, concept C18 "ถอดหมวกกลางอวกาศ", `scripts/edu-series/concepts.md` `### C18`). Research date: 2026-11-08. Mode: documented-fact myth-buster plus one named real case.
- Central question: what actually happens to an unprotected person in vacuum, and does the 1966 NASA suit accident support the "saliva boils, ~15 s awake, survived" story?
- Short answer: (1) No explosion: `settled` (swelling yes, bursting no; "about double" is a dog-data figure). (2) No instant freeze: `settled`, but mouth/nose/eyes can chill and the cause is evaporation, not "cold space". (3) Awake about 10–15 s: `settled as a range, hedge as a rule`; sources span about 5 to 30 s (see F003). Say "ราว 10–15 วินาที" with "ประมาณ". (4) The incident is real and widely retold (Jim LeBlanc, NASA suit test, 1966), but every account I could open is secondary or a retelling; I did not read the primary report text (scanned PDFs, no OCR here). Date 1966 `hedge-minor` (1965 appears in NASA's own FAQ), the recovery time is inconsistent, and the saliva quote is retold, not traced to a transcript. Naming him: not cleared, keep him anonymous. (5) "Breathe out, never hold your breath": `settled` as physiology and as NASA/aerospace-medicine advice; the exact survival value for a person is untested. (6) Rescue: survival after short exposure is `hedge` (animal data plus this one accident and a few anecdotes); never promise "you would live".
- Tooling limits: web search excerpts plus fetched pages. Four NASA NTRS PDFs (CR-1223, CR-329, Bancroft and Dunn, the ebullism CliFF, UTMB 2025) were downloaded but are scanned images or compressed and no PDF/OCR tool exists here, so they are `lead-only` for content; only their NTRS record titles and dates were read. The Landis page (geoffreylandis.com/vacuum.html, the usual reference) was unreachable (connection refused); its claims come through search excerpts. PubMed and the NASA Ask an Astrophysicist page (imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov, 970603) were not fetchable; the latter appears only through a blog repost and Wikipedia's citation.

## What the evidence supports for a 40–60 s Short

- Supported spine: myth (explosion, instant freeze) -> truth (swell, slow heat loss) -> seconds of awareness (hedged) -> one real case, anonymous, told as "reported" -> breathe out, do not hold -> survival hedge.
- Not supported: "only man to survive", "blood boils", "1:1 15-second timer", "most dangerous = holding breath", full-vacuum survival promise.

## Hedges the Thai narration must carry

1. Consciousness: "ประมาณ 10–15 วินาที" and "ในหลายการทดลองกับสัตว์และอุบัติเหตุจริง" (not a promise for each person).
2. The 1966 case: "ตามรายงาน" or "เขาเล่าว่า"; anonymous; no exact repressurisation time; no "คนเดียวที่รอด".
3. Swelling: no number or "อาจบวมได้มาก"; no imagery.
4. Survival: "อาจรอด ถ้าช่วยเร็วมาก" and note it is "ยังไม่เคยทดลองกับคนเต็มรูปแบบ" in one clause; the rescue scene is fiction.
5. Breath rule: "ควรหายใจออก" and "ไม่ใช่คำแนะนำให้ลอง" if the line reads as advice.
6. Fiction label: the helmet falling off is a "what if" premise (real helmets lock).
