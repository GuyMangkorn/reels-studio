## Story core

- **Logline.** Western Australia, 1932: about twenty thousand emus are eating the wheat, the farmers call the army, and the army sends one major, two men and two machine guns. Plan A, the birds scatter; plan B, a thousand birds walk up to the dam and the gun jams; plan C, the truck cannot keep up. The minister pulls them out, they come back, and by December they are pulled out again. The emu, meanwhile, has been on Australia's coat of arms since 1912 and never left once.
- **Central question.** How does an army with machine guns end up withdrawing from birds, twice?
- **Victim, intention and obstacle.** The mission (Major Meredith's three-man unit) wants a quick clean job. Each plan ends worse: the birds will not stand still, then the gun fails on the one day they do, then the truck cannot follow (ER02). The narrator teases the plan, the gear and the ground, never the men's courage or skill (research CAT02 check).
- **Feeling.** A war-movie parody where the friend telling it is plainly on the birds' side; the "จริงดิ" that the major himself compared them to tanks; the closing irony that the bird they shot at sits on their own coat of arms.
- **Takeaway.** In 1932 Australia really did send soldiers with machine guns against emus and withdrew twice; the major said the birds could face machine guns like tanks; the emu has been on the Australian coat of arms since 1912.

## Truth and ending

- **What actually happened (pending Boat fact-check).** Troops arrived at Campion on 2 Nov 1932 (F001); Meredith with two gunners and two Lewis guns (F002); about 20,000 emus, an estimate (F003); farmers asked for army help (F004). The emus scattered out of range (F007); at a dam on 4 Nov more than 1,000 were sighted and the gun jammed after about a dozen kills (F005); a gun on a truck could not keep up and the ground was too rough to aim (F006). Defence Minister Pearce withdrew the men on 8 Nov; they returned about 12–13 Nov; Meredith was recalled 10 Dec (F001). Meredith said the birds could face machine guns "with the invulnerability of tanks" (F010, a reported spoken remark, not a line in his written report). The emu has been a supporter on the Commonwealth coat of arms since 1912 (F013).
- **Kept off narration on purpose.** Kill counts and Meredith's 986/9,860 claim (F007, F008; disputed and likely inflated); "no casualties except dignity" (F009; reported wording only); the medals remark (F012; speaker disputed); emu speed (F014); any claim that Australia "lost" or officially declared war. "Won/lost" exists only as the joke framing of L18 ("ไม่เคยถอยสักรอบ"); the true bold fact is two withdrawals (L01, L13, L16).
- **Joke lines that must stay jokes.** "สองสัปดาห์ก็จบมั้ง" (L05) is the narrator's own guess, not a quote from Meredith or anyone, and never appears on a title card or as a caption attributed to a person. "เต็มกำลัง" (L04) is irony against a three-man list.
- **Ending.** L18 punchline "วันนี้ก็ยังอยู่… ไม่เคยถอยสักรอบ", then a 2.0 s hold (ER03). It calls back the hook's count (CL02): the army withdrew twice; the emu on the coat of arms has not withdrawn once. L17 says "มาตั้งแต่ปี 1912" so nobody hears that the emu got onto the arms because of the war (F013 must-fix honoured: "ยังอยู่", never "ได้ขึ้นตรา"). The ending was chosen first; CL01–CL02 feed it.
