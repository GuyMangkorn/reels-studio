## (b) Government guidance on depicting the arms

ARMS-S2 (April 2014) is written for Commonwealth use and third-party permission; it does not mention video, animation, drama or news-style depiction of the arms as a building feature. Statements below are quoted or closely paraphrased from it.

| ID | What it says | Section | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AC10 | The arms are "the formal symbol of the Commonwealth of Australia that signifies Commonwealth authority and ownership"; authorised users are departments and agencies, authorities, Parliament, Commonwealth courts and tribunals; MPs and senators in their duties; currency and honours insignia. A non-government channel is not an authorised user. | 1.1, 3.1 | High (edition currency medium) |
| AC11 | Use "without permission may breach the Competition and Consumer Act 2010, the Trade Marks Act 1995 or the Criminal Code Act 1995." Requests for uses outside the authorised list go to the Honours, Symbols and Territories Branch (governmentbranding@pmc.gov.au). | 4, 1.2 | High (statutes named by PM&C; I did not read the Acts) |
| AC12 | Dignity rules: not with political logos; dignity not compromised by adjacent signs or features; not "as decorative or artistic element"; not "overprinted with words or images"; not as a watermark; conventional and stylised versions single colour only (the colour version is a separate third form). | 3.1–3.3 | High |
| AC13 | Educational use: "Publishers may seek permission to reproduce the 1908 and 1912 Arms in educational publications and media." Considered case by case; the text and illustrations accompanying the arms are sighted first; "no material should be placed above or near the Arms in a way which diminishes its dignity." | 6 | High |
| AC14 | Endorsement: the arms "must not be produced on the cover or dust jacket of a book or shown in a way which might lead a reader to believe that the material is of Australian Government origin or has Australian Government endorsement." This is written for educational publications but states the principle. | 6 | High |
| AC15 | Pre-existing arms that form part of a building's fabric may be retained on a building by occupants; the section covers government buildings, not depiction of them. | 3.5 | Medium (not on point) |
| AC16 | Importing goods bearing the arms is prohibited under the Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations 1956. Not relevant to a video; noted so nobody proposes merchandise. | 4 | High |

## Findings for Film's decision (Q2)

1. **Supporter sides: confirmed** (AC03). Keep kangaroo viewer's left, emu viewer's right. Optional: a flat mirror of the relief by mistake would invert this; flag in the build ticket.
2. **Simplified relief versus close copy.** The guidelines do not say a simplified painted rendition is allowed or forbidden for a video; they regulate faithful reproduction and ask that permission be sought for educational media. A relief carved on a sandstone building, seen as part of a scene, is the least risky depiction I can see (AC15 principle, low confidence it is covered); an accurate flat reproduction of the colour version used as a graphic is the riskiest. A simplified, stylised relief is not "accurate", so it may be less likely to read as an official reproduction, but it can also read as an altered arms. Neither option removes the AC11 notice. Confidence in this paragraph: low (inference).
3. **Safest route (optional, Film and the user decide):** (i) write to governmentbranding@pmc.gov.au with B07's description before publishing (answer unknown; the 2014 text says requests are sighted with accompanying text and pictures); or (ii) show the arms for the shortest time at the most distance as a place-identification beat, as a painted building relief, nothing overlaid, plus the label from frame 0 (already in the brief) which says the images are simulated. Item (ii) lowers the endorsement risk but does not make the use authorised.
4. **Must-fix or optional:** the permission question is a legal/policy risk, not a script factual error. I am marking it **optional for Film** and **a decision for the user** (whether to ask PM&C before posting). I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.

## Related finding for the brief (must-fix for the picture, not for the script)

- **BR-FLAG: 1908 versus 1912 arms.** Script L17 (approved) says 1908, and B07's rationale says "The emblem was already there (since 1908)". VD30-04 draws the **1912** arms (six-quarter shield, seven-point star, wattle). The arms on a wall today are the 1912 design; the 1908 design had a different shield and the animals stood on a grassy mound (AC05). The line "since 1908" is true for the kangaroo-and-emu arms as a grant of arms (the 1908 grant was the first), and the emblem on the wall today is a descendant; but a viewer who sees a 1912 shield and hears "1908" is not misled about the takeaway (the kangaroo and emu supporters have been there since 1908, and no fact-check finding contradicts that). I rate this **optional**: keep L17, and have Film note in B07 that the pictured relief is the current (1912) design, or Preaw adjust the caption to "ตั้งแต่ 1908". Preaw's open question 3 (script.md still says 1912 in places) is a related housekeeping item; the 1912 date is correct for the design pictured.
