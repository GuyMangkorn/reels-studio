## Claim ledger

Confidence is qualitative. "Safe wording" is Thai, bold per ER10 with at most one hedge.

### Phases and dates

- **F001** Operation timeline: troops arrived at Campion on **2 Nov 1932**; first action that day against about 50 emus (SRC001 [1], SRC002). On **8 Nov** Defence Minister Sir George Pearce withdrew the men and guns after critical press; on about **12–13 Nov** operations resumed after the Minister approved renewal (SRC001 [4][7][16][17]); Meredith was **recalled 10 Dec 1932** (SRC001 [1]). Contrary: 10 Dec is the recall date; one source writes "8 December", which SRC001 notes is wrong. Confidence: **medium-high** (several secondary sources agree, no primary opened).
  Safe wording: "พฤศจิกายน 1932 กองทัพออสเตรเลียส่งทหารไปที่แคมเปียน ทางตะวันตกของประเทศ" / "ถอนทัพได้แค่ไม่กี่วัน แล้วก็ต้องกลับมาอีกรอบ" / "ถอนทัพจริงสองรอบ ถึงธันวาคมก็จบ".
  Warning: "กองทัพถอนทัพ" is true for both phases but the second phase was said by Meredith to be more productive (F008). Do not say the army left once and never returned.
- **F002** Unit and weapons: Major Gwynydd Purves Wynne-Aubrey Meredith ("G. P. W."), Royal Australian Artillery, 7th Heavy Battery; with Sergeant S. McMurray and Gunner J. O'Halloran; **two Lewis guns, 10,000 rounds** (SRC001 [1][7][9][10], SRC002). Confidence: **medium-high**. The pitch's "two soldiers" is **right**: three men total (Meredith plus two). Safe wording: "ทหารปืนใหญ่สองนายกับผู้บังคับการ ปืนกลสองกระบอก กระสุนหนึ่งหมื่นนัด". Note: the pitch says "two soldiers"; SRC001 names two men besides Meredith, so "แมเจอร์ กับลูกน้องสองคน" is safe. Other popular pages say "a few soldiers"; do not state a larger troop count.
  Thai term: Lewis gun is a light machine gun / "ปืนกลเบา". "ปืนกล" is fine for voice-over.

### Why the farmers asked

- **F003** About **20,000 emus** in the area (Gill 2007 via SRC001 [3]; SRC002 "at least 20,000"). Estimate, never counted. Confidence: **medium; estimate**. Safe wording: "ราว ๆ สองหมื่นตัว" (the hedge carries it). The pitch says "about 20,000": fine.
- **F004** Why: emus moved in from the interior looking for water and trampled and ate wheat (SRC002). Farmers, many of them ex-soldiers settled after WWI, petitioned Defence Minister Pearce for army help (SRC002; Johnson 2006 title "soldier settlers"). Confidence: **medium-high** for the ex-soldier detail, medium for the exact causes. Safe wording: "ชาวไร่ส่วนใหญ่เป็นทหารผ่านศึก นกอีมูแห่กันมากินข้าวสาลี ชาวไร่เลยขอกองทัพมาช่วย". Do not state a wheat price or a drought as the cause without a source (one site mentions an unkept 1929 wheat subsidy promise; not verified).

### Events

- **F005** Dam ambush: **4 Nov 1932**, more than 1,000 emus sighted near a dam; the gun jammed after about **12 birds** were killed (SRC001 [9] = Burton 2013). The pitch's "jams after a few rounds" is slightly off: the jam came after a dozen kills, not "a few rounds". Another summary says the first-day gun jam came after a few rounds; accounts differ on which day the jam happened. Confidence: **medium**. Safe wording: "เจอนกเป็นพันตัวที่เขื่อน ปืนกลติดตอนยิงไปได้แค่สิบกว่าตัว". Avoid "ยิงไปไม่กี่นัด".
- **F006** Truck attempt: Meredith mounted a Lewis gun on a truck; the truck could not keep pace with the emus and the ride on rough ground was too bumpy for the gunner to fire (SRC001 [1]). Confidence: **medium-high**. Safe wording: "เอาปืนกลไปติดรถบรรทุก รถวิ่งตามนกไม่ทัน พื้นก็สะเทือนจนมือปืนยิงไม่ได้". Do not say "นกวิ่งชนะรถ" as a race; "ตามไม่ทัน" is the source. Emu speed: about 48–50 km/h in general sources (SRC011); rough ground, not a speed contest, is the documented reason. Do not state a speed as a measured number of the 1932 emus.
- **F007** First phase: scatter. Emus split into small groups and ran out of range (SRC002 and others). By 8 Nov **2,500 rounds** were fired with only **about 50 to 200–500 kills** by various estimates (50 per one source, 200–500 from settlers; Australian Geographic says "50 to 200"). **Disputed**; do not give a single kill count for phase one. Confidence: **low-medium** for any number; "แยกฝูง" scatter is **high**. Safe wording: "ยิงไปสองพันห้าร้อยนัด ตายไม่กี่สิบถึงไม่กี่ร้อยตัว ตามแต่ใครนับ" or simply "กระสุนไปเป็นพันนัด นกตายเป็นหลักสิบ".
- **F008** Second phase and total: Meredith claimed **986 confirmed kills from 9,860 rounds** (exactly 10 rounds per bird; the 9,860 and "about 10 per emu" figure come from his report as quoted by SRC001 [1]) plus about 2,500 wounded that he said later died (SRC001). Historian Greg Beyer calls the figure "highly disputed" and "likely to be very inflated" (SRC001 [19]). Confidence: **medium that Meredith reported the figures**, **low that the figures are accurate**. The "10 bullets per emu" is Meredith's claim of the ratio, not a measured fact. Safe wording: "ในรายงาน ผู้บังคับการเคลมว่าใช้กระสุนราวหมื่นนัด ได้นกประมาณพันตัว คือประมาณสิบนัดต่อหนึ่งตัว" (attribute to the report; the report claim is bold and true). The "ability" to say "ทหารชนะ 986 ตัว" is only the report's number.
  Pitch's "withdraws" is true but the second round makes "แพ้" a framing: Meredith's own report said the second phase worked better, and the general view is that the operation had minimal effect on emu numbers and farmers got no lasting relief. The bounty system (from 1923; 57,034 bounties claimed in six months of 1934 per SRC001 [7]) worked better than soldiers. Safe wording for the ending: "สุดท้ายค่าหัวนกได้ผลกว่ากองทัพ" is sourced (SRC002) but not needed.
- **F009** Casualty line: Meredith's report said his men had suffered **no casualties "except for their dignity"** (SRC001 [1], a quote from Johnson 2006 as repeated; wording is a rendering). Confidence: **medium** on exact wording; the source wording in English is as quoted in secondary texts, not checked in the NAA file. Use it as paraphrase: "ทหารไม่มีใครบาดเจ็บ นอกจากศักดิ์ศรี". Direct quote status: **reported, unverified wording**; mark as "ตามรายงาน".

### The "tanks" quote

- **F010** Wording as found: "They can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks" (SRC001 [13], SRC002) and, per the SRC009 note citing the *Western Mail* 10 Nov 1932, "They could face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks." Wikipedia cites it to the *Sunday Herald* (Sydney) **5 Jul 1953 p.13** (a retrospective). SRC009 says Meredith meant it as a general description of emu tenacity, not about the thousand-emu fight, and it was a **statement to the press, not a line in the written report**. Confidence: **medium that Meredith said it** (several chains, three Trove leads, none opened), **medium-low on exact tense and venue**. Direct quote: **yes, a reported spoken statement, original English**, tense differs between sources. The pitch frames it as "Meredith's own report praises the enemy". **That framing is not supported.** The safe frame: "ผู้บังคับการเองบอกสื่อว่านกพวกนี้ ... ด้วยความอยู่ยงคงกระพันของรถถัง". Safe wording: "แมเจอร์เมอร์เรดิธเองยังพูดว่า นกพวกนี้ทนปืนกลได้อย่างกับรถถัง". Do not say "เขียนในรายงานของกองทัพ" and do not present it as a lament after losing. Severity if used as "his report": **must-fix** under ER11 (a wrong source for the wow line); safe to say "พูดกับหนังสือพิมพ์".

### Parliament and medals

- **F011** Ministerial: Defence Minister Sir George Pearce ordered the operation and the withdrawal on 8 Nov (SRC001 [4][7][8]); Pearce defended the use of the army in the Senate (secondary, search summary, not checked). Senator James Dunn later called him the "Minister of the Emu War" in parliament (SRC001 [8]; SRC002 says he "unofficially earned" the title). Confidence: **medium**. Hansard was not opened; the Senate 9 Nov 1932 page on historichansard.net was only partly read and shows no emu text in its first half (chars 0 to 100,000 of about 193,000).
- **F012** Medals: an MP asked in the House whether a medal would be struck for the soldiers, and a suggestion followed that the medals should go to the emus. Speaker and exact words are **disputed**. SRC001 [18] (*West Australian* 9 Nov 1932 "Questions in Representatives") names **Rowley James** (Labor, Hunter, NSW) as the asker, addressing PM Lyons. SRC002 names **A. E. Green** (federal Labor) and gives the quip "won every round so far". Another popular site gives no name. The remark was about 8–9 Nov 1932, probably during the first withdrawal. Confidence: **medium that a medal joke was made in the House, low on who**. The pitch fallback "a politician asked whether medals would be struck, and if so, whether they should go to the emus" is **safe in the anonymous form**. Safe wording: "มี ส.ส. คนหนึ่งในสภาถามประชด ว่าจะมีเหรียญให้ทหารไหม แล้วเหรียญควรไปอยู่กับนกรึเปล่า". Do not put a name or the exact "won every round" line in anyone's mouth without Hansard or Trove. Do not say Pearce or the PM answered; not shown.

### The coat of arms ending

- **F013** The emu is one of the two supporters (with the kangaroo) of the Australian Commonwealth coat of arms; the arms were granted by King George V on 19 Sept 1912 (SRC010). It was there 20 years **before** the Emu War. Confidence: **high** (well documented). Safe wording: "นกอีมูอยู่บนตราแผ่นดินของออสเตรเลียมาตั้งแต่ปี 1912". The pitch's ending "ทุกวันนี้นกอีมูยังอยู่บนตราแผ่นดิน… คนชนะสงครามก็ควรได้อยู่ตรงนั้น" is a joke and fine as a joke, but a line implying the emu got the arms **because** of the war would be false (**must-fix**). Keep it as "ยังอยู่" (still there), never "ได้ขึ้นตรา".
- **F014** Emu top speed about 48–50 km/h (general reference; SRC011 not primary). Confidence: **medium**. Use only with the hedge "เกือบห้าสิบกิโลเมตรต่อชั่วโมง" or leave out.

### Other pitch statements

- "Australia lost" is a joke framing; sources disagree on how to score it. Facts: withdrawn twice, small effect on emu numbers, no soldier casualties (F008, F009).
