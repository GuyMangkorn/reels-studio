## Story core

- **Logline:** imagine rain with no air: the sky goes silent, a drop leaves a cloud up to two kilometres high and never stops speeding up; after one kilometre it is past 500, and it hits you at about 700 km/h; the big ones would sting like a BB-gun shot. In the real world the air pushes back harder the faster the drop falls, until the push matches its weight and it stops speeding up: even the biggest drops land at only thirty-something km/h. The same push shapes the drop: it is not a teardrop but a bun, and a bigger drop is hollowed like an upside-down bag and bursts at about half a centimetre. Every drop on your hand was held back by the air.
- **Central question:** if a raindrop falls from up to two kilometres, why does rain only pat your hand?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the drop "wants" to keep speeding up; the obstacle is the air it cannot see. In the no-air sequence there is no obstacle, which is the threat ("มันเร่งไม่หยุด", L02b, answered by "มันก็เลิกเร่ง", L06).
- **Feeling:** an eerie hush; dread as the gauge passes 500; a jolt at 700 and the "โอ๊ย"; relief as colour and rain sound return and the needle stalls; a quick surprise (not a teardrop); curiosity at "why no ball-sized drop"; calm warmth at the hand in the rain.
- **Takeaway:** the faster something falls through air, the harder the air pushes back, so a raindrop stops speeding up at a gentle speed; the same push flattens big drops and bursts them, so raindrops stay small (on-screen text: "ส่วนใหญ่ไม่เกินราวครึ่งเซน"; 8.8 mm was recorded once, not spoken). Raindrops are round or bun-shaped, not tear-shaped.
- **The one rule (ER03 refrain):** "ยิ่งตกเร็ว… อากาศยิ่งดันกลับแรง". B01 shows the world without it ("เร่งไม่หยุด"); B02 it is the brake (L05–L06); B03 the same push shapes the drop (L11 "อากาศดัน…"); B04 it bursts big drops (L12 "อากาศยิ่งดัน…"); B05 the refrain returns (L14) and flips into the reason rain is gentle on your hand (L15).
