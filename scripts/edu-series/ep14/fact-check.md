## Answers to the script questions

| Q | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| 1. CLN1 (L02a) | **Supported; keep C1 uncut for fact reasons.** Sound is a mechanical wave and needs a medium such as air (Smithsonian "How does sound travel through space", inspected: "Sound can't be carried in the empty vacuum of space"). Wind is moving air, so "ไม่มีลม" is true by definition. "ฟ้าเงียบสนิท" is true for the sky. Two limits: (1) FIX-1, the script breaks its own rule at V01. (2) "ไม่มีเสียง" is about the air-borne sound the viewer would hear; vibration can still travel through solids (the ground, her body), a distinction that does not matter for a Short and needs no qualification (ER07). Whether to cut C1 for time is a W1.4 call, not a fact call. |
| 2. L02c | **Fair.** v = sqrt(2gh): 1 km gives 140 m/s = 504 km/h, so "ห้าร้อยกว่า" is true; it depends only on the 1 km fallen, not on the cloud height, so it holds with L02's "ได้ถึงราวสอง". Inside "สมมติว่า" and the gauge reads "แบบจำลอง". Keep. |
| 3. L02b | **Not an overstatement in the model.** With nothing pushing back, the pull is constant and the speed rises all the way to the ground; "ไม่หยุด" means it never stops speeding up, and it does hit the ground first, which the viewer understands. It sets up L06 "เลิกเร่ง". Keep. |
| 4. L13 | **Fair.** SRC009 (abstract and Science News, lead-only): 6 mm drops break into fragments of about 1 to just under 4 mm; drops of 6 mm or larger likely shatter before the ground. UW release (inspected): in the lab drops typically split at about 5 mm, and air rushing through the thin top film splits them. "ราวครึ่งเซนกว่า ๆ… ส่วนใหญ่ก็แตกเป็นเม็ดเล็ก ๆ" is a range with "ส่วนใหญ่" and no "never". "Before the ground" in the picture is supported for 6 mm and above; the ruler reads "~5–6 มม." so the burst-before-ground picture is slightly strong at 5 mm. Acceptable under "ส่วนใหญ่"; Film may show the burst at the 6 mm end of the ruler. |
| 5. Frozen cloud | **Enough.** Chain of cover: the spoken "สมมติว่า" (L01), the "ภาพจำลอง" label from L01 through V01, stillness stated in the script as stylised staging, and no boiling, freezing or dissolving shown (CL04 note: in a true vacuum water boils and freezes and clouds would not form, so the world is a thought experiment). | 7. "โอ๊ย" sting | **Fair for the reaction, with FIX-1 for the sound.** Own arithmetic re-done: 5 mm drop mass 0.065 g, 198 m/s, E = 0.5 x 6.5e-5 x 198² = 1.28 J. A 0.20 g airsoft BB at 100 m/s is 1.0 J (energy formula, airsoft calculator pages via search; many countries cap airsoft at 1 J). So 1.3 J is airsoft-BB energy, and a BB at that energy is known to sting; a water drop spreads out on impact so the sting may be a little softer, which "น่าจะแสบ" covers. A real air-rifle pellet (several joules) is stronger, so "ลูกปืนอัดลม" is fair only for the weak end and "น่าจะ" + "เม็ดใหญ่ ๆ" must stay. The 5 mm drawn drop is a realistic size for the biggest drop, so "oversized" is only mild exaggeration. The reaction "โอ๊ย" = sting is fine; the problem is the sound, FIX-1. |
| 8. "สามสิบกว่า" | **Keep.** 9 m/s = 32.4 km/h. Inspected: an AMS/Copernicus ASR paper (2021) gives a 3 mm drop at close to 8 m/s (28.8 km/h); the UW release and search summaries say the maximum is 9 to 10 m/s. | 10. "ก้นยิ่งบุ๋ม… ถุงคว่ำ" | **Confirmed; the script text is "ยิ่งใหญ่ ก้นยิ่งบุ๋มเหมือนถุงคว่ำ" (no "เข้าไป"); either wording is true.** USGS (inspected): a larger drop turns parachute-like, then a bag, then breaks up; NASA (inspected): round on top, flat below, parachute shape above about 4.5 mm.
