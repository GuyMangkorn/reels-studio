## Visual intent

- **Audience knowledge and mood.** Every Thai viewer has waited out a downpour under a bus-stop roof; nobody thinks of the air as the thing holding the rain back. The first frame must say *outside, raining hard, stuck under a shelter* with no words (UT01). Feelings by beat: the ordinary wet afternoon → an eerie hush when the air goes (colour drains, sound dies) → awe at the height (2 km) → dread as the gauge climbs and the city rushes up → a held-breath freeze over her arm → a jolt ("โอ๊ย") → relief as colour and the rain roar return and the needle stalls → a small surprise (not a teardrop; the teardrops were on the roof edge all along) → curiosity (why no ball-sized drop) → satisfaction at the burst → calm warmth as the rain pats her palm and she laughs.
- **Story first (designed before the kit was opened).** GIRL is about 9, a Thai primary schoolgirl waiting for the bus home after school, around 16:30 in the rainy season, with no umbrella: that is why she is stuck under the shelter and why, at the end, she dares to put her hand out. Her uniform is the Thai one (white short-sleeve blouse with a name-badge patch, navy pleated skirt, black shoes, white socks) with a red school backpack that matches her hair ties. She starts bored, standing right at the front edge of the shelter, one hand shading her eyes as she checks the sky, so her forearm is just past the drip line (this is what lets the no-air drop reach her). The **shelter** is a plain Bangkok bus stop: green-painted steel posts, a sloping corrugated tin roof whose front lip drips, a steel bench, a textless backlit ad panel, a route-sign pole, a green city bin; behind it her school's wall and rain trees; in front a wet two-lane road and, across it, a row of four-storey shophouses with half-open shutters, a food cart under a striped tarp and concrete poles with tangled cables. The **no-air world** is the same place with the colour and every motion drained out: no rain in the air, no wind, puddles like glass, the drops on the lip frozen; only the hero drop moves. The **sky** above is a flat, heavy nimbostratus base. The **drop** is the star: clear water with a cold cyan rim and the street refracted upside-down inside it; in the no-air world a perfect sphere, in the real world round when small, then a bun, an upside-down bag and a burst. The **macro world** is the air just in front of the shelter at millimetre scale, the bus stop behind it dissolved into soft colour shapes (her white blouse, the green posts, the cool lightbox, warm shop lights).
- **Medium.** Painted 2D p5.brush through the kit yaw camera (`ycam.ts`, `cam-moves.ts`) in world units for every set: the bus stop, the sky column and the city in metres, the cloud and drop macros in millimetres, the palm in the kit hand-macro centimetres. Characters are 4-head kit rig cards squared to the lens. Screen layers (gauges, labels, arrows, icons, bracket, ring marker, ruler, captions, channel mark) are Remotion DOM above the painted canvas, the series practice. No footage, no photos, no Three.js (Blender deactivated).

- **Light.** Real world: overcast downpour at about 16:30, soft top light from the whole sky, no hard shadows, soft contact shading under feet and props; the ad lightbox behind her gives a cool rim on her back and shoulders; shop interiors across the road glow warm and reflect in the wet road. No-air world: the same light, drained and flattened, nothing moves. Macro: the drop lit from the bright sky above (white rim on top, darker bottom), warm bokeh from the shops. End: the same set, the rain a little softer and the grade +3 % warmer over the last two shots (the storm easing with her mood, never a sunbeam).
- **Camera language.** B01 begins grounded and observational (a pull-back from the dripping lip to the girl), then becomes vertical and floating for the no-air sequence: a slow crane up to the frozen cloud, a millimetre close at the cloud base, a long-lens elevation, a fall beside the drop, a straight-down drop-eye rush and a bullet-time arc. These are the "longer sci-fi feel" the user asked for: slow, smooth, near-silent moves, except the drop-eye rush. B02 repeats the fall beside the drop in colour so the difference is the drop's behaviour, not the camera. B03–B04 are steady macro follows (the camera falls with the drop, so it holds still in frame while the world streaks up). B05 returns to the bus stop, close (UT13), warm and slow.
- **Conceal and reveal.** K01 (the teardrop-shaped drops hanging from the lip) is the first thing seen and reads as scenery ("that is what raindrops look like"); paid in B03 when the cut returns to the same lip. The gauge (K02) teases 700 in the hook, then really climbs through B01, stalls in B02 and splits in L07. The drop's real shape is held back until B03: in B02 the falling drop is framed small with a ring marker, so its shape is not readable before L10.
- **Truth limits on picture (binding on every shot).** "ภาพจำลอง" in the label slot from the no-air hit in B01-SH01 through V01 (B01-SH09) and on every macro diagram shot (B03-SH01, B03-SH03, B04-SH02, B04-SH03). "แบบจำลอง" on the no-air dial every time it shows a reading (hook tease, L02b, L02c, W700). Nothing is damaged anywhere: no leaf, roof, car, skin mark or wound; the no-air drop only stings her forearm (a small flash, she rubs it). No train, no ratio. A falling drop is never drawn tear-shaped; the only teardrops are the pendant drops on the lip (and the crossed-out icon). The no-air drop is a perfect sphere (no air, nothing to flatten it). Real speeds: needle A just over 30, needle B lower with no digits; the rider is picture only, never labelled. No "terminal velocity" or "แรงต้านอากาศ" anywhere. Never show what water does in a vacuum, cloud formation or fall times. The ruler is a true 5 mm at the drop's plane.

## Sound direction

Following the script's [sound intent](script.md#sound-intent-english-notes-narration-unchanged) (its B01–B05 rows are the base); each cue below was designed from the shot first, and a library ID is named only where its description fits ([fit beats reuse](../../../docs/sound-generation-workflow.md#fit-beats-reuse)); Ton makes every NEEDS-GEN item, Editor sets levels and places exactly the cue sheet at W5.3. Sound never carries a clue: K01 and K02 are visual. Codes A, M, D, R, S and E are used in the storyboard's sound lines. Prompt fields: source / action / material / space / length / feeling.

- **Music family and arc.** Two families on purpose. **B01 has no music, only a score of tones** (cold drone, plucks, riser): the no-air world must feel like a different physics, not a different song. **B02–B05** use a warm, light explainer bed in a "rain" colour (kalimba and marimba, soft pizzicato bass), entering on the colour cut. Energy (of 5): rain bed 2 → hit to 0 → 0.5 (thin drone) → 0 (L02a near silence) → 1 (pluck) → 2 → 4 (riser through L02b–L02c) → 5 at W700 → 0 (0.3 s dead silence) → 1 (bullet time) → jolt (V01) → **B02 relief** 2 (bed enters, rain back) → 1.5 at the stall → 2 (L07) → B03 1.5 (thin, airy macro) → B04 2 (pizzicato curiosity) → 2.5 at the burst → B05 2 → 2.5 lift on L14 → the closing tag → rain only, fading to zero.
