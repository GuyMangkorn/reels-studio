# POOLDEPTH topic brief (coordinator, rev R1)

**Working title:** สระน้ำตื้นแค่ไหน ทำไมตาเราโกหก

**Question:** Why does a pool (or any clear water) look shallower than it really is, and why does that matter for safety?

**Core idea:** Light bends (refracts) when it leaves water into air, so the brain places the pool floor higher than it is. Looking straight down, apparent depth is about real depth divided by water's refractive index (~1.33), i.e. roughly 3/4 of the real depth; at oblique angles it looks even shallower. Payoff: step in carefully, never judge depth by eye, check the depth markers.

**Why this topic:** one idea, one visual demonstration (coin/pool floor, straw, fish), strong Thai everyday relevance, a concrete takeaway, checkable with standard optics sources. Not covered by CATBOX.

**Beat sketch for Preaw (suggestion, not instruction):** hook (a child jumps toward "knee-deep" water) → what the eye assumes (light travels straight) → what actually happens (bends at the surface) → numbers (1.33, about 3/4) → why angle makes it worse → takeaway/loop back to the hook.
