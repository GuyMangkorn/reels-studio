## Story core

- **Logline:** A child is about to jump into water that looks knee-deep. Light bending at the surface lifts the image of the floor, so the water is deeper than it looks; do not judge depth by eye.
- **Central question:** Water looks knee-deep. Is it?
- **Intention / obstacle:** The viewer wants to know whether what they see can be trusted. Obstacle: the eye assumes light travels straight, but it bends at the surface.
- **Intended feeling:** a small "oh!" at the coin demo, then calm caution.
- **Learning takeaway:** (1) light bends leaving water, so the floor's image is raised; looking straight down the water looks about 3/4 of its real depth, and more shallow when viewed obliquely; (2) so check depth markers, enter feet first the first time, and never dive into shallow, unknown-depth or no-diving-sign water.

## Truth and ending

- Optics: documented fact with stated conditions (F001, F002, F003, F004).
- Safety lines: guidance from safety organizations, stated as advice (F006). The script does **not** say refraction causes injury or drowning (F008, F010 unsupported) and does **not** say everyone always perceives pools as shallower (F009 open).
- Hook child: dramatization, not a real event or statistic. Labeled on screen.
- Ending `B_loop`: the final line is an unfinished question that the first line completes ("...แล้วน้ำตรงหน้าคุณตอนนี้" / "ดูตื้นแค่เข่า จริงเหรอ?"). The payoff (safety advice) lands before the loop line.
- Not used on purpose: the 95% / 1.5 m injury statistic (F007; would need the Parachute source named, omitted to keep the Short compact); the "ตาเราโกหก" framing; the KiwiCo vanishing-coin trick; the lifeguard glare finding; any exact oblique number (0.63, 0.50).
