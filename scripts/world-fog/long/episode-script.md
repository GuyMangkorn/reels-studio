## Story core

**Logline:** Stranded by a worldwide fog in a petrol-station minimart, a woman learns that the delivery worker she let in was human, and that something outside has learned to wear his face, his voice and his cart. It cannot come in unless someone opens the door, and every time light touches its face, it comes closer.

**Central question:** Which delivery worker is the real one, and will anyone open the door? (EP1 question "what followed me?" is answered first, inside the retelling.)

**Intention / obstacle:** The narrator wants to get home, then to stay alive until the thing leaves. Obstacles: zero visibility, a door that the others want to open, darkness after the power fails, and the rule that the only tool for seeing (light) is the thing that brings it closer.

**Feeling:** EP1 dread → relief (it was a man) → cold return of dread (the second knock) → claustrophobic darkness and suspicion → recognition → the smile (peak) → exhausted relief → a new, wider dread (EP2 hook).

**Learning takeaway:** none (profile variant: pure fiction horror, no moral, no lesson, no card).

## Truth and ending

**Underlying truth (within the story):**

1. EP1 is unchanged: the roadside shape and the follower at the glass were always the ordinary delivery worker with his overloaded cart (cartons + helmet = high tilted "head"; umbrella handle = long reach; jammed front caster = squeak/scrape; helmet slips and is caught). He is human and stays human for the whole episode.
2. Something walked out of the fog near the road collision. It heard the worker shout and copied his voice back to him; it followed him toward the shop at a distance in the dark fog, unseen, and did not approach the lit shop until C04. It is the **imposter** (demon rules below). It never gets inside.
3. Its copy is a disguise: the body and face are its own (solid, it can knock and smear misted glass), but what it "carries" — cart, cartons, helmet, umbrella — is part of the disguise: it copies the outline whole but never damage, wear or weight, so the copy is too perfect, weightless and silent. Its cart wheels are all straight (the real bent caster is not copied), glide silently, leave no track on the wet forecourt, its cartons never sway, and its feet leave no prints.
4. The power failure is not explained and not caused by the imposter on screen; it is circumstance.
5. Layout: the storeroom is a small room behind the counter; the shop's exterior back door (knob + deadbolt) is inside it. The worker went into the dark storeroom only to lock that back door. He opened nothing. Every voice and knock "at the back" is outside the back door, heard through the storeroom.
6. **Ending:** the narrator proves the inside worker is real (squeak, crooked wheel, black scuff line), reads the silent straight wheels outside with a low beam that avoids its face, and says it aloud. Recognized, it drops the disguise in her beam (impossibly wide smile, hollow black eyes), and because it put its face into her light it lunges along the beam to the door gap. Torch off at that instant freezes it at the threshold (no advance in the dark); the door is slammed and bolted in front of it, nobody answers — not even to its imitation of the narrator's own voice. In the dark it can only move away; the knocks circle, weaken and stop. When the backup light comes on, the glass is empty except a long curved smear in the condensation. Episode threat resolved.
7. **EP2 hook (concept C seed, unconfirmed):** the backup light dies; knocks start from the front, the side and the back at once; a woman's voice at the front says their cars crashed on the main road, and a man's voice outside the back door asks to be let in. The worker remembers that nobody at the collision moved. Nothing is explained. Hard cut.

**Remaining uncertainty (deliberate):** what the imposter is, where it came from, whether the collision people are imposters, victims or something else, why the power failed, the fog's cause, toxicity and duration.
