## Preaw writing handoff

1. Preserve first-person limited perception: “ผมเห็นเงา…” / “มันดูเหมือน…” can describe a frightened narrator's impression. A terrified character may call it a creature; the omniscient narration or objective camera must not certify a nonhuman body and then erase it.
2. Keep the tall shape caused by stacked cargo and helmet. The worker must remain visible as a human after reveal; no last-frame monster or secretly nonhuman worker. A tilting helmet can be caught; a literally detached human head would contradict the reveal.
3. Keep the character's arrival chronological and ordinary. The initial approach occurs while a safe path is still visible; the near-zero fog then traps the parked narrator. Do not turn speeding through opaque fog into a successful escape technique.
4. The worker's voice and light reveal lead to recognition; entry is a character action, not an endorsed unknown-air safety protocol. Avoid dialogue declaring the outside safe merely because the visitor is human. Do not add smell/breath-holding/cloth-mask “tests” for toxicity.
5. End after the worker reports the blocked road and fog swallows the forecourt lights. No mandatory lesson, “fear is the real monster” slogan, fog-science explanation, casualty claim or EP2 plot production.
6. No psychology/generalization claim such as “everyone panics in crises” or “the brain always invents monsters” is needed. Show these fictional people misreading these fictional clues instead. ## K001 addendum (2026-10-30)

- **Scope:** the one first-person perception line in S03-B02 of `WFOG-SCRIPT-R3`, approved by the user in chat on 2026-10-30 (see [delivery](delivery.md) handoff log). The S04-B01 motive and the S04-B02 closing line are checked in [WFOG-FACTCHECK-R3](short/fact-check.md) against R1 boundaries; they needed no new sources.
- **Line under review (R3):** `ในหมอกแบบนี้ ตาเห็นแค่บางส่วน… สมองเราก็มักจะเติมส่วนที่ขาดเอง แล้วฉันก็เติมมันด้วยความกลัว`
- **Finding:** `K001a` (seeing distance shrinks in fog) and `K001b` (the brain tends to complete missing parts) are supported as hedged general statements. The fear clause is supported only as a tendency to read ambiguous cues toward threat (`K001c`). The current wording says fear fills the gap with content (`K001d`), which no inspected source supports. The narrower wording below keeps the approved insight and the first-person voice.
- **Recommended wording (R-1, for Preaw):** `ในหมอกแบบนี้ ตาเห็นแค่บางส่วน… สมองเราก็มักจะเติมส่วนที่ขาดเอง แล้วความกลัวก็ชวนให้ฉันตีความมันว่าเป็นอันตราย`
- **Usable:** the R-1 wording above. Keep “มักจะ” (tends to) in the completion clause. The fear clause is the narrator's own tendency-framed reading, not a demonstrated cause in this case.
- **Avoid:** `เติมมันด้วยความกลัว` as a statement about content; `ทุกครั้ง`, `ทุกคน`, `always` or `ทุกอย่าง`; any fear-made creature; any pareidolia claim; any statement that the fog, the outside air or the building is safe.
- **Access gaps:** `SRC005`, `SRC006`, `SRC007`, `SRC008`–`SRC011`, `SRC013` and `SRC014` were inspected from abstracts, PubMed records or summaries only; full texts not accessed. `SRC004` is an author reprint, not the publisher page. `SRC012` was read in part. Publisher Nature pages redirect to sign-in and PubMed web pages require cookies, so E-utilities records were used. `SRC016` and `SRC017` are not relied on.
- **Missing evidence:** no inspected source tests a fear-induced misreading of a human-with-cart silhouette in fog or dim light. The `K001c` evidence concerns faces, sentences, attention and labels, not shapes in fog.
- **Confidence (qualitative):** high for `K001a` and for the completion phenomenon; moderate for trait or clinical anxiety effects on interpretation; low for acute fear effects on visual shapes.
