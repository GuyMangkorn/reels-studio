## Story core

- **Logline:** In 1997 NOAA's deep-sea microphones caught a sound louder than any known animal; the internet imagined a sea monster, but years later the same sound pattern turned up near Antarctica, coming from giant icebergs cracking apart.
- **Central question (hook):** what made a sound that loud under the ocean? Was it a giant animal?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the listeners (NOAA scientists, and the viewer) want to name the source. The obstacles: the sound is too low to hear without speeding it up, it "sounds alive", and it came from somewhere far away and empty. The test that settles it is listening near Antarctica years later.
- **Intended feeling:** a chill at a sound with no owner, then the fun of the monster rumour, then the click of "the microphones were built to hear quakes, and they heard one, made of ice".
- **Learning takeaway:** (1) hydrophones (underwater microphones) sit in a deep "sound channel" where low sounds travel thousands of kilometres; (2) cracking icebergs make icequakes loud enough to cross an ocean; (3) the Bloop's rising, "alive-sounding" pattern matches icequakes recorded near Antarctica, which is why NOAA (around 2012) called it consistent with ice, not an animal.

## Truth and ending

- **What happened (lead-level evidence):** NOAA PMEL's equatorial Pacific hydrophone array, built to monitor undersea earthquakes/volcanoes, recorded the Bloop repeatedly in 1997 (`F001`–`F003`). It was very low (public audio sped up 16×), rose in frequency over about a minute and was heard more than 5,000 km away (`F005`–`F007`). Some scientists wondered about a biological source; the public imagined a monster (`F008`, key conclusion 2). Later Antarctic hydrophones recorded icequakes with very similar spectrograms; NOAA publicised the icequake explanation around 2012 (`F010`, `F012`).
- **Kept uncertain or unused:** NOAA's wording is "consistent with", so the script says "ตรงกับ", never "พิสูจน์แล้ว". No specific iceberg or glacier (`F011`). No Hz figure (`F006`, `F016`). No "8 years later" (`F012`). No coordinates, no Lovecraft/R'lyeh, no mermaid mockumentary (`F004`, `F013`, `F014` unused; see open questions). Location is only "ทิศของเสียงชี้ไกลลงไปทางใต้… ทางขั้วโลกใต้".
- **Ending chosen before clues:** the reveal is ice (icequakes from giant icebergs near Antarctica). The last line dismisses the creature as imagination, explains why the sound seemed alive, then invites a second listen, which hands back to the hook's "เสียงนี้…" for the `B_loop` seam. The final image is cracking ice, never the creature.
