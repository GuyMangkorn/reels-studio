## Story core

- **Logline:** In 1997 NOAA's deep-sea microphones caught a sound unlike anything the listeners had heard. The internet imagined a sea monster. Years later a very similar sound pattern turned up near Antarctica, from giant icebergs cracking apart. Then the narrator hands the mystery back to the viewer.
- **Central question (hook):** what made a sound that loud and that strange under the ocean? Was it a giant animal?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the listeners (NOAA scientists, and the viewer) want to name the source. Three things stand in the way: the sound is very low, and NOAA sped it up 16×, it "sounds alive", and it came from somewhere far away and empty. The test that settles it is listening near Antarctica years later.
- **Intended feeling:** a chill at a sound with no owner, then the fun of the monster rumour, then the click of "the microphones were built to hear quakes, and they probably heard one, made of ice". It ends on a last playful shiver: an open question and a creature that swallows the camera.
- **Learning takeaway:**
  1. Hydrophones (underwater microphones) sit in a deep "sound channel", where low sounds travel thousands of kilometres.
  2. Cracking icebergs make icequakes loud enough to be detected over 5,000 km away.
  3. The Bloop's rising, "alive-sounding" pattern is consistent with icequakes recorded near Antarctica. NOAA published its icequake explanation around 2012.

## Truth and ending

- **What happened (primary evidence opened, `BLOOP-RESEARCH-R2`):**
  - NOAA PMEL's equatorial Pacific hydrophone array was built to listen for undersea earthquakes and volcanic activity. It recorded the Bloop repeatedly in 1997 (`F001`–`F003`).
  - The sound was extremely loud and unlike anything the listeners had heard (`F018`). It is very low (the public audio is sped up 16×), it rose rapidly in frequency over about a minute, and it was heard on multiple sensors at a range of over 5,000 km (`F005`, `F006a`, `F007a`).
  - Some scientists wondered about a biological source, and the public imagined a monster (`F008`, `F017`).
  - Antarctic hydrophones recorded icequakes with very similar spectrograms (Antarctic hydrophone arrays deployed from 2005, per the `SRC014` abstract; the Scotia Sea years differ within `SRC014`, so none is given). NOAA dates the discovery to 2005 and published the icequake explanation around 2012 (`F010`, `F012`).
- **Kept uncertain or unused:**
  - NOAA's wording is "consistent with". The script says "สอดคล้องกับ" (`B08`, `B09`) and "น่าจะ" (`B08`), never "พิสูจน์แล้ว", and never as an identity claim ("X คือ Y"). The `B10` question "มันคืออะไรกันแน่" is not an assertion.
  - "ต่ำมาก" is qualitative (`F006b`): no Hz figure (`F006`, `F016`). No specific iceberg or glacier (`F011`). No "8 years later" (`F012`).
  - No animal-loudness comparison (`F007c` is a 2002 inference, not a NOAA finding).
  - No coordinates, no Lovecraft/R'lyeh, no mermaid mockumentary (`F004`, `F013`, `F014` unused; see open questions).
  - Location is only "ทิศของเสียงชี้ไกลลงไปทางใต้… ทางขั้วโลกใต้" (`F019`).
- **Ending (user direction 2026-10-29, unchanged in R4):** the reveal is still ice (icequakes from giant icebergs near Antarctica). It is fully stated in `B07`–`B09` before the ending:
  - `B07`: the Antarctic recordings.
  - `B08`: NOAA's explanation, announced around 2012.
  - `B09`: "สัตว์ยักษ์ตัวนั้นมีแค่ในจินตนาการ" and "สอดคล้องกับเสียงน้ำแข็งแตกร้าว".
  - `B10`: the narrator asks "คุณคิดว่ามันคืออะไรกันแน่?". The question is an open invitation to the viewer. It is not a new claim and does not withdraw the explanation. A realistic Bloop creature then rises out of the dark under the "จินตนาการ" label, the camera rushes into its mouth, and the picture fades to black.
- **The tease is style, not evidence.** The user chose this tease. Evidence still points to ice, and no creature was ever found. Nothing spoken after `B09` suggests otherwise.
