## Story core

- **Logline:** In 1997 NOAA's deep-sea microphones caught a sound louder than any known animal call. The internet imagined a sea monster. Years later the same sound pattern turned up near Antarctica, from giant icebergs cracking apart. Then the narrator hands the mystery back to the viewer.
- **Central question (hook):** what made a sound that loud under the ocean? Was it a giant animal?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the listeners (NOAA scientists, and the viewer) want to name the source. Three things stand in the way: the sound is too low to hear without speeding it up, it "sounds alive", and it came from somewhere far away and empty. The test that settles it is listening near Antarctica years later.
- **Intended feeling:** a chill at a sound with no owner, then the fun of the monster rumour, then the click of "the microphones were built to hear quakes, and they probably heard one, made of ice". It ends on a last playful shiver: an open question and a creature that swallows the camera.
- **Learning takeaway:**
  1. Hydrophones (underwater microphones) sit in a deep "sound channel", where low sounds travel thousands of kilometres.
  2. Cracking icebergs make icequakes loud enough to cross an ocean.
  3. The Bloop's rising, "alive-sounding" pattern matches icequakes recorded near Antarctica. That is why NOAA, around 2012, called it consistent with ice, not an animal.

## Truth and ending

- **What happened (lead-level evidence):**
  - NOAA PMEL's equatorial Pacific hydrophone array was built to monitor undersea earthquakes and volcanoes. It recorded the Bloop repeatedly in 1997 (`F001`–`F003`).
  - The sound was very low (the public audio is sped up 16×). It rose in frequency over about a minute and was heard more than 5,000 km away (`F005`–`F007`).
  - Some scientists wondered about a biological source, and the public imagined a monster (`F008`, key conclusion 2).
  - Later, Antarctic hydrophones recorded icequakes with very similar spectrograms. NOAA publicised the icequake explanation around 2012 (`F010`, `F012`).
- **Kept uncertain or unused:**
  - NOAA's wording is "consistent with". The script says "ตรงกับ" (`B08`, `B09`) and "น่าจะ" (`B08`), never "พิสูจน์แล้ว" or "คือ".
  - No specific iceberg or glacier (`F011`). No Hz figure (`F006`, `F016`). No "8 years later" (`F012`).
  - No coordinates, no Lovecraft/R'lyeh, no mermaid mockumentary (`F004`, `F013`, `F014` unused; see open questions).
  - Location is only "ทิศของเสียงชี้ไกลลงไปทางใต้… ทางขั้วโลกใต้".
- **Ending (R2, user direction 2026-10-29):** the reveal is still ice (icequakes from giant icebergs near Antarctica). It is fully stated in `B07`–`B09` before the ending:
  - `B07`: the Antarctic recordings.
  - `B08`: NOAA's explanation.
  - `B09`: "สัตว์ยักษ์ตัวนั้นมีแค่ในจินตนาการ" and "ตรงกับเสียงน้ำแข็งแตกร้าว".
  - `B10`: the narrator asks "คุณคิดว่ามันคืออะไรกันแน่?". The question is an open invitation to the viewer. It is not a new claim and does not withdraw the explanation. A realistic Bloop creature then rises out of the dark, the camera rushes into its mouth, and the picture fades to black.
- **The tease is style, not evidence.** The user chose this tease. Evidence still points to ice, and no creature was ever found. Nothing spoken after `B09` suggests otherwise. Label handling for the `B10` creature is left for Boat to rule on (proposal in the Film handoff, `LBL-B10`).
