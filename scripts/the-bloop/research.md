## Brief

- **User concept (production material, Thai, kept verbatim):** "คิดบทเรื่องลึกลับ the bloop 1997 ในคลิปอาจจะทำตัว the bloop แต่จริง ๆ แล้วเป็นเสียงน้ำแข็งแตก ไมค์ใต้ทะเลจับได้ คนคิดว่าเป็นสัตว์ยักษ์"
- **Central question:** What made the huge, strange sound that underwater microphones picked up in 1997? Was it a giant sea creature?
- **Learning takeaway:** Breaking ice (icequakes from large icebergs) can be heard across thousands of kilometres of ocean (NOAA: icequakes detectable at over 5,000 km, F005). The ocean has a "sound channel" (SOFAR) that carries low sounds far (F015). NOAA later matched the Bloop to icequakes recorded near Antarctica (F010).
- **Period/context:** 1990s NOAA PMEL ocean-acoustics monitoring of undersea earthquakes and volcanoes (F003). NOAA's explanation appeared on its Vents page between June 2012 and January 2013 (F012). Internet creature mythology grew from the late 1990s and 2000s.

## Key conclusions that shape the telling (R2)

1. **The icequake explanation is verified in NOAA's own words, with its hedge.** The PMEL page says the 1997 sounds "are consistent with icequakes generated by large icebergs as they crack and fracture" (`SRC001`, F010). The popular NOAA OceanService page is stronger ("is now known to have originated from an icequake", `SRC015`). The script should keep PMEL's "consistent with / น่าจะ" hedge.
2. **"Some people thought it was an animal" is supported; "louder than any animal" is not a NOAA finding.** NOAA lists the public theories ("giant squids, whales, or some sea creature unknown to science", `SRC015`). Scientists floated an animal origin in 2002 (Fox's "hunch" and Lobel, `SRC012`). The "louder than any animal" comparison is a 2002 journalist's inference from the sensor range (`SRC012`, `SRC017`). NOAA says only "extremely loud … unlike anything they had heard before" (`SRC015`).
3. **The giant creature is fiction.** No primary source documents a creature. The boundary in the fiction section stands.
4. **The 50°S 100°W location was NOAA's own general estimate, not the current NOAA position.** The Vents page carried "a general location near 50oS; 100oW" with "the origin of the sound is unknown" in captures from 2009 through June 2012 (`SRC002`, F004). The current page replaced it with an arrival-azimuth region (`SRC001`, F011). The script does not use coordinates.
5. **Dates:** NOAA's own page says the source was discovered "in 2005" (`SRC015`; the Bransfield deployments began in December 2005, `SRC014`). The Antarctic icequake work for A53a was in early 2008 (`SRC001`, `SRC007`). The explanation was public in 2012 (captures and Boing Boing, F012). "NOAA concluded around 2012" conflicts with the 2005 discovery date. Say "NOAA published its explanation around 2012."
6. **The real clip is NOAA's own file (`bloop.wav`, sped up 16×).** Its licence basis is public domain as a federal work (17 U.S.C. §105(a) and §101). Usable by Editor after a `NOTICE` is recorded (see "Sound notes and AUD-1").

- "NOAA concluded it was an animal" or "NOAA thought it was a monster." (`F008`, `F017`: scientists and the public considered it; NOAA's conclusion is icequake.)
- "Louder than any animal sound we know" as a measured fact. (`F007c`: a 2002 inference.)
- "NOAA concluded the explanation in 2012." (It discovered the source in 2005 per `SRC015`; the explanation was published in 2012.)
- "50°S 100°W is NOAA's current location." (Historical, `F004`.)
- "A giant creature / Cthulhu / megalodon / giant squid made it." (Speculation or fiction only.)
- "It came from R'lyeh's coordinates." (Roughly 1,980 km away, and the NOAA point is historical.)
- "The Navy's secret SOSUS recorded it." (`F003`, `F021`: NOAA's own autonomous array.)
- "Mermaids proved the Bloop was a mermaid / NOAA confirmed it." (The show was a pseudo-documentary; NOAA's statement was about mermaids.)
- "An ice shelf broke off glacier X." (NOAA: icebergs; no specific iceberg named.)
- "It was bigger than a blue whale." (Speculation.)
- Any Hz value, any "one-minute recording", or any iceberg or A53a tag tied to the 1997 sound.

## Fact-vs-fiction boundary for the giant creature

- **Allowed:** a painted giant shape framed as imagination or rumour, with on-screen "จินตนาการ" and a sketch treatment (script `B04`–`B07`), or a labelled "what if" tease that is clearly a question (`B10`, label L1 in the fact-check).
- **Not allowed:** the narrator asserting that the creature exists; the creature shown as the source of the real recording without a frame; the creature standing unchallenged after the explanation. The reveal must replace the creature with ice (F010, F017).
- NOAA lists animal theories as having been considered (`SRC015`). That is not a NOAA finding that an animal was involved.
