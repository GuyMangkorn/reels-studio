## Brief

- **User concept (production material, Thai, kept verbatim):** "คิดบทเรื่องลึกลับ the bloop 1997 ในคลิปอาจจะทำตัว the bloop แต่จริง ๆ แล้วเป็นเสียงน้ำแข็งแตก ไมค์ใต้ทะเลจับได้ คนคิดว่าเป็นสัตว์ยักษ์"
- **Central question:** What made the huge, strange sound that underwater microphones picked up in 1997? Was it a giant sea creature?
- **Learning takeaway:** Breaking ice (icequakes from big Antarctic icebergs) can be loud enough to cross thousands of kilometres of ocean. The ocean has a "sound channel" (SOFAR) that carries low sounds very far. Scientists used later recordings near Antarctica to match the Bloop's sound pattern.
- **Period/context:** 1990s NOAA PMEL ocean-acoustics monitoring of undersea earthquakes and volcanoes. The explanation was published by NOAA around 2012. Internet creature mythology grew 2000s–2012.

## Key conclusions that shape the telling

1. The twist the user wants is **supported in substance (lead-level)**. NOAA PMEL's current Bloop page reportedly says the sound is "consistent with icequakes generated by large icebergs as they crack and fracture" (`SRC001`, `F010`).
2. **The "people thought it was a giant animal" frame needs nuance.** At least one scientist (marine biologist Phil Lobel) and, in 2002 press, Fox himself leaned toward a biological origin (`F008`). The "giant monster" image came mostly from the public, the internet and Lovecraft fans (`F013`). Fox reportedly told CNN in 2001 that he suspected Antarctic ice calving (`F009`). The safe frame is: *some scientists wondered whether it could be an animal, and the internet imagined a giant monster.* Do not say "scientists believed it was a monster."
3. **The giant creature is fiction.** If the clip shows it, it must be clearly presented as what people imagined (dream, legend, rumor, "what if"), never as a real event or as something NOAA recorded (see the boundary section).
4. **The 50°S 100°W location is widely repeated but not confirmed on the current NOAA page** (`F004`). One search summary of the current PMEL page says it gives only a broad arrival-azimuth estimate: between Bransfield Strait and the Ross Sea, or possibly Cape Adare (`F011`). Use soft wording ("กลางมหาสมุทรแปซิฟิกตอนใต้ ไกลจากแผ่นดิน" / "ทางใต้ ใกล้แอนตาร์กติกา") until verified.

## Myths to avoid stating as fact

- "The Bloop is still unexplained" (outdated since ~2012). "Unexplained for years" is fine.
- "A giant creature / Cthulhu / megalodon / giant squid made it." Present only as speculation or fiction.
- "Scientists proved / NOAA thought it was a monster." Some scientists considered a biological source. "Monster" was popular imagination.
- "It was bigger than a blue whale" as a measurement. It was an inference from loudness under the animal hypothesis.
- "Recorded by the Navy's secret SOSUS." It was NOAA's own autonomous array, which complemented SOSUS. SOSUS is a separate system.
- "It came exactly from R'lyeh's coordinates." The locations are ~2,000 km apart and the Bloop point itself is unverified.
- "*Mermaids* proved the Bloop was a mermaid / NOAA confirmed it." The show was labelled fiction and NOAA denied mermaids.
- "A specific ice shelf from glacier X broke off." No single source was identified.
- "Heard once, live, by scientists' ears." It was recorded repeatedly and analysed later. Audible playback needs 16× speed-up.
- Exact Hz values or exact dates, unless verified.

## Fact-vs-fiction boundary for the giant creature

- **Allowed:** a painted giant shape framed as imagination ("ถ้าเป็นสัตว์… มันต้องใหญ่ขนาดไหน?", "อินเทอร์เน็ตจินตนาการว่า…", a rumor/sketch/legend look, a question mark). Clear framing devices: narration that says imagined/speculated, a visual style shift (sketchy/dream treatment), or on-screen text such as "จินตนาการ".
- **Not allowed:** the narrator asserting the creature exists, or the creature shown as the source of the real recording without a frame, or the creature still standing unchallenged at the end. The reveal must replace the creature with ice. The final image/line should leave viewers with "icequake from Antarctic icebergs", not with an ambiguous creature tease that contradicts `F010`. A playful loop or ending tease is fine if it is clearly a joke or a question, not an assertion.
- The NOAA conclusion is "consistent with / most likely". A stronger "100% solved" claim needs qualification. "นักวิทยาศาสตร์พบว่าตรงกับเสียงน้ำแข็งแตก" is fine.

## Sound notes

- **Real recording:** the NOAA PMEL Bloop audio (16× sped-up WAV on `SRC002`/`SRC001`). Wikimedia Commons tags it public domain as a work of NOAA (US federal works are generally not copyrighted under 17 U.S.C. §105). **Status: lead-only.** Before use, open the PMEL page and NOAA's site disclaimer to confirm the file is NOAA-produced (not third-party) and record the source URL, date and credit ("Audio: NOAA PMEL") in a `NOTICE` beside the asset, per production-requirements licences. If the real clip is used, label it as the real recording and say on screen or in VO that it is sped up 16× (e.g. "เสียงจริง (เร่ง 16 เท่า)").
- **Fictional/reconstructed sound:** any creature roar, whale-like call or "monster" sound is fictional and must sit inside the imagination framing. It must not be presented as, or spliced into, the real Bloop. Don't present a synthesized "bloop" as the NOAA recording.
- An ice-crack/rumble SFX at the reveal is an illustrative reconstruction, not the Bloop recording. Avoid implying it is the 1997 audio.
- No sound should imply casualties, ships or military events (Fox checked with Navy intelligence; nothing military was involved per `SRC010`, a report).

## Supported story angles

1. **Mystery → imagination → reveal:** a sound louder than any animal crosses 5,000 km. Some think "giant animal", the internet adds Lovecraft and mermaids. Years later, microphones near Antarctica hear the same "voice" from cracking icebergs.
2. **Knowledge hook:** "the sea has a sound highway" (SOFAR, `F015`) explains how ice in Antarctica can be heard near the equator.
3. **Fair clue:** the real sound is sped up 16× and rises over a minute (`F006`, `F007`). Also the location is far south, toward the ice (`F004`/`F011`; soft wording until verified).
