## 3. Claim ledger

Confidence is qualitative. "Usable wording" is a suggestion. Quantities give the safest rounding.

### Detection

**WOW-C01 — When, where and who.** On 15 August 1977, at about 11:16 p.m. Eastern Daylight Time, Ohio State University's Big Ear radio telescope (Delaware, Ohio) recorded the signal while it was running as a SETI search. Volunteer astronomer Jerry Ehman found it a few days later, "probably on August 19", while reviewing the computer printout.
- Source: SRC001 §Introduction. SRC009 (Big Ear ran as a full-time SETI facility 1973–1998).
- Confidence: high for the date, telescope and discoverer. The discovery day (19 Aug) is Ehman's own "probably".
- Note for the hook: Thai time would be 16 Aug, about 10:16 a.m. (Asia/Bangkok). Use the U.S. date, 15 Aug 1977, as the standard reference date.
- Usable wording: "15 สิงหาคม 1977 กล้องโทรทรรศน์วิทยุ 'บิ๊กเอียร์' ของมหาวิทยาลัยโอไฮโอสเตต อเมริกา จับสัญญาณประหลาดได้ ไม่กี่วันต่อมา นักดาราศาสตร์ชื่อเจอร์รี เอห์แมน เห็นมันบนกระดาษพิมพ์"

**WOW-C02 — "6EQUJ5" and the circled "Wow!".** Ehman circled the characters "6EQUJ5" in red and wrote "Wow!" in the margin. Each character is the signal strength in one 12-second sample, measured against background noise: digits 0–9, then A = 10 … Z = 35. The values are 6, 14, 26, 30, 19, 5. The peak "U" (30–31) means about 30 times stronger than the noise.
- Source: SRC001 §The Computer Printout, §Signal Strength = Intensity.
- Confidence: high.
- Myth to avoid: "6EQUJ5" is **not** a message, code or word. It is a rise-and-fall of strength.
- Usable wording: "6EQUJ5 ไม่ใช่รหัสลับ มันคือค่าความแรงของสัญญาณ ยิ่งตัวอักษรไกลจาก A ยิ่งแรง และ U คือแรงกว่าเสียงรบกวนพื้นหลังราว 30 เท่า"

**WOW-C03 — About 72 seconds, and why.** Big Ear was fixed to the ground. Earth's rotation swept its beam across the sky, so any point in the sky passed through the beam in about 72 seconds (six 12-second samples). The strength rose and fell exactly as expected for a point source drifting through the beam. So "72 seconds" is the length of the telescope's viewing window, and the signal was present across the whole window.
- Source: SRC001 §Intermittency, Duration and Modulation and the fixed-telescope passages. SRC003 §VIII.3 ("for the full 72-second window"). SRC003 Table 4 revises the minimum duration to ≥73.4 ± 0.5 s.
- Confidence: high.
- Qualification: do not say the signal "lasted exactly 72 seconds and then stopped". The source may have been on for longer, but the telescope could only see it for about 72 seconds. "ราว 72 วินาที" is safe.
- Usable wording: "กล้องตัวนี้ขยับไม่ได้ มันอาศัยโลกหมุนพาให้มองกวาดไปทั่วฟ้า จุดหนึ่งบนฟ้าจะผ่านสายตามันแค่ราว 72 วินาที และสัญญาณก็อยู่ครบทั้ง 72 วินาทีนั้นพอดี"

**WOW-C04 — Near the 1420 MHz hydrogen line, and why SETI listened there.** Neutral hydrogen, the most common element in the universe, naturally emits radio waves at about 1420.406 MHz (the "21-cm line"). SETI researchers reasoned that another civilization might choose that frequency for a beacon. The band is protected internationally, so broadcasting and satellite transmission there are prohibited. The Wow! signal sat in one 10 kHz channel close to that line. Ehman's frequency was 1420.4556 ± 0.005 MHz. The 2025 reanalysis revises it to 1420.726 ± 0.005 MHz (WOW-C11).
- Source: SRC001 §Frequency of Observation (hydrogen as a logical beacon guess; the band "declared off limits"). SRC003 §VII, §IX.
- Confidence: high that it was near the hydrogen line and that the band is protected. The exact frequency has been revised, so do not put a 7-digit number on screen. "ใกล้ 1420 เมกะเฮิรตซ์" is safe.
- Usable wording: "ไฮโดรเจนเป็นธาตุที่มีมากที่สุดในจักรวาล และมัน 'ส่งเสียง' วิทยุที่ราว 1420 เมกะเฮิรตซ์ นักล่าเอเลียนเลยเดาว่า ถ้ามีใครอยากทักเรา ก็น่าจะใช้คลื่นนี้ และสัญญาณ Wow! ก็มาใกล้คลื่นนี้มาก" (avoid "เป๊ะ ๆ": the signal sat tens to hundreds of kHz off the line, depending on the analysis)

**WOW-C05 — Direction: Sagittarius.** The source lies in the constellation Sagittarius (ราศีธนู / กลุ่มดาวคนยิงธนู), at declination about −27°. Because Big Ear had two feed horns, the position has two possible right ascensions. The 2025 reanalysis narrows it to two small adjacent fields, still in Sagittarius.
- Source: SRC001 §Summary of the "Wow!" Signal Parameters. SRC003 abstract.
- Confidence: high for Sagittarius. No star, planet or object has been identified as the source.
- Myth to avoid: "it came from a particular star" or "from a planet that could support life". Neither has been shown.

**WOW-C06 — Never detected again.** Big Ear kept observing the same strip of sky for about 60 days, and in later years scanned it many times. It never saw the signal again. Later searches with larger instruments, including the VLA (Gray & Marvel 2001) and a 26 m dish in Hobart, Tasmania (Gray & Ellingsen 2002), also found nothing.
- Source: SRC001 §Intermittency. SRC005 ("Despite extensive follow-up observations, the signal was never detected again"). SRC011 (lead-only for the later searches).
- Confidence: high that no repeat has been reported. The specific later search details are lead-only.
- Usable wording: "หลังจากนั้นนักวิทยาศาสตร์ส่องจุดเดิมซ้ำแล้วซ้ำเล่าหลายสิบปี ไม่เคยเจอมันอีกเลย"

### The alien question

**WOW-C07 — Why it looked artificial, and why it was never confirmed.** It looked like what a SETI beacon was expected to look like: very narrow in frequency (one 10 kHz channel), strong, from a single point on the sky, and near the hydrogen line, in a band where human transmitters are prohibited. Ehman checked and ruled out planets, asteroids, satellites, aircraft, spacecraft and ground transmitters as far as the data allowed. He wrote that an ETI "might have sent" it, but also that "the origin of the Wow! signal is still an open question for me" and that there is "simply too little data". It never repeated, it carried no detectable modulation or message (six data points cannot show one), and the second of the two feed horns should also have seen a fixed sky source shortly afterwards but did not, which is still unexplained. SRC001 puts the gap at about five minutes, while SRC008 paraphrases Ehman as about a minute and a half, so give no number.
- Source: SRC001 §ETI, §Ruling out alternatives, §Effect of Dual-Horn Feed System, §Modulation. SRC002 §II (narrowness near the hydrogen line made an extraterrestrial origin "a natural hypothesis", but no repetition or modulation "further complicates this interpretation").
- Confidence: high.
- Wording boundary: "it was aliens" is unsupported. "Aliens have been ruled out" is also unsupported (SRC002 §VIII says the cloud idea "does not conclusively rule out" other origins, including extraterrestrial ones). Safe: "ไม่มีหลักฐานยืนยันว่าเป็นเอเลียน" / "ไม่เคยมีใครพิสูจน์ได้".

### Explanations tried and dropped

**WOW-C08 — The comet idea (Antonio Paris, 2016–2017) did not hold up.** Antonio Paris (St. Petersburg College, Florida) and Evan Davies proposed that hydrogen around comets 266P/Christensen and P/2008 Y2 (Gibbs), which passed through that region of sky in 1977, caused the signal. In 2017 Paris reported 1420 MHz observations of 266P. The Big Ear team rejected the idea. Ehman: "We do not believe the two-comets theory can explain the Wow! signal." Their reasons were that the comets were in the wrong place (266P was roughly 2° north and offset by nearly an hour in right ascension), that hydrogen emission from comets at 21 cm had never been reported, that a slow-moving comet should have shown up in both feed horns, and that a diffuse comet cloud would not produce such a point-like, sharp signal. SETI Institute's Seth Shostak also doubted comets emit enough at 1420 MHz.
- Source: SRC007 (abstract), SRC006 (Ehman 2016, Dixon 2017), SRC008 (quotes and the 2° offset). SRC005: comets "are not sufficiently compact, bright, or transient".
- Confidence: high that the comet idea was proposed and widely criticized. The criticisms come mainly from the original Ohio State team and the Méndez project (who have a competing idea), plus Shostak.
- Usable wording: "ปี 2017 มีคนเสนอว่าเป็นดาวหาง แต่ทีมที่ค้นพบสัญญาณเองแย้งว่า ดาวหางอยู่ผิดตำแหน่ง และไม่เคยมีใครเห็นดาวหางส่งคลื่นแบบนี้แรงขนาดนั้น"
- Optional (not needed for a Short): in Sep 2025 Avi Loeb blogged that interstellar object 3I/ATLAS arrived from a direction within roughly 9° of the Wow! position and called it a long-shot coincidence (SRC013, lead-only). SRC006 argues it cannot explain the signal. If used at all, call it speculation.

### The reveal (hypothesis)

**WOW-C09 — Arecibo Wow! I (2024): cold hydrogen clouds that briefly flare.** Abel Méndez (Planetary Habitability Laboratory, University of Puerto Rico at Arecibo) and colleagues searched archived Arecibo data from February–May 2020 using settings similar to Big Ear's. They found narrowband signals near the hydrogen line that resemble the Wow! signal but are about 100 times weaker and in several places in the sky. They identify these as small, cold clouds of neutral hydrogen in our galaxy (kinetic temperatures below ~97 K). They hypothesize that the Wow! signal was one such cloud brightening suddenly, by about 100 times for a few minutes, through stimulated, maser-like emission (or superradiance) triggered by a powerful transient source such as a magnetar flare or soft gamma repeater. They call this possibly "the first recorded instance of maser-like emission of the hydrogen line" and a new source of false positives for SETI.
- Source: SRC002 abstract, §V, §VI, §VII, §VIII. SRC004.
- Confidence: high that this is what the authors propose and found. The hypothesis itself is unconfirmed (WOW-C10).
- **Publication status (31 Oct 2026):** preprint only. arXiv lists "submitted to ApJ" (v2, Sep 2024) with no journal reference. The authors' own page still says it "will soon be submitted for peer review". One Aug 2026 Spanish article (SRC014) says Méndez explained it "in the Astrophysical Journal" without any citation, and Boat found no ApJ record. Treat "published in a peer-reviewed journal" as **unverified**, and do not say it in the script.
- Hook detail: the 2020 data were among the last from the 305 m Arecibo telescope, whose platform collapsed on 1 Dec 2020 (SRC012, lead-only, consistent across outlets). If used, keep it simple: "ข้อมูลชุดสุดท้าย ๆ ก่อนกล้องอาเรซีโบพังถล่มปลายปี 2020". This would need an inspected source before the fact-check.
- Usable wording: "ปี 2024 ทีมนักวิทยาศาสตร์จากเปอร์โตริโกค้นข้อมูลเก่าของกล้องอาเรซีโบ แล้วเจอสัญญาณหน้าตาคล้าย Wow! หลายจุด แต่แผ่วกว่าราว 100 เท่า และต้นตอคือ... เมฆก๊าซไฮโดรเจนเย็น ๆ ในอวกาศ"

**WOW-C10 — What the cloud hypothesis has not shown (the honesty boundary).**
- No specific cloud has been identified at the Wow! position. SRC003 links compact HI clouds only to two weaker signals ("Wow2", "Wow3") in other locations, and for Wow! itself only notes that the velocity is "compatible with" intermediate-velocity hydrogen clouds.
- No trigger (magnetar or otherwise) has been identified. SRC002 §VII: "we cannot rule out other sources of radiation".
- The physics is unconfirmed in nature. SRC005 (June 2026): stable hydrogen-line maser action "is believed to be very difficult to sustain under natural astrophysical conditions", and hydrogen superradiance has "not yet been firmly confirmed in astrophysical environments". Confirmation "will require detecting additional similar events". SRC002 §VI.3: astronomical hydrogen masers "are rare".
- It does not rule out other origins. SRC002 §VIII: it "does not conclusively rule out" other origins, "encompassing both artificial terrestrial or extraterrestrial origins". SRC005: no explanation "has been independently verified".
- Both papers are by one team, preprints, not independently replicated.
- Confidence: high (the authors state these limits themselves).

**WOW-C11 — Arecibo Wow! II (Aug 2025): revised numbers from recovered Ohio SETI data.** The same team, now with 11 authors including David Kipping, recovered decades of unpublished Ohio State SETI records and reanalyzed the 1977 detection. Results: peak flux density of at least about 250 Jy (≥256 ± 63 Jy, a lower limit), stronger than the earlier 54 or 212 Jy; frequency 1420.726 ± 0.005 MHz, which implies a source moving toward us faster than assumed (heliocentric about −84 km/s); a sky position narrowed to two adjacent fields at declination about −26°57′; "no evidence" of radio interference; and an astrophysical origin supported, with a maser flare or superradiance burst from a small, cold HI cloud "plausible". The paper does not rule out transmissions at that frequency.
- Source: SRC003 abstract, §VI, §VII, §IX, §X, Table 4.
- Confidence: high that this is what the preprint reports. The numbers are the authors' revision and have not been independently checked or peer reviewed ("to be submitted to ApJ").
- Usable wording: "ปี 2025 ทีมเดิมขุดข้อมูลต้นฉบับปี 1977 มาคำนวณใหม่ พบว่าสัญญาณแรงกว่าที่เคยคิด และไม่น่าจะมาจากคลื่นรบกวนบนโลก"

**WOW-C12 — 2025–2026 status: still open, cloud idea is the most developed natural explanation.** As of 31 Oct 2026 Boat found no published rebuttal of the cloud hypothesis, no "Arecibo Wow! III" preprint, no peer-reviewed journal version of either preprint (WOW-C09), and no repeat detection. The June 2026 Ohio SETI history (SRC009) mentions the Arecibo Wow! project without judging the origin. The authors' FAQ (SRC005, June 2026) says the origin "remains uncertain".
- Confidence: moderate. This is an absence-of-evidence finding from web and arXiv searches on one date. Boat did not query ADS or IOP directly (the ADS page did not render), so a recent journal version could have been missed. Recheck at fact-check time.
- Wording boundary: "most developed / newest natural explanation" is defensible. "Most scientists now agree" or "consensus" is **not** supported by anything inspected.

### Side facts (optional hooks)

**WOW-C13 — Big Ear no longer exists.** The telescope was demolished in spring 1998 (work began 22 April; the last pieces came down in early May) after the land was sold for housing and a golf-course expansion.
- Source: SRC010. Confidence: high.
- Usable wording: "กล้องที่จับสัญญาณได้ ถูกรื้อทิ้งไปตั้งแต่ปี 1998 เพื่อทำสนามกอล์ฟกับหมู่บ้าน"

## 4. Reveal wording boundaries for Preaw

The requested reveal ("it was a cloud") is usable **only as a hypothesis**. The script must leave the viewer knowing it is the best new explanation, not the proven answer.

Suggested reveal line (accuracy draft, not narration):
"คำตอบใหม่ที่นักวิทยาศาสตร์เสนอ อาจไม่ใช่เอเลียน… แต่เป็นเมฆก๊าซไฮโดรเจนเย็น ๆ กลางอวกาศ ที่จู่ ๆ ก็สว่างวาบขึ้นชั่วครู่ อาจเพราะถูกแสงจากการระเบิดของดาวแม่เหล็กยิงใส่ แต่จนวันนี้ ยังไม่มีใครเห็นมันเกิดซ้ำ ปริศนานี้จึงยังไม่ปิดซะทีเดียว"

Terminology: say "เมฆก๊าซไฮโดรเจน" or "กลุ่มก๊าซไฮโดรเจนในอวกาศ", not just "เมฆ", so viewers do not picture a rain cloud. "แมกนีตาร์ (ดาวนิวตรอนที่มีสนามแม่เหล็กแรงจัด)" is accurate if the term is used.

The B_loop ending works naturally with the open status: "…ถ้ามันเกิดขึ้นอีก กล้องทั่วโลกพร้อมฟังอยู่".

## 5. Hook-worthy verified details

- The astronomer literally wrote "Wow!" in red pen next to the numbers (WOW-C02).
- "6EQUJ5" looks like a code but is a strength reading, and "U" means about 30 times the background noise (WOW-C02).
- The telescope could not move. Earth's spin gave it about 72 seconds per patch of sky, and the signal filled the whole window (WOW-C03).
- It arrived on the exact "cosmic channel" that SETI had guessed aliens would use, which is also a channel nature uses (WOW-C04). This is the irony that carries the reveal.
- Decades of searching, and it never came back (WOW-C06).
- The new clue came from old data of a telescope that later collapsed (WOW-C09, collapse lead-only), and the 1977 telescope itself is now a golf course (WOW-C13).

## 6. Common myths to avoid

| Myth | Correction | Claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6EQUJ5 is a coded message | Strength values per 12 s sample | WOW-C02 |
| The signal lasted exactly 72 s, then stopped | 72 s is the telescope's viewing window. The source may have lasted longer | WOW-C03 |
| There is a recording of the Wow! sound | Only printed numbers exist. Online "sounds" are sonifications | §1 |
| It came from a specific star or habitable planet | Only a sky region in Sagittarius is known | WOW-C05 |
| Comets explained it (2017) | Rejected by the discoverer's team and others | WOW-C08 |
| Arecibo picked up the Wow! signal again | It found weaker, similar signals elsewhere in the sky | WOW-C09 |
| It is now solved, it was a hydrogen cloud or magnetar | Hypothesis, preprints, no cloud or trigger identified at the position | WOW-C10, C12 |
| Scientists have proven it was not aliens | Not ruled out, just unsupported | WOW-C07, C10 |

## 7. Visual and sound notes for later stages (facts only)

- **Printout:** the famous printout image is a scan held by the Big Ear Radio Observatory / NAAPO (SRC001). Rights and licensing are unverified. A redrawn or reconstructed printout should be labelled or obviously stylized, and must keep the real characters "6EQUJ5" in a vertical column with a circled "Wow!".
- **Big Ear:** a large flat, fixed reflector of the Kraus type, not a steerable dish. Drawing a classic movable dish (like a VLA antenna) would be inaccurate for the 1977 scene. Arecibo, by contrast, was the giant dish in a valley (305 m).
- **Sound:** an alarm, beeping or voice-like transmission must not imply that the detection was heard live. It was found on paper days later (WOW-C01). A printer clatter or a stylized tone is fine as dramatization.
- **Cloud/magnetar:** any depiction is an illustration of a hypothesis. On-screen text should carry the hedge (e.g. "สมมติฐาน" / "อาจเป็น").
