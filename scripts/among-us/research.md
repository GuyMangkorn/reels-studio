## 1. Fair-play deduction design

### Principles (with sources)

| ID | Finding | Label | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FP01 | **Equal opportunity.** The viewer must have every clue the solver has, plainly stated. Van Dine rule 1: "The reader must have equal opportunity with the detective for solving the mystery." Knox 8: the detective "is bound to declare any clues". | safe | SRC001, SRC002 |
| FP02 | **Culprit seen early and given a real part.** Knox 1 ("mentioned in the early part"), Van Dine 10 ("a more or less prominent part"). All five suspects are introduced in the first ~10 s and the alien speaks or acts on screen at least once before the reveal. | safe | SRC001, SRC002 |
| FP03 | **The rule of the monster is stated before it is used.** Knox 4/10 forbid an explanation needing "a long scientific explanation at the end" and unprepared doubles. Adapted: the one thing the copy gets wrong (the "flaw rule") is told to the viewer in the setup, not invented at the reveal. | safe | SRC002, SRC015 |
| FP04 | **No luck, no intuition.** Knox 6. The reveal is earned by a clue, not by the alien slipping by accident at the end or a character "just knowing". | safe | SRC002 |
| FP05 | **No lying narrator.** Van Dine 2 bans tricks on the reader beyond those the culprit plays; Knox 9 says the Watson must not conceal thoughts. The narrator states only true things; characters (including the alien) may lie, and dialogue is marked as dialogue. | safe | SRC001, SRC002 |
| FP06 | **Challenge to the viewer.** Ellery Queen pauses the story to say the reader now has "all the pertinent facts". A short "หยุดคลิปแล้วเดา" beat before the reveal is the Short-form equivalent. | option | SRC004 |
| FP07 | **Clue by absence.** Silver Blaze: the dog that did *not* bark. Strong for audio ("four sounds, not five"). | option | SRC005 |
| FP08 | **Truth visible on rewatch.** Van Dine 15: "The truth of the problem must at all times be apparent--provided the reader is shrewd enough to see it." Each clue has an innocent first reading and a damning second reading. | safe | SRC001, SRC015 |
| FP09 | **Social-deduction tells come from behaviour, claims and contradictions,** not physical tests. Werewolf/Mafia players read behaviour, claims and voting records; the article quotes a designer: "If the villagers are allowed to keep a pencil and paper, they always win" (i.e. a written record exposes contradictions). Adapted: the viewer's "pencil and paper" is the narration/captions; the alien's line must contradict an earlier stated fact. | safe | SRC006, SRC007 |
| FP10 | **Known fairness critique.** Even the *Roman Hat* challenge is accused (lead-only reviews) of letting a clue slip "by lazy implication". Risk for us: a clue carried only by a painted detail, too small in a vertical frame or under a caption. | avoid (visual-only decisive clue) | SRC004 leads |

### Clue-budget recommendation for ~60 s, 5 suspects (option, Boat's design proposal)

- **1 flaw rule (setup, 0–10 s):** one sentence telling what the copy cannot do or gets wrong. Example shape, not script: "it copies faces and voices, but only what it saw".
- **2 true clues:** one primary tell + one corroborating tell, both pointing at the same suspect, both carried by **narration or dialogue** (so they appear in captions and work with sound off). The visual may echo a clue but never be the only carrier (FP10).
- **2 red herrings:** each falls on a *different* innocent suspect and has an innocent explanation that the script states or makes checkable by the reveal (e.g. a sweating suspect is the one who said earlier they are afraid of lifts). A red herring must never satisfy the flaw rule.
- **Every suspect gets one "suspicious" moment** so all five feel live: alien (true clue), two red-herring suspects, and two suspects with neutral/clearing beats (one of them can voice the challenge or be cleared by passing the flaw rule on screen).
- **Single solution test (for the fact-check):** apply the flaw rule to each of the five; exactly one fails, and that failure is in the narration/dialogue before the challenge beat. Write a 5-row check table in `short/script.md` (suspect × clue × innocent reading × why cleared).
- **Rewatch anchor:** the decisive line occurs in the first half (around 15–35 s), early enough that a rewatch finds it, and the reveal can quote or echo it ("ย้อนไปฟังตอนที่ ... พูดว่า").
- **Comment-friendly IDs:** give suspects a number or a single distinct tag (seat 1–5, shirt colour, or nickname) so viewers can answer in one word. Numbers also help Mark/Film keep the five readable at Short scale.
- **Size check:** 5 suspects + 2 clues + 2 herrings + rule + challenge + reveal is already dense for ~60 s of Thai narration. If the voice lock runs long, cut a red herring before cutting a true clue.

### Five distinctive tells (option; Preaw picks one primary + one corroborating)

Each works in audio and in a painted-2D vertical frame with simple staging, and each can be verified from narration/captions alone.

| ID | Tell | How it works | Viewer verification from narration alone | Painted-2D note | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | **Knowledge cut-off** | The copy was made at a moment (e.g. when the lights flickered). It does not know something that happened *before it joined*, or knows something from *before the real person arrived*. | Narration states the earlier event and who was present ("ตอนนั้นมีแค่สี่คนที่ได้ยิน"); later one suspect refers to it wrongly or should not know it. | Pure dialogue; no special art. | Timing must be unambiguous: who was present when, stated in words. |
| T2 | **Wrong name: formal name instead of ชื่อเล่น** | The copy learned people from ID cards/name badges, so it calls a colleague by the formal given name where everyone uses nicknames. | Setup introduces each person by nickname; one line uses a formal name nobody said aloud. Rewatch: "no one ever said that name". | Name badges can be one establishing insert; the clue itself is spoken. | Relies on F001 (nickname norm). Some people do use given names; make it a name the person never said in the room, not just "formal". |
| T3 | **Absence count (Silver Blaze)** | In a beat everyone reacts audibly (lights out: gasps; joke: laughs; sneeze; a phone buzz), the narration counts "4" not "5", or one suspect's reaction is to the wrong cue. | Narration counts aloud ("ได้ยินเสียงหายใจ... สี่เสียง"). | Works in a dark/black frame; cheap to paint. | Sound design must match the count exactly (see Sound). A count alone can't tell *who*; pair with a second tell. |
| T4 | **Mirrored copy** | The copy was made from a reflection (lift mirror wall), so one asymmetrical detail is on the wrong side: a scar, watch, side parting, wedding ring hand. | Narration states the detail and side in the intro ("ไฝที่แก้มซ้าย"), and later states it again on the other side, or a character notices it aloud. | Needs one clear close-up; side must stay consistent across painted shots (continuity risk for Mark/Boss). | Visual continuity is the hardest; must be stated in words, not left to the picture (FP10). Avoid making it about a body difference (CS03). |
| T5 | **Answers the wrong question / literal reply** | A natural Thai small-talk ritual ("กินข้าวยัง", "ไปไหนมา") answered as if it were a literal question, or a habitual response missed (everyone replies the same way to a set-up phrase; one doesn't). | The setup shows the ritual once with the expected answer; later the suspect answers differently. | Dialogue-only. | Many real humans answer literally; make it a *room-specific* ritual established in the clip (e.g. a password the group agreed on at 0–10 s), not a "real Thai person would know" test (CS02). |

**Recommended pairing (option):** T1 or T2 as the primary (single suspect, checkable on rewatch) + T3 or T4 as corroboration. T5 works best as a red-herring generator (an innocent suspect fumbles the ritual for a stated reason).

### Cultural sensitivities (all `avoid` unless noted)

- **CS01 — ผีปอบ echo.** The "someone among us is not human" frame resembles Thai Phi Pop beliefs, where real accusations have targeted elderly, sick, disabled or isolated people (SRC012). Keep the story clearly sci-fi (alien), not a village-spirit accusation; do not borrow Phi Pop signs (raw-blood cravings, wasting illness).
- **CS02 — No "real Thai would know" test.** A tell that says a true human/Thai would do X can read as excluding foreigners, migrants, hill-tribe or regional people. Tie every tell to something established *in this room*.
- **CS03 — No tells that map onto real human differences:** regional accent/dialect (Isan, southern), gender particle ครับ/ค่ะ (would cast LGBTQ+ speakers as "not human"), flat affect or no eye contact (autism stereotype; also PA04), disability, skin colour, age.
- **CS04 — Violence and gore:** Shorts audience is broad; reveal by look/sound and implication rather than gore. (Platform-policy specifics not researched in this pass.)
- **CS05 — Real brands/places:** no real condo, company, school or lift maker names.

## Sound implications (for later roles)

- T3 (absence count) makes sound load-bearing: the number of gasps/laughs heard must exactly match the narration's count, and no extra reaction may be heard off screen.
- Lift/meeting-room ambience (hum, alarm bell, rain) is fictional; label as such in the sound plan. No real emergency recordings needed.
- A siren or crowd must not imply a casualty or event the story does not have.
