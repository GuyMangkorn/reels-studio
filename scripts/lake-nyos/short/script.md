## Story core

- **Logline:** One night in 1986, about 1,700 people around a quiet crater lake in Cameroon died, many in their sleep. Houses stood, trees were fine, but cattle, birds and insects died too. The viewer is invited to guess a beast or something that rose from the lake. Something did rise from the lake: the deep water flipped and released the CO2 stored in it, which flowed down the valleys like an invisible river.
- **Central question (hook):** what kills about 1,700 people in one night without breaking a single thing?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the viewer (standing in for survivors and investigators) wants to name the killer. Obstacles: nothing is broken, nobody saw an attacker, and rumours point in the wrong directions (bomb, secret weapon, spirits). The test: which suspect could also kill birds and insects while leaving houses untouched?
- **Intended feeling:** quiet dread → a shiver at the imagined thing rising from the lake → the click of "it *was* something from the lake, just not a creature" → respect and unease at the ending question.
- **Learning takeaway:**
  1. A deep crater lake can store CO2 from magma in its bottom water, held down like a capped soda bottle.
  2. When the layers overturned, the gas came out at once; nobody knows for sure what triggered it.
  3. CO2 is heavier than air, so it flowed down valleys like an invisible river and suffocated people and animals up to about 25 km away.
  4. Since 2001, pipes let Nyos release its gas slowly.

## Truth and ending

- **What happened (per `NYOS-RESEARCH-R1`, snippet-level):** on the night of 21 Aug 1986 (`F001`), survivors reported a rumble from Lake Nyos (`F002`). Mostly-CO2 gas stored in the lake's deep water was released and killed about 1,700 people by asphyxiation (`F003`, `F008`); the CO2 is magmatic, with no significant direct volcanic activity (`F009`). Thousands of livestock, birds and insects died; houses and plants were not damaged (`F004`, `F005`). The gas flowed down valleys, lethal to about 25 km (`F007`, `F013`). The lake turned red-brown afterwards (`F015`). Survivors did not know the cause; rumours (bomb, secret weapon) and spirit explanations followed (`F023`, `F024`). Degassing pipes since 2001 (`F021`).
- **Kept uncertain or unused:**
  - Trigger: stated as unknown (`B10`, `F011`). No landslide/rain/quake named.
  - No volcano eruption wording (`F009`). No gas tonnage, cloud speed, fountain height, lake-level drop or depth figure (`F010`, `F012`–`F014`).
  - Deaths "ราว 1,700" only (`F003`); livestock "หลายพันตัว" (`F004`).
  - "หลายคนหลับไปแล้ว ไม่ตื่นอีกเลย" is "many", not all (`F018`).
  - Smell reports (`F016`), flames going out (`F017`), skin lesions (`F019`), Monoun (`F020`), named villages beyond Nyos (`F006`), insect silence (sound notes) and named survivors: **not used**.
  - No country named in the bomb rumour (respectful-handling notes).
  - Lake Kivu (`F022`): named on screen only; spoken as "อีกทะเลสาบที่ใหญ่กว่ามาก", "มีคนอยู่รอบ ๆ นับล้าน", "ก้นของมันก็มีก๊าซอัดอยู่", then a hypothetical question. No risk size or extraction claim.
- **Ending (`B_loop`):** the reveal and knowledge land in `B09`–`B12`. `B13` turns to Kivu and closes on the question "แล้วถ้าวันหนึ่ง มันหายใจออกล่ะ?". The loop seam returns to `B01` "คนราว 1,700 คน… ตายในคืนเดียว", which reads as the answer to that question ([loop-friendly writing](../../../docs/scriptwriting-principles.md#loop-friendly-writing)). Hold the closing line ≈2–3 s on the still Kivu surface before the seam.
