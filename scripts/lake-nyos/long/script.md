## Story core

- **Logline:** A hypothetical opens the video: something rises from the lake behind your village, and you would never know. Then the narrator says it really happened. In 1986, about 1,700 people around a quiet crater lake in Cameroon died in one night. Some smelled rotten eggs or gunpowder and collapsed. By morning, cattle, birds and insects were dead, yet houses stood and survivors in the valleys saw no cloud at all. Beast? Something invisible? A bomb? Each suspect fails a clue. Then a witness on high ground saw a white cloud burst out of the water, and later the lake turned red-brown. Something did rise from the lake: carbon dioxide from its deep water, flowing down the valleys like an invisible river.
- **Central question (hook):** if something came up out of the lake behind your village tonight, would you know? → what killed about 1,700 people in one night without breaking anything, and without anyone seeing it?
- **Intention / obstacle:** the viewer (standing in for survivors and investigators) wants to name the killer. Obstacles: nobody saw it, nothing was broken, and two clues point the wrong way: the gunpowder smell (bomb) and the rumours of that time.
- **Intended feeling:** intimate dread (second person) → "this is real" chill → sensory unease (smell, collapse) → the eerie wrongness of an intact village → the guessing game → the satisfaction of ruling suspects out → the shiver of "something *did* come out of the lake" → understanding → respect and unease at the closing question.
- **Learning takeaway** (same as the Short, minus the distance):
  1. A deep crater lake can store CO2 from magma in its bottom water, held down like a capped soda bottle.
  2. When the water overturned, the gas came out at once; nobody knows for sure what triggered it.
  3. CO2 is heavier than air and cannot be seen, so it flowed down valleys like an invisible river and suffocated people and animals.
  4. Since 2001, pipes let Nyos release its gas slowly.

## Truth and ending

- **What happened (per `NYOS-RESEARCH-R1` + fact-check leads, snippet-level):** on the night of 21 Aug 1986 (`F001`) people reported a rumble from Lake Nyos (`F002`). Some who came out of their homes smelled rotten eggs or gunpowder, felt warm and quickly lost consciousness (`F016`, `SRC015`/`SRC016`). Mostly-CO2 gas stored in the lake's deep water was released and killed about 1,700 people by asphyxiation (`F003`, `F008`); the CO2 is magmatic, no significant direct volcanic activity (`F009`). Thousands of cattle, birds and insects died; houses and plants were not damaged (`F004`, `F005`). Survivors in the valley saw no visible cloud; one witness on high ground saw a white cloud rise from the lake (`SRC015`). The lake turned red-brown afterwards (`F015`, `SRC017`). Rumours of a bomb or secret weapon followed (`F023`). Pipes vent the gas since 2001 (`F021`).
- **Kept uncertain or unused:**
  - Trigger unknown (`B11`, `F011`); no landslide, rain or quake named or drawn.
  - **The smell is a reported sensation, not the gas's smell.** CO2 itself is odourless. The USGS team (`SRC002`, `Q10`, PDF p22, p25) attributes the reported rotten-egg/gunpowder smell, and the warmth, to high CO2 exposure itself (an "olfactory hallucination"); the lake water was low in sulfur and there was no toxicologic evidence for H2S (PDF p13, p22). This is the 1987 team's medical interpretation from one primary source (medium), and it is contrary evidence to `research.md` `F016`'s "trace sulfur" lead. The VO says "ได้กลิ่นเหมือน…" (they smelled something like…), never "ก๊าซมีกลิ่น". **By user decision (2026-10-31, `LR01` not applied), the smell stays unexplained in the narration and on screen** (open question 1).
  - No volcano eruption wording (`F009`); no tonnage, speed, fountain height, depth or distance figure (`F007`, `F010`, `F012`–`F014`).
  - "ราว 1,700" (`F003`); cattle "หลายพันตัว" (`F004`); "หลายคนหลับไปแล้ว" is many, not all (`F018`), and `B03` shows that some were awake.
  - Kivu (`F022`) unchanged from the Short: named only on screen; no risk size, no extraction claim.
- **Ending (`B_loop`):** reveal and knowledge land in `B10`–`B13`. `B14` turns to Kivu and closes on "แล้วถ้าวันหนึ่ง มันหายใจออกล่ะ?". The seam returns to `B00` "ถ้าคืนนี้…", which reads as the continuation of that question ([loop-friendly writing](../../../docs/scriptwriting-principles.md#loop-friendly-writing)). The last line and hook are a pair: lake breathing out ↔ something rising from the lake tonight. Hold ≈2–3 s on the still Kivu surface, last ≈0.5 s free of speech.
