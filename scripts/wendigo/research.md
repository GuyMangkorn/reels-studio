# WENDIGO research packet

- **Revision:** research r1 (Boat, 1 Nov 2026)
- **Deliverable:** `WDG-EP01-SHORT` (Thai Short, ~60 s). Profile `wdg-horror-fact`, tier and scope: [delivery.md](delivery.md).
- **Mode:** a fictional horror cold open (`fiction/dramatization`, labelled as such), then real folklore plus documented history. The Wendigo itself is presented as **folklore/belief**, never as a real creature. Named real cases are presented as **documented history** that involved belief in the windigo, not as proof that a windigo exists.
- **Central question (proposed):** What is the Wendigo really, according to the peoples whose tradition it comes from, and what does the horror-movie version get wrong?
- **Learning takeaway (proposed):** In Algonquian-speaking peoples' tradition the windigo is a winter cannibal spirit or monster tied to hunger and greed. A *person* can become one. The antlered deer-skull creature is a later pop-culture image. Real court cases (Swift Runner 1879, Jack Fiddler 1907) show how seriously people took the belief.
- **Period/context:** sub-Arctic and Great Lakes North America (now Canada and the northern US). Written records exist from the 17th century (Jesuit missionaries, lead-only) through the fur-trade era, with Canadian court cases in 1879 and 1907.
- **Research date:** 1 Nov 2026.

## Unsupported or circulated-only (do not assert)

- That the Wendigo is real, or that any killing proves its existence (reported belief ≠ cause).
- A single universal look (antlers, deer skull, glowing eyes). Glowing eyes and voice mimicry come from modern media and creepypasta. No tradition source was found for them.
- Exact Swift Runner victim count, the hour of execution, and the exact camp location.
- "Wendigo psychosis is in the DSM" / "a recognized disease".
- The Proto-Algonquian "owl" meaning (uncertain gloss).
- Saying the word "wendigo" summons it, or similar internet lore.

## Cultural framing notes

- The tradition belongs to living nations (Ojibwe/Anishinaabe, Cree, Innu and others). Present it as *their* teaching about hunger, winter and greed, not as "a Native monster" or "a savage belief". Avoid "Indian" and costume imagery (headdresses, war paint, drum/chant horror cues).
- Some communities consider the windigo a serious subject and are uneasy about entertainment use. A short respectful line, or simply accurate framing, is enough. Do not mock the belief.
- Real cases involve real families and deaths (Swift Runner's family; Wahsakapeequay; the Fiddler family, whose descendant co-wrote SRC002). No gore in the fact section, no victim re-enactment, and no implication that the defendants were "monsters". Mention Jack Fiddler's suicide only briefly and without detail, or omit it.
- The fictional cold open should not use a real named person, place or nation as the victim or villain unless the framing makes clear it is fiction.

## Sound implications (for later roles)

- Winter wind, snow crunch, creaking trees and breath are generic fictional ambience. Label them as dramatization in the cold open.
- Do not use Indigenous drumming, chant or flute as a horror cue. Do not use a gunshot, a scream or a hanging sound that implies details of the real cases.
- No real archival recordings exist for 1879/1907. Any "historical" sound is reconstructed and must be labelled.

## Supported story angles and hooks (Preaw chooses)

1. **Hunger twist:** the cold-open hunter starves in a blizzard. The fact turn: "ตามตำนาน เวนดิโกไม่ได้เกิดมาเป็นปีศาจ… มันเคยเป็นคน" (F003, F004). Strongest learning beat.
2. **Myth-bust:** open on the familiar antlered skull, then: "แต่ในตำนานจริง มันไม่มีเขากวาง" (F005, hedged as "ส่วนใหญ่เป็นภาพยุคหลัง").
3. **Real court case:** "ปี 1879 มีชายคนหนึ่งถูกแขวนคอ… หลังอ้างว่าเวนดิโกสิงเขา" (F006). Keep the framing as his claim.
4. **Greed meaning (loop/ending):** "ยิ่งกิน ยิ่งหิว" → ending on greed as the real monster (F003, F009). Loops back to the hunger in the cold open.
