## Story core

**Logline:** A man snowbound in a northern forest hears a voice outside his tent asking "หิวไหม…" every night — until he follows the footprints and finds the voice is his own. Then the video turns: this was fiction, but the fear comes from a real tradition, the windigo of Algonquian-speaking peoples, where an ordinary person can become the monster.

**Central question (hook):** Who is whispering "หิวไหม…" outside the tent? → after the turn: what is the Wendigo really, in the tradition it comes from?

**Intention / obstacle (fiction):** the man wants to survive the storm with his companion; the obstacle is hunger, which turns out to be inside him rather than outside the tent.

**Feeling:** isolation → dread (something circles the tent) → cold shock (it is him) → calm, serious curiosity in the fact section → a quiet chill at the end (the monster is a want that is never satisfied).

**Knowledge takeaway:** In the tradition of Algonquian-speaking peoples such as the Ojibwe and Cree, the windigo is a cannibal being of winter and hunger, said never to be satisfied; in many tellings a *person* can become one. The antlered deer-skull creature is mostly a later pop-culture image. The belief was serious enough to appear in a real 1879 court case. Indigenous writers today use it as a figure of greed.

**Plot function of the knowledge:** the fact section re-reads the cold open. "A person can become one" (F004) is exactly what happened to the fictional man; "ยิ่งกินยิ่งหิว" (F003) explains the voice; "no antlers" (F005) explains why the fiction never showed a creature.

## Truth and ending

- **Inside the fiction (S01):** there is no creature outside. Starving for days, the man has been walking around the tent at night himself (his own footprints) and whispering "หิวไหม…" himself. The last fiction image is him standing over his sleeping companion. Nothing further happens on screen; no attack, no blood, no eating is shown or said.
- **Real facts (S02–S03):** limited to what the ledger allows, hedged as it says (see [claim mapping](#claim-mapping)). Swift Runner's windigo explanation is stated as **his claim**; the court convicted him. No victim count, no execution detail, no hanging sound.
- **Remaining uncertainty (kept visible):** all sources are lead-only; F005 rests on weak sources and is hedged (`ส่วนใหญ่`); F003 "ยิ่งกินยิ่งหิว" is hedged as `เล่ากันว่า`; the mechanism of transformation varies by community (F004), so the script says `ในหลายเรื่องเล่า` and names no single rule.
- **Ending:** the last line returns the fiction's whisper as a metaphor (`ปีศาจตัวนี้ไม่ต้องมีเขา… แค่มีเสียงเล็กๆ ที่คอยถามว่า`) and seams into the frame-0 whisper `หิวไหม…`. See [ending proposal](#ending-proposal).

**Twist logic (fair):** planted by CL01 (the voice comes "every night" and only he hears it), CL02 (footprints circle the tent every morning although nobody has come), CL03 (`เรา`: he is not alone; his companion sleeps), and CL04 (the whisper is cast in the man's own voice, whispered). Payoff in S01-B03: the footprints fit his boots and the whisper comes from his own mouth. No new information is introduced at the twist.

## Ending proposal

- **Proposed primary: `C_seamless`.** The last line and the hook are a pair: `…แค่มีเสียงเล็กๆ ที่คอยถามว่า…` → (seam) → `หิวไหม…` → `เสียงนั้น… ถามผมทุกคืน`. On replay the opening whisper reads as the "small voice" the narrator just named, and the man's line becomes the answer to the narrator's metaphor. Last ~0.5 s speech-free; last picture = frame-0 tent composition; wind level matched at the seam.
- **Also deliverable: `B_loop`.** The ending line `ที่คอยถามว่า…` would hang unfinished without a loop, so `B_loop` appends the whisper `หิวไหม…` (≈1.5 s) and then holds ~3 s. This makes the two endings differ by one line of audio, not only by the hold — the Editor must confirm the shared ending behavior can do this (open question 4). `A_fade` is not proposed (a fade softens the final chill and breaks the pair).
- Final pick is the coordinator's/user's.
