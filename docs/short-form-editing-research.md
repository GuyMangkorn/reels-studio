# Short-form Editing Research

Research date: 27 October 2026. Scope: James's editing of Thai mystery, educational and twist videos in Remotion, with Reels/Shorts as the portrait delivery context.

**Status:** Research reference and proposed editorial defaults. This document introduces no runtime capability or automatic performance guarantee. [Production requirements](production-requirements.md), the matched Film brief and Mark board remain authoritative for a production task.

## Findings and evidence boundaries

### R01 — Start with a meaningful reason to watch

YouTube's January 2025 interview with Shorts creator Jenny Hoyos emphasizes immediate intrigue and a concise mini-story. Her one-second hook advice is a practitioner's approach, not a platform eligibility rule or experimental proof of a universal threshold. [SRC01](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)

**Project recommendation:** Make the opening frame intelligible and establish an anomaly, question, consequence or action promptly. Use the repository's existing first-two-seconds hook requirement; research does not replace it with another fixed interval. Fulfill the opening promise with the actual payoff.

### R02 — Build progression, rather than a quota of effects

TikTok's Creative Codes recommends hook/body/close structure and describes movement, text, sound and transitions as attention tools. Its evidence and conversion-oriented close concern advertisements, with underlying studies largely from 2020–2022; this is not proof that an organic Thai story needs a CTA card or a cut every N seconds. [SRC02](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)

**Project recommendation:** For each shot, identify what changes in action, knowledge, expectation or emotion. Remove redundant explanation and idle gaps while keeping cause, clue, reaction and consequence readable. A crop change alone needs a storytelling purpose. Retain deliberate suspense or comprehension holds.

### R03 — Use the simplest motivated transition

Adobe describes split edits: a J cut introduces the incoming audio before its picture, while an L cut continues outgoing audio over the next picture. They can support continuity, reaction and anticipation; their use is a stylistic decision. [SRC03](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/j-cut-and-l-cut.html)

**Project recommendation:** Use clean cuts as the baseline. Cut on an action, glance, new evidence, contrast or reaction when it improves comprehension. Use J/L audio bridges where the story calls for them and the current runtime can reproduce them. A whoosh, whip pan, speed ramp or zoom requires a stated function, not a trend quota.

### R04 — Compose for the mobile interface

Meta Blueprint's Reels training describes vertical content, sound and safe zones as creative essentials in a business-results context. The inspected page offers no durable pixel margins or organic storytelling performance guarantee. [SRC04](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/584573-reels-creative-supercharger-march-19?sid=456d68a8-e51d-446b-b2bf-49316dea669b&sid_i=20)

**Project recommendation:** Preserve the specified 1080×1920 Short and 1920×1080 Long outputs. Check subtitles, faces, hands, held objects and clues against current destination UI references at a realistic phone viewing size. Record the platform/reference date. UI changes, ad CTAs and expanded descriptions can change usable space; one universal inset is not established here.

### R05 — Treat captions as information, including meaningful sound

YouTube warns that automatic captions can misrepresent speech and recommends reviewing and correcting them; Thai is listed among supported automatic-caption languages. Availability does not prove transcription accuracy. [SRC05](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554?hl=en)

W3C's prerecorded-caption guidance includes synchronized speech, speaker identity where needed, and meaningful non-speech audio. It is an accessibility reference for web media, not a certification of this repository or a platform ranking rule. [SRC06](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html)

**Project recommendation:** Retain viewer-facing Thai, manually check names and meaning, and segment at readable phrase boundaries. Inspect Thai combining marks, line breaks, timing, contrast and occlusion. Avoid revealing the answer in a subtitle before its intended beat. Where essential, caption a sound such as a knock so sound-off viewers receive the same clue at the same moment. Kinetic emphasis should clarify a word without breaking the whole phrase's readability.

### R06 — Design audio for story and intelligibility

TikTok explicitly identifies voiceover, sound effects and music as storytelling tools; this is platform/business guidance, not a mandate to make all layers loud. [SRC02](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads)

**Project recommendation:** Use narration as the intelligibility reference, purposeful effects as cues, and music/ambience as support. Reduce competing layers under essential speech; test the exported mix on headphones and a phone speaker. Check clipped peaks, abrupt starts/stops, missing cues and the last-to-first audio seam. Silence can be an intentional anticipation or contrast beat. No universal LUFS target is established by these sources.

### R07 — Verify music rights for the actual destination

YouTube describes its Audio Library as copyright-safe on YouTube, distinguishes tracks requiring attribution, and states that it cannot advise about off-platform music issues. [SRC07](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)

**Project recommendation:** Record the track, source, license, attribution and permitted destinations before use. A trending in-app sound or platform library entry is not evidence of permission to bake that sound into a commercial cross-platform master. Use the repository's asset-license requirements; return an unresolved licensing need before final delivery.

### R08 — Finish the promise before designing a loop

Meta's Instagram announcement explains that total watch time includes replays; the published average-watch-time definition divides watch time by total plays. This 2023 announcement is useful context, not a guarantee that every current dashboard uses unchanged labels or denominators. [SRC08](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/04/instagram-reels-trending-audio-and-gifts-updates/)

**Project recommendation:** A loop can connect action, sound, composition or a recurring question after the promised answer has landed. Preserve the repository's closing hold and ending variants. Avoid cutting off the answer or manufacturing a misleading replay to inflate a metric. Review both visual and audio seams; an intermediate scene ending is not the publishable output ending.

### R09 — Diagnose retention with context

YouTube's Shorts guidance distinguishes feed exposure and chose-to-view versus swiped-away behavior, recommends comparing the same content type, and cautions against judging every upload against a viral hit. [SRC09](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en)

YouTube explains that retention dips can indicate abandonment/skipping and spikes can reflect rewatching or unclear material. Highlighted key moments require detection and eligibility; the inspected Help page says at least 60 seconds and 100 views for those highlights, so a 40–45-second Short cannot be assumed to expose every diagnostic. [SRC10](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)

YouTube's August 2026 explanation distinguishes first-frame views from engaged views and states that the vast majority of analytics remain anchored on engaged views. Raw starts therefore are not evidence of sustained attention. [SRC11](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)

**Project recommendation:** Capture the platform, metric definition, export/date window, clip version, duration, audience and traffic source. Map available dips/spikes to shot IDs and frame ranges. Test a specific hypothesis, such as an unreadable clue or redundant hold, while preserving the story. Observational differences do not isolate editing causality; topic, audience, distribution and sample size can differ. Replays may indicate enjoyment or confusion. No universal retention score or viral threshold is supported here.

### R10 — Control flashes and excessive motion

W3C's flash guidance allows at most three flashes in any one-second period or compliance with its general/red-flash thresholds; brightness, area and red flashing matter. The guidance explains that a flash is a pair of opposing changes, not a single cut. [SRC12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)

**Project recommendation:** Prefer non-flashing emphasis. Inspect authored flashes, lightning, glitch effects and rapid high-contrast alternation across the affected range. Do not treat a three-flash count, a warning label or sparse contact-sheet samples as a complete accessibility evaluation. Use a suitable analysis method when relying on threshold exemptions; record unverified cases. Keep camera shake and motion proportionate to the intended information.

## Technique selection for James

These are proposed project defaults derived from the evidence and storytelling needs, not additional runtime APIs. Select only techniques that serve the matched brief/board; verify implementation under the Remotion toolkit.

| Technique ID | Technique and purpose | Use when | Review evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EDIT01 | Immediate visual question/action | Establishing the opening promise | First frame plus opening playback; promise/payoff mapping |
| EDIT02 | Remove redundant setup | A beat repeats without useful development | Before/after playback with cause and clue still understandable |
| EDIT03 | Cut on action or attention | Directing the viewer to a new event or detail | Cut F−1/F/F+1, screen direction and contact |
| EDIT04 | Insert or reaction shot | A clue or consequence needs its own readable moment | Clue visibility and reaction order in both formats |
| EDIT05 | J/L audio bridge | Maintaining flow or anticipating the next event | Audio offset, intelligibility and direct-seek playback |
| EDIT06 | Motivated crop/push-in | Emphasizing a specific change in knowledge/emotion | Target remains visible; geometry and safe regions stay correct |
| EDIT07 | Selective text emphasis | A term or contrast needs support | Thai phrase remains readable at phone size and reveal timing is preserved |
| EDIT08 | Sound cue or purposeful silence | Marking attention, tension or payoff | Mixed export; meaningful clue also accessible without sound |
| EDIT09 | Deliberate hold | Reading, anticipation, reaction or payoff needs time | Continuous playback with narration and subtitle timing |
| EDIT10 | Match/action/sound loop | A complete story benefits from a natural return | Final-to-first picture/audio review; answer remains complete |
| EDIT11 | Before/after or comparison | Demonstrating an educational change | Difference is visible without a spoken claim alone |
| EDIT12 | Minimal transition effect | A temporal/spatial change needs explicit signaling | Reason recorded; effect does not mask action or introduce flashes |

## Practical review questions

1. Does the opening communicate a real reason to watch before explanation accumulates?
2. Does each shot change what viewers see, know, expect or feel?
3. Are cause, clue, action, reaction and payoff ordered fairly?
4. Can someone identify the key event with sound muted, and understand narration with the picture ignored?
5. Are Thai text, focal action and meaningful sounds accessible at the intended time?
6. Do transitions connect story moments while preserving deliberate cuts and time jumps?
7. Does the ending deliver the promised answer and hold long enough under the existing production rules?
8. Are suggested edits supported by observed frames/audio or labeled as hypotheses?

## Access limitations and unresolved evidence

- Meta's main Reels ads page redirected to a login/block page during inspection; its search snippet was not treated as inspected evidence. SRC04 is the accessible first-party support for the limited safe-zone/audio/vertical recommendation.
- The first-party sources inspected do not establish a universal ideal shot duration, cut frequency, transition count, caption word count, LUT, loudness target or guaranteed retention/reach result.
- Current trend sounds/templates, platform UI overlays and channel analytics were not inspected for this production. Select them from live evidence only when a brief calls for them.
- The recommendations have not been validated by an experiment on this channel. James should report production QA separately from post-publication performance analysis.
