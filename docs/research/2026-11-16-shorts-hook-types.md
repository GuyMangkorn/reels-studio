# Shorts hook types: a catalogue to pick from

Research note, 16 Nov 2026, for the user request "อยากมีการทำคลิปที่มี hook หลายแบบ … hook -> question + delay -> rehook -> question + delay -> answer … rule … ลอง research ให้หน่อยว่า มันมี hook แบบไหนบ้าง". Proposal material for the [edu script rules](../../channels/edu/script.md) ER01–ER10; it changes no rule. Craft background already in [scriptwriting-principles](../scriptwriting-principles.md#hook-และ-mini-story) is linked, not repeated. Channel numbers come from the [clickbait titles note](2026-11-16-clickbait-titles-covers.md).

## Summary

- No platform publishes a list of hook types or a "best" hook. What platforms and data-driven creators agree on: the first second decides the swipe, and the video must keep the opening's promise. Every catalogue entry below is a different way to make that one promise.
- On this channel the two top Shorts both open with **"you" in a physical situation plus a question** (78 % and 77 % stayed). HK02 and HK03 are that shape; they are the safest first picks.
- The **rehook chain (HK04)** works when the delay is filled with visible progress and smaller answers. No source gives a safe delay length; the channel's own data says a stretch with no open question loses viewers within ~5 s (ER06). Delay the main answer, never the next piece of information.
- The **rule format (HK05/HK06)** is itself an open loop: "กฎข้อที่ 1" promises more. One rule that is tested and finally flips is the same shape as ER03 and fits the channel unchanged; several escalating rules fit ER02's "new problem every 10 s" but need ER03 reworded.
- Formats with only blog support (list/countdown, "you're doing it wrong", challenge) are marked as such; two conflict with ER03/ER07.
- Biggest rule conflicts: ER01 allows only the "broken certainty" hook, and ER02's "pay the hook by ~30 %" rules out a delayed main answer. Section 6 proposes wording.

## 3. Deep dive: rehook / open-loop chain (HK04)

**Mechanism.** An open loop is an information gap the viewer believes will close (Loewenstein 1994). Curiosity makes people accept a cost, including waiting, to get the answer (Kang et al. 2009). A rehook re-opens or raises the gap just as the previous beat resolves, so there is no natural exit point; the MrBeast memo places its re-engagements where viewers "could get bored of the story and click off" (creator experience, long-form).

**Template for a 50 s Short** (proposal, not tested on this channel):

| ≈ s | Beat | What the viewer holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 | **Hook Q1**: stake + main question, picture shows the danger | Q1 |
| 3–7 | Answer the sharp objection; show the obvious move | Q1 |
| 7–12 | **Delay = progress**: the character tries; a partial answer lands (a sub-answer, ≤10 s per ER09) | Q1 + why the move fails |
| 12–15 | **Rehook Q2**: "แต่นั่นยังไม่ใช่ปัญหาที่ใหญ่ที่สุด…" — a bigger obstacle between the character and Q1 | Q1 + Q2 |
| 15–25 | Q2 is met and answered; the answer narrows Q1 | Q1 |
| 25–30 | **Rehook Q3**: the last obstacle, or "มีทางเดียว…" | Q1 + Q3 |
| 30–40 | Q3 answered; it sets up the payoff | Q1 |
| 40–48 | **Answer Q1** in one line, with a body comparison (ER02's comparison) | — |
| 48–50 | Hold ~2 s (ER03) | — |

**How long can a delay last?** No platform or study gives a number. What the evidence supports:

- The main question can stay open for most of the video (Q1 above stays open ~40 s) **only if** something new lands every few seconds. On this channel, a 17 s block with no open question held EP02's steepest drop, and EP05's longest gap was ~3 s (observational, [ER06](../../channels/edu/script.md)).
- So the working limit is ER06's existing one: **no stretch over ~5 s without an open question, and no stretch over ~10 s without a small answer or a new problem** (ER02/ER09). "Delay" means the big answer waits, not the viewer.
- "Re-hook every 7–10 s" and "80 % at 3 s" figures in vendor blogs are creator lore; no first-party source was found.

**Rehook phrasings (Thai, for Preaw to adapt):**

- "แต่นั่นยังไม่ใช่ส่วนที่แย่ที่สุด…" (raise the stakes)
- "แล้วถ้า … ล่ะ?" (new obstacle as a question)
- "ปัญหาจริงเพิ่งเริ่ม…" (reframe what came before as the easy part)
- "มีทางเดียวที่ช่วยคุณได้… และมันไม่ใช่สิ่งที่คุณคิด" (promise the answer, contradict the guess)
- "จำ … ไว้นะ เดี๋ยวมันจะสำคัญ" (plant for the flip; one per video at most)

**Failure modes:**

1. **Stalling**: the rehook line arrives with no new picture or fact. Viewers feel the answer is being withheld (the memo's "misled" warning applies to the body as well as the title).
2. **Too many loops**: more than two questions open at once; the viewer cannot remember Q1 by the payoff.
3. **Rehook that changes the question**: Q2 replaces Q1 instead of standing between the character and Q1; the final answer then pays a question nobody asked.
4. **Weak payoff**: the answer is less surprising than the delays promised. The payoff must be the counter-intuitive line (ER01's claim moved to the end) or the flip (ER03).
5. **Answer outside the 10 s budget** (ER09): a long explanation after a long wait loses the people who stayed.

## 4. Deep dive: rule format (HK05 and HK06)

**Why it hooks.** "กฎข้อที่ 1" does two jobs in two seconds: it puts "you" in a role with stakes (rules exist because something can kill you) and it opens a counting loop, since "ข้อที่ 1" implies there is more. The rule itself should sound wrong or odd, which also satisfies ER01's contradiction. Numbered survival rules are an established pop-culture device (Columbus's rules in *Zombieland*, 2009, ["Rule #1: Cardio"](https://zombie.fandom.com/wiki/Zombieland_Survival_Rules)), and rule-style survival Shorts exist ([example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q-zqYF14Fyg), ["If You Were" channel](https://www.youtube.com/@Ifyouweredaily/shorts)). **No retention data for the format was found**; the user's memory that some versions use only one rule could not be confirmed with a specific video.

### Variant A — HK05, one rule that flips (ER03 as written)

The video names rule 1 and never reaches rule 2. The "ข้อที่ 1" loop is paid by showing why there is only one rule, or by the rule turning against you.

| ≈ s | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Transformation + rule 1, picture shows the danger it protects from |
| 4–14 | Test 1: the character obeys, survives; one-line cause |
| 14–24 | Test 2 (harder): obeying costs something; "แต่…" |
| 24–34 | Test 3: the rule saves you again, but the planted object appears |
| 34–46 | **Flip**: the same rule leads to the trap (or is the reason everything else doesn't matter) |
| 46–50 | Hold |

Worked seed (Boat must verify): newborn sea turtles head for the brightest horizon, normally the sea; artificial light pulls them inland ([Ocean Conservancy](https://oceanconservancy.org/?p=12999), [Quartz](https://qz.com/1177595/scientists-put-baby-sea-turtles-on-tiny-treadmills-to-find-out-why-man-made-light-is-killing-them)). Rule 1 "วิ่งไปหาแสงที่สว่างที่สุด" saves you on a dark beach and kills you next to a resort. Survival-rate figures differ by source (about 1 in 100 vs 1 in 1,000 reaching adulthood), so ER07/ER10 apply.

### Variant B — HK06, rule ladder (2–4 rules)

Each rule is one problem (ER02) with one cause (ER09), and the stakes rise each time. Endings that keep the promise:

- **Last rule breaks rule 1** ("กฎข้อสุดท้าย: ลืมกฎข้อที่ 1 ไปได้เลย"), which is a flip.
- **All rules were one rule**: the last line names the principle under all of them (turns HK06 into ER03's refrain).
- **Count reveal**: "แล้วกฎข้อที่ 4 ล่ะ? … ไม่มี เพราะเต่า 999 ตัวไม่รอดถึงตรงนั้น" — the missing rule is the payoff (needs a verified number).

Escalation: smaller → larger threat, or nearer → further in time (hour 1, day 1, year 1). Three rules fit 45–50 s; four need ~60 s at ≤10 s each.

**Rule-format failure modes:** rules that are just tips (ends as a list, ER07); rule 1 that the viewer already knows; a ladder with no link between rules; numbering promised in the title but not delivered.

## 6. Rule changes these formats would need (proposal only)

Per DP01 these are proposals for the user to review; [channels/edu/script.md](../../channels/edu/script.md) is unchanged.

- **ER01**: replace "one concrete claim that contradicts something the viewer is sure of" with "one of the catalogued hook types (HK01–HK10) that makes a concrete promise with stakes the picture can show within ~5 s; for HK01 that promise is a contradiction". Keep line 1 = title promise, "คุณ", no term (ER05).
- **ER02**: add an exception: "For HK03/HK04/HK05, pay a **sub-answer** by ~30 % and the main question by ~80–90 %; a new problem or rehook still lands every ~10 s."
- **ER03**: for HK06, "one rule" may be several stated rules when one principle explains them and the last rule flips or names it.
- **ER06**: add a column to the open-question map: which question is the **main loop** and where each rehook re-opens it. The ~5 s gap rule stays.
- **ER08**: pitch line (1) names the hook ID; a concept may pitch two hook IDs for an A/B.

## 7. Open questions and A/B tests

1. **HK02 vs HK05 on the same topic** (e.g. turtle): does "กฎข้อที่ 1" beat "ถ้าคุณเป็นเต่าแรกเกิด… จะรอดมั้ย?" on viewed-vs-swiped and stayed %? The [A/B hook pilot](../../scripts/edu-series/ab-hook-pilot/delivery.md) compares different content; a same-content pair isolates the hook.
2. **Payoff timing (HK04 vs HK01)**: main answer at ~30 % vs ~85 %. Compare the retention curve after 30 % and at the end.
3. **HK05 vs HK06**: one rule with a flip vs three rules; compare average view duration and the drop at each rule.
4. **Visual-first layer (HK10)**: same script, first second silent picture vs VO from frame 0.
5. Log the hook ID per episode in `channels/edu/performance.md` so the channel table can confirm or drop formats; Galloway's 60 % viewed-vs-swiped floor is a rough 2023 reference, not a target.
