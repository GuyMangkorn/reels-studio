# Cover and thumbnail principles for short educational videos

Research note, 7 Nov 2026. Question from the user: "อยากมาเปลี่ยนรูปแบบการสร้างปกคลิปใหม่ มี principle ไหม — text, จุดดึงสายตา, จุดที่ทำให้คนหยุดดู หรือ กด … research ไว้ผมอาจจะทำเป็น skill" (rework how covers are made: principles for text, focal point and what makes people stop or click; possibly a future skill). Scope: 9:16 1080×1920 covers for @TamMaiLa's painted-2D educational Shorts/Reels/TikToks, rendered by Remotion with a text overlay.

Each claim is labelled **(primary)** (platform help pages, official blogs, peer-reviewed papers) or **(secondary)** (industry studies, consultants, blogs, press coverage). Where a page could not be fetched and the claim rests on a search snippet, that is said. Points marked **(practice)** are design reasoning from this repo, not sourced.

This note proposes rule changes. It does not change [cover-principles](../cover-principles.md) (CV01–CV10); the user reviews the change first ([DP01](../contributing.md#tools-and-document-improvements)).

## Summary

- **In the feed, nobody sees the cover.** Shorts, Reels and TikTok feeds autoplay the video; the cover shows on the profile grid, channel page, search and shelves. So "stop" is the first frame and first second; "click" is the cover. Both must carry the same subject and question.
- **YouTube Shorts now take a custom uploaded cover** (desktop Studio, verified account, 9:16, 2160×3840 recommended) **(primary)**. There is no A/B testing for Shorts; Test & compare is long-form only and picks winners by watch-time share, not CTR **(primary)**.
- **Faces and text both pull the eye** almost automatically (looked at 16.6× and 11.1× more than matched regions) **(primary)**. A face that looks at the object sends the viewer's eye to it **(primary)**. Whether faces raise views is contested: positive in one academic sample, about neutral overall in a 300k-video industry set.
- **The cover's promise must be paid off in the video.** YouTube defines egregious clickbait as a title or thumbnail claim "not delivered within the video" and counts survey-rated "valued watchtime" **(primary)**. Curiosity needs a noticed, answerable gap **(primary, Loewenstein)**: the education cover asks; the video answers.
- **Current CV01–CV10 are mostly sound.** The main gaps are the first-frame link, measured Thai text sizing, gaze direction, crop zones for every surface (3:4 grid, 4:5 auto crop, view-count badge), and a written scoring rubric with a promise timecode.

## 1. Platform facts

### YouTube Shorts

- **Custom Shorts thumbnails exist.** YouTube Help: "Custom thumbnails for Shorts are currently only available to add in YouTube Studio on a computer"; the account must be verified; recommended 9:16 at 2160×3840, minimum height 640 px, JPG/PNG; there is a daily custom-thumbnail limit; thumbnails must follow Community Guidelines (strikes possible) **(primary)**, [Add video thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en).
- **Auto 4:5 crop.** The same page: vertical videos with 16:9 thumbnails may get an auto-generated 4:5 thumbnail on home, explore and subscriptions **(primary)**. Which band of the 9:16 frame a 4:5 crop keeps is not documented (gap).
- **Rollout and surfaces.** Announced on Creator Insider by Rene Ritchie, rolling out from late July 2026 to YPP channels first; choosing a frame stays a mobile-app function; the cover appears on the home page and the channel's Home/Shorts tabs, while most Shorts views come from the Shorts feed where no thumbnail is shown; Ritchie advised keeping key content away from the edges **(secondary)**, [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/). Its claim that the usable area is "closer to 3:2" is unclear and unverified.
- **Test & compare.** Up to three titles and/or thumbnails; long-form only ("Shorts … aren't eligible"); desktop Studio; winner by **watch time share**, explicitly not CTR; results Winner / Performed same / Inconclusive within about two weeks; variants should differ enough to give a clear result **(primary)**, [Test & compare](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714).
- **Feed stop metric.** Shorts analytics show viewed versus swiped away, which measures the first frames, not the cover **(secondary)**, [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics).

### Instagram Reels

- **Profile grid is 3:4.** Reels play 9:16; since the January 2025 rollout announced by Adam Mosseri, the profile grid shows a 3:4 tile (tested from August 2024) **(secondary press coverage)**, e.g. [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagrams-experimental-profile-grid-has-rectangular-images-instead-of-squares-160007086.html), [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/instagrams-new-profile-grid-layout-from-squares-to-rectangles/). Instagram Help pages did not render for this note.
- **Cover choice.** Pick a frame or "Add from camera roll"; editable after posting; a grid-crop preview lets the tile be repositioned **(secondary)**, [Later](https://later.com/blog/instagram-reels-cover-photo/), [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels-cover/).

### TikTok

- **Cover = a selected frame** chosen with "Select cover" on the post screen; the cover "previews your video in places like your profile or search results" **(primary, TikTok Support "Editing, posting, and deleting", read from the search snippet; the page redirected and did not render)**, [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/editing-posting-and-deleting).
- **Cover text** is added in the app's cover editor with preset styles; editing after posting is reported both as possible and impossible **(secondary, contradictory)**, [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-change-tiktok-thumbnail/).
- **Implication (practice):** on TikTok the cover usually has to be a frame of the video. A designed cover therefore needs a frame inside the video that holds it, e.g. a short title card or the plate frame the cover was built from.

### Grid badges (practice, unverified)

Instagram's Reels tab and TikTok's profile grid draw a play-count badge in the tile's bottom-left. Text placed in the bottom ~10 % of the visible tile can collide with it. Confirm with a screenshot of the channel's own grid.

## 2. Text

- **Text is a gaze magnet in itself.** In free viewing, people fixated text 11.1× and faces 16.6× more than size- and position-matched control regions, and found these regions hard to avoid even when told to **(primary)**, Cerf, Frady & Koch 2009, *Journal of Vision* 9(12):10, [Caltech record](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20130816-103355264). So every word on a cover competes with the subject; fewer words leave more attention for the picture.
- **Few words, read at a glance.** No primary study gives a word limit for thumbnails. Consultants say the thumbnail should communicate the core concept "at a glance" and titles stay under ~50 characters **(secondary)**, Paddy Galloway via [podcast notes](https://share.transistor.fm/s/d34a7e0e). The MrBeast production guide treats title and thumbnail as one package that sets expectations **(secondary, leaked 2024)**, [Simon Willison's notes](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/).
- **Cover text adds to the title.** Practitioners agree the cover text and title should not repeat each other; the pair works as one message **(secondary, widely repeated; no primary test found)**. CV02 already says this.
- **Headline wording.** In a news-headline dataset (Blendle, original vs rewritten headlines), "most, but not all, of the clickbait features lead to a statistically significant increase in the number of clicks", but effectiveness could not be predicted from the features alone **(primary)**, Kuiken et al. 2017, *Digital Journalism*, [UvA record](https://dare.uva.nl/id/90a8f0f4-0d37-4154-b4b9-d20115474a2d). A 2021 follow-up concluded any single headline technique has a marginal effect **(primary, via snippet)**. Read: wording tricks help a little; the idea matters more.
- **Thai-script specifics.**
  - *Loops.* In a 2024 Chulalongkorn study run by Typotheque, looped Thai read faster at distance overall (N 41), but younger readers found looped harder and older readers loopless harder; loopless is commonly preferred for short headlines and by younger readers when little text is shown (N 139) **(primary but vendor-run)**, [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai). Noto Sans Thai (CV08) is loopless, which fits a short headline for a young audience.
  - *Stacked marks (practice).* Thai stacks an upper vowel and a tone mark above the consonant (e.g. "ที่", "น้ำ") and lower vowels below ("ดู", "รู้"). "Cap height" does not map to Thai; measure the **consonant body height** (e.g. of "อ") instead. Leave line spacing of about 1.4–1.6 em so stacked marks of line 2 do not touch descenders of line 1, and check that the outline does not fill the small counters of marks at 120 px.
  - *No spaces between words (practice).* Break lines by hand at word boundaries; never let the renderer wrap Thai. Count length in **spacing glyphs** (combining marks take no width), not "words": "จ้องตาแมว" is 8 spacing glyphs. At 184–192 px in Noto Sans Thai 800 that is close to the 768 px text-safe width, so about 8 spacing glyphs is a practical ceiling per headline line (measure, do not assume).
- **Contrast and outline.** No primary thumbnail study on outline weight was found. WCAG's text-contrast ratios ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)) are the closest primary yardstick; on painted plates with variable ground, a dark outline or soft scrim keeps contrast across the whole word **(practice)**.

## 3. Focal point

- **Faces and gaze capture attention automatically** (Cerf et al. 2009, above): over 80 % probability of fixating a face within the first two fixations in the conference version **(primary)**.
- **Gaze direction steers the eye.** A face looking toward a product raised the odds of looking at the product about fourfold and raised purchase intention; a static face alone did not raise attention over no face (N 137, eye-tracking) **(primary)**, Palcu, Sudkamp & Florack 2017, *Frontiers in Psychology* 8:881, [PMC5454066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5454066/). It cites the same effect in print and banner ads (Hutton & Nolte 2011; Sajjacholapunt & Ball 2014; Droulers & Adil 2015). Direct gaze holds attention on the face; averted gaze passes it to the target.
- **Do faces raise views? Contested.**
  - Positive: faces in thumbnails correlate with video success among the top 100 German creators, varying by category **(primary, book chapter, abstract via snippet; page 403)**, *Handbook of Social Computing* 2024, [Elgar](https://www.elgaronline.com/edcollchap/book/9781803921259/book-part-9781803921259-15.xml). Strong sentiment (positive or negative) in thumbnails went with more views, while strong sentiment in captions went the other way (~500k thumbnails) **(primary, abstract via snippet; page 403)**, *Journal of Business Research* 2022, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296322005938).
  - Neutral: in 300k+ "viral" 2025 videos, faces performed about the same as no faces overall, better in some niches (finance) and worse in others (business); multiple faces did best **(secondary)**, 1of10 via [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/).
  - Emotion: top creators mostly use happy or surprised faces **(secondary, Kapwing 2024, method not published)**, [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/10/24/emotion-in-thumbnails-ai-study-kapwing/).
  - For this channel: a painted 4-head character's face reads at small size only if the eyes and mouth are large in frame (practice). A clear emotion is the safe bet; a face is not mandatory.
- **One subject, contrast, negative space.** Bottom-up saliency models predict fixations from local contrast in luminance, colour and orientation, plus the face/text channel Cerf added **(primary)**. So the subject should be the highest-contrast region and the background low-detail; empty space around the subject makes it the only salient thing (practice consequence). No primary study found on rule of thirds versus centre for thumbnails; on a 3:4 tile with text, centre-weighted subjects survive every crop (practice).
- **Size at grid scale (practice).** A 3-column Instagram grid on a 390-pt-wide phone shows each tile about 130 pt wide, roughly 0.12× the 1080 px cover. A 192 px headline renders about 23 pt; a 92 px sub-line about 11 pt (borderline). CV10's 120 px check matches this.

## 4. Stop and click factors

- **Stop happens in the first frame (practice, from §1).** In all three feeds the viewer sees the video, not the cover. The first frame and first second carry the "stop": a pattern interrupt, a face reacting, the odd detail. The cover is the second chance on the grid, search and channel page.
- **Curiosity gap.** Curiosity arises when attention is drawn to a specific gap in one's knowledge; some prior knowledge is needed, and the gap must feel closable **(primary)**, Loewenstein 1994, *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1):75–98, [summary](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/). For education this means a question the viewer half-knows ("จ้องตาแมว แมวอ่านว่าท้าทาย?"), not a riddle with no foothold.
- **Promise kept.** YouTube enforces against titles/thumbnails with "promises or claims that aren't delivered within the video itself" (India first, Dec 2024) **(primary)**, [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/strengthening-enforcement-against-egregious-clickbait-on-youtube/). Recommendations weigh survey-based "valued watchtime" (only 4–5-star ratings count) besides clicks and watch time; a click "does not guarantee" a watch **(primary)**, Goodrow 2021, [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/). Test & compare scores by watch time for the same reason.
- **Practitioner levers (secondary, unproven individually):** stakes, before/after or wrong/right, an odd detail, a concrete number, "you" framing, a pattern interrupt, a consistent series look; all are ways to open a gap or raise stakes. Industry framing: "good clickbait = curiosity; bad clickbait = deception" **(secondary)**, [Snappa](https://snappa.com/blog/clickbait-thumbnails/).
- **Education specifics (practice):** the strongest edu angles are (1) the misconception ("ที่เราทำกันมันผิด?"), (2) the counter-intuitive fact with a number from the script, (3) the "try this" action, (4) the subject at the moment before the answer. Every number or claim on the cover must pass Boat's fact-check, as CV04 says.

## 5. Evidence quality

- Thin: no peer-reviewed study tests text amount, outline or placement on video thumbnails, nor anything on vertical-video covers. The face evidence conflicts. Most "CTR lift" numbers online are vendor claims without methods.
- Strong: attention capture by faces/text, gaze cueing, the curiosity-gap mechanism, and YouTube's own statements that watch time and satisfaction, not clicks alone, decide.
- Platform UI changes often; recheck §1 before encoding numbers.

## 7. Skill outline (draft for a future `cover-design` skill)

Design/proposed. Owner Film; output feeds the existing `<ID>-COVERS` composition.

1. **Inputs (by path and revision):** final script, title (and description first line), Boat's fact verdicts, approved plates (`PLATES` / episode registry), cover style of the series (`src/videos/covers/<episode>.ts` of a previous episode), the deliverable's platforms.
2. **Extract the promise:** write one line "the viewer will learn ___" and the payoff timecode. List 3–5 gaps the script opens (questions the viewer half-knows), each with its answer line ID.
3. **Generate five angles** (one candidate each): A question/gap, B subject reveal, C face + emotion (gaze chosen), D odd/impossible detail or misconception, E number/fact from the script. For each: plate ID + frame, headline (≤ 8 spacing glyphs, manual breaks), optional sub-line, stop factor tag, promise timecode.
4. **Compose:** subject centre-weighted inside the 3:4 ∩ 4:5 zone; headline in the upper or middle band clear of the badge zone; series font, outline, accent; channel mark per default.
5. **Render** through `scripts/render.mjs <ID>-COVERS --stills` (one Chrome), then `scripts/qa-sheet.sh` at 270 px and 120 px, plus 3:4 and 4:5 crops.
6. **Score each candidate 0–2 per row** (max 20; ship nothing below 14 or with any 0 in T1, F1, S3):
   - T1 headline readable at 120 px; T2 length/lines within limits; T3 adds to the title, no repeat.
   - F1 one subject, the most salient region (squint test); F2 face/eyes readable or gaze points to the key element; F3 contrast text vs ground and subject vs ground.
   - S1 opens a closable gap; S2 concrete hook (number, odd detail, stakes or wrong/right); S3 promise paid off at the recorded timecode; B1 series identity kept.
7. **Small-size and crop check:** every candidate passes at 120 px wide, 3:4 tile, 4:5 tile and with a mock badge bottom-left.
8. **First-frame check:** frame 0 of the Short shows the same subject/question (CV11 proposal).
9. **Hand back:** the sheet image paths, the score table, and the recommended pick; the coordinator asks the user with the structured-question tool.
10. **After release:** log the picked angle and score; after 28 days note channel-page/search views and viewed-vs-swiped to build a channel-own evidence base.
